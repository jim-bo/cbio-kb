ERBB2 mutations define a subgroup of endometrial carcinomas associated with high tumor mutational burden and the microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) molecular subtype

Authors

Pier Selenica

Weining Ma

Sara Moufarrij

Christian Dagher

Thais Basili

Carol Aghajanian

Qin Zhou

Doi

PMID: 39031567 · DOI: 10.1002/1878-0261.13698 · Journal: Molecular Oncology (2024)

TL;DR

Brodeur, Chui, Weigelt and colleagues at Memorial Sloan Kettering screened 2,638 endometrial carcinomas (UCEC) that had clinical tumor-normal MSK-IMPACT sequencing. They found 69 tumors (2.6%) with OncoKB-pathogenic ERBB2 mutations, and 11 of these were also ERBB2-amplified. The most common hotspots were V842I (38%, kinase domain) and R678Q (23–25%, juxtamembrane domain). ERBB2 mutations were clonal in 87% of evaluable tumors. Compared with ERBB2-wildtype ECs, ERBB2-mutated/non-amplified ECs were mostly low-grade endometrioid tumors. They were enriched for the MSI-H molecular subtype (59% vs 24%) and, to a lesser extent, the POLE subtype (11% vs 5.6%). They had much higher tumor mutational burden (median 43.2 vs 6.1 mut/Mb) and very low chromosomal instability. ERBB2-amplified ECs were the opposite: overwhelmingly CN-H/TP53-abnormal and high-grade. Most ERBB2-mutated ECs had low HER2 protein (IHC 0/1+ in 66%). ERBB2 mutation was not prognostic. ERBB2 amplification was linked to worse PFS and OS on univariate analysis only. The authors conclude that ERBB2-mutated EC is a rare subgroup, pathogenically distinct from ERBB2-amplified EC, that may need different HER2-directed strategies such as antibody-drug conjugates or combinations with immunotherapy (PMID:39031567).

Cohort & data

  • Screening cohort: 2,638 consented EC patients with clinical FDA-authorized tumor-normal MSK-IMPACT sequencing between 01/2014 and 03/2022 (IRB #12-245). Of these, 69 (2.6%) had pathogenic ERBB2 mutations by OncoKB (PMID:39031567). The 69 ERBB2-mutated ECs are deposited in cBioPortal as ucs_msk_2024, which cBioPortal names “Endometrial Carcinoma (MSK, Molecular Oncology 2024)” and lists with 69 samples.
  • Comparison cohort: 1,790 ERBB2-wildtype ECs, including 99 with ERBB2 amplification, drawn from a previously published MSK molecularly classified dataset (01/2014–12/2020; Rios-Doria et al., Gynecol Oncol 2023). Table 1 has N = 1,859 in total: ERBB2-wt/non-amp n = 1,691, ERBB2-mut/non-amp n = 58, ERBB2-wt/amp n = 99, ERBB2-mut/amp n = 11 (PMID:39031567).
  • External validation: TCGA EC cohort of 529 whole-exome-sequenced ECs (whole-exome-seq). ERBB2 mutation, histology and molecular subtype were pulled from cBioPortal, citing the TCGA 2013 study (PMID:23636398). The 529-sample count matches cBioPortal’s ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018 rather than the 373-sample ucec_tcga_pub. This mapping is inferred; the paper does not name a studyId.
  • Cancer types (histology, ERBB2-mut/non-amp, n = 58): endometrioid UEC 66% (79% of them FIGO grade 1–2), serous USC 6.9%, clear cell UCCC 6.9%, carcinosarcoma UCS 6.9%, mixed/high-grade EC-NOS 10%, undifferentiated/dedifferentiated 3.4%. Histology was re-reviewed by a gynecologic pathologist using WHO 2020 criteria (PMID:39031567).
  • Assay and analysis:
    • Sequencing: MSK-IMPACT covering 341–505 genes (IMPACT341, IMPACT410, IMPACT468, IMPACT505).
    • Copy number and LOH: FACETS.
    • Clonality: cancer cell fraction by ABSOLUTE v1.0.6. A mutation was called clonal if P(clonal) > 50% or if the lower 95% CI bound of its CCF was > 90%.
    • MSI: MSIsensor, with a score ≥ 10 called MSI-H (PMID:39031567).
  • Molecular subtyping: integrated molecular–IHC classifier, assigning subtypes in this order:
    • POLE: POLE exonuclease-domain hotspot mutation.
    • MSI-H: MSIsensor ≥ 10 and/or MMR-deficient by IHC.
    • CN-H/TP53abn: pathogenic TP53 alteration.
    • CN-L/NSMP: none of the above (PMID:39031567).
  • HER2 protein: HER2 immunohistochemistry (clone 4B5, Ventana) on 41 ERBB2-mutated ECs, done on the same block used for sequencing. Scoring followed the EC-specific guidelines based on the Fader et al. trial criteria, as endorsed by CAP. One trastuzumab-treated case had ERBB2 amplification by FISH only (PMID:39031567).
  • Survival cohort: n = 1,012 patients with primary treatment at MSK and MSK-IMPACT sequencing of the primary tumor. Groups were ERBB2-wt/non-amp n = 936, ERBB2-mut/non-amp n = 34 in Methods but n = 37 in Results, ERBB2-wt/amp n = 39, and ERBB2-mut/amp n = 3. Methods: Kaplan–Meier curves and Cox proportional-hazards models with left truncation to correct for sequencing-date selection bias. Group comparisons used Wilcoxon rank-sum and Fisher’s exact tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, in R 4.1.2 (PMID:39031567).

Key findings

  • Prevalence and spectrum of ERBB2 mutations.

    • 69/2,638 ECs (2.6%) carried pathogenic ERBB2 mutations. 11 (16%) also had ERBB2 amplification, and 8 (12%) had more than one pathogenic ERBB2 mutation.
    • Hotspots: V842I 26/69 (38%, kinase domain) and R678Q 16/69 (23%, juxtamembrane domain; the abstract says 25%).
    • Other recurrent kinase-domain mutations: L755S n = 5, D769H n = 3, T862A n = 3, V777M n = 2.
    • Other recurrent mutations outside the kinase domain: furin-like extracellular domain S310F/Y n = 6 and juxtamembrane V697L n = 2 (PMID:39031567).

  • Clonality. Among 61 cases with enough tumor purity to assess, ERBB2 mutations were clonal in 87% (n = 53). The authors read this as an early event in tumorigenesis followed by clonal expansion, and as evidence that ERBB2 mutations are drivers rather than passengers even in a high-TMB/MSI-H background (PMID:39031567).

  • High TMB, low chromosomal instability.

    • TMB in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs: median 43.2 mut/Mb (range 1.8–436.2). This was higher than in ERBB2-wt/non-amp ECs (median 6.1; range 0.8–667.9; P < 0.001) and ERBB2-wt/amp ECs (median 4.4; range 1.8–16.7; P < 0.001).
    • Fraction of genome altered (FGA) was low in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs (median 0.5%) vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp (5.1%; P < 0.001) and ERBB2-wt/amp ECs (23.4%; P < 0.001).
    • The higher TMB held within subgroups:
      • MSI-H: median 50.3 vs 29.8 mut/Mb, P < 0.001.
      • MSS: median 10.1 vs 5.3 mut/Mb, P < 0.001.
      • FGA did not differ significantly within these subgroups.
    • The 11 ERBB2-mut/amp ECs had low TMB (median 6.1) and FGA (2.5%) (PMID:39031567).

  • Molecular subtype enrichment. Comparing ERBB2-mut/non-amp with ERBB2-wt/non-amp ECs (P < 0.001):

    Molecular subtype ERBB2-mut/non-amp ERBB2-wt/non-amp
    MSI-H 59% 24%
    POLE 11% 5.6%
    CN-L/NSMP 13% 33%
    CN-H/TP53abn 17% 37%

    ERBB2-wt/amp ECs were 92% CN-H/TP53abn. Of the 11 ERBB2-mut/amp ECs, 10 were CN-H/TP53abn (8 serous, 1 grade 3 endometrioid, 1 carcinosarcoma); the remaining case was an MSI-H clear cell carcinoma (PMID:39031567).

  • Clinicopathologic contrasts.

    • ERBB2-mut/non-amp vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp: similar histology distribution (P = 0.12), BMI (P = 0.08) and stage (P = 0.22); median age 60 vs 63 (P = 0.041).
    • ERBB2-mut/non-amp vs ERBB2-wt/amp: significant differences in age, stage, histology and molecular subtype (all P < 0.001). ERBB2-amplified patients were older (median 66 years), more often stage IV (38% vs 7.1%), and enriched for serous/mixed carcinomas and carcinosarcoma (PMID:39031567).

  • Co-mutations.

    • Most frequent in ERBB2-mutated ECs: ARID1A 65%, PTEN 57% and PIK3CA 54%, a pattern typical of endometrioid and clear cell carcinoma.
    • High-grade EC alterations were also present: TP53 46%, FBXW7 35%, PPP2R1A 17% (PMID:39031567).

  • MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs. These were enriched for V842I/R678Q hotspots: 25/32 (78%) vs 14/33 (42%) in other subtypes, P = 0.005. The mechanism of MMR deficiency across the 32 tumors:

    • MLH1 promoter hypermethylation: 9 (28%).
    • Germline results among 21 tested non-hypermethylated cases:
      • Lynch syndrome: 9/21 (43%), from germline MLH1 n = 1, MSH2 n = 4 and MSH6 n = 4.
      • Germline MUTYH: 1 (4.8%), with somatic MSH2 mutations.
      • Somatic MMR-gene mutations: 12/21 (57%).
    • The authors note that germline/somatic MMR mutations make up “up to 72%” of MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs. That is the opposite of the general MMR-deficient EC population, where MLH1 hypermethylation accounts for about 70%. This matches earlier MSK data reporting ERBB2 mutations in 3% of MLH1-hypermethylated vs 29% (germline) and 13% (somatic) MMR-deficient ECs (PMID:35849120; PMID:39031567).

  • HER2 protein expression (n = 41).

    • Score distribution: IHC 0 in 27% (n = 11), 1+ in 39% (n = 16), 2+ in 27% (n = 11), 3+ in 7.3% (n = 3).
    • All 3+ tumors, and 3/11 (27%) 2+ tumors, had both ERBB2 mutation and amplification.
    • HER2 2+/3+ was associated with TP53 mutation (11/14, 79% vs 9/27, 33% in 0/1+; P = 0.009) and with the CN-H/TP53abn subtype (10/14, 71% vs 1/27, 3.7%; P < 0.001).
    • The common mutations (V842I, R678Q, S310F/Y) occurred across all IHC scores, with no apparent relationship between specific mutation and HER2 level (PMID:39031567).

  • Survival. In the 1,012-patient survival cohort: median follow-up 21.8 months (range 0.6–214.5), 83 deaths.

    Group vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp Median PFS (months) PFS HR (95% CI) Median OS (months) OS HR (95% CI)
    ERBB2-mut/non-amp not reached 0.51 (0.19–1.36) not reached 0.39 (0.05–2.78)
    ERBB2-wt/amp 12.8 (95% CI 7.8–18.4) 4.32 (2.84–6.57), P < 0.001 31.9 4.35 (2.16–8.76), P < 0.001
    • PFS and OS could not be estimated for the 3 ERBB2-mut/amp patients.
    • On multivariate analysis adjusting for age, stage and molecular subtype, ERBB2 status was no longer significant:
      • PFS: P = 0.52; mutated HR 0.89 (0.33–2.43); amplified HR 1.28 (0.83–1.99).
      • OS: P = 0.69; mutated HR 0.54 (0.07–3.89); amplified HR 1.24 (0.60–2.56).
      • CN-H/TP53abn stayed prognostic vs POLE/MSI-H (PFS HR 2.86; OS HR 3.91).
    • Among ERBB2-mutated ECs, V842I or R678Q tumors did not differ in survival from those with other ERBB2 mutations (PMID:39031567).

  • Trastuzumab-treated patients (n = 6).

    • All received trastuzumab plus chemotherapy in the recurrent setting, every 3 weeks, 1–19 doses (median 8).
    • 5/6 were ERBB2-amplified by MSK-IMPACT. The sixth (EC22) had low-level amplification by FISH only (ERBB2/CEP17 ratio 2.1; copy number 3.5).
    • Best responses: complete response in 1 (R678Q), stable disease in 2 (V697L, S310Y), progressive disease in 3 (V842I, D277H, E265Q).
    • Median time to progression 164 days (range 20–456) (PMID:39031567).

  • TCGA validation. 15/529 (2.8%) TCGA ECs carried pathogenic ERBB2 mutations; one case had both V842I and L755S. The recurrent mutations were again R678Q (n = 5), V842I (n = 4) and L755S (n = 3). 11/15 (73%) were endometrioid and 11/15 (73%) were MSI-H (PMID:39031567; TCGA data from PMID:23636398).

Genes & alterations

  • ERBB2: pathogenic mutations in 2.6% of ECs.
    • Hotspots: V842I (38%), R678Q (23%), S310F/Y (8.7%), plus L755S, D769H, T862A, V777M and V697L.
    • Clonal in 87% of evaluable cases.
    • Co-occurs with ERBB2 amplification in 16% of mutated cases (11/69).
    • HER2 IHC is mostly 0/1+. ERBB2 mutation and ERBB2 amplification define distinct EC subgroups: mutation goes with MSI-H/high TMB and low FGA, amplification with CN-H/TP53abn and high FGA (PMID:39031567).
  • POLE: its exonuclease-domain hotspot mutation defines the POLE molecular subtype, which was over-represented in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs (11% vs 5.6%) (PMID:39031567).
  • TP53: its pathogenic alteration defines the CN-H/TP53abn subtype, which was 92% of ERBB2-wt/amp but only 17% of ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs. TP53 was mutated in 46% of ERBB2-mutated ECs and associated with HER2 IHC 2+/3+ (P = 0.009) (PMID:39031567).
  • ARID1A (65%), PTEN (57%) and PIK3CA (54%): the most frequent co-mutations in ERBB2-mutated ECs, consistent with endometrioid/clear cell biology (PMID:39031567).
  • FBXW7 (35%) and PPP2R1A (17%): high-grade EC-associated co-mutations seen in ERBB2-mutated ECs (PMID:39031567).
  • MLH1, MSH2 and MSH6: mechanisms of MMR deficiency in the 32 MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs.
    • MLH1 promoter hypermethylation in 28%.
    • Lynch germline mutations in 9/21 tested non-hypermethylated cases (MLH1 n = 1, MSH2 n = 4, MSH6 n = 4).
    • Somatic MMR mutations in 12/21.
    • One case had isolated MSH6 loss by IHC; another had MSH2/MSH6 loss with somatic MSH2 mutations (PMID:39031567).
  • MUTYH: one MSI-H ERBB2-mutated EC (4.8% of 21) had a germline MUTYH mutation together with somatic MSH2 mutations (PMID:39031567).

Clinical implications

  • Diagnostics and targeting. Because ERBB2-mutated and ERBB2-amplified ECs barely overlap, the authors argue that “more ECs patients may potentially benefit from novel anti-HER2 therapies.” Most ERBB2-mutated ECs are HER2 IHC 0/1+, so they would not be picked up by current HER2 overexpression/amplification-based selection. Current NCCN guidance restricts trastuzumab to advanced/recurrent serous EC with HER2 overexpression/ERBB2 amplification (PMID:39031567).
  • Antibody-drug conjugates. HER2 ADCs are proposed as particularly promising for ERBB2-mutated EC, because they work in HER2-low/negative tumors and across extracellular- and kinase-domain mutations. The agents named are ado-trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). The authors cite a 55% objective response rate for T-DXd in ERBB2-mutant NSCLC, independent of HER2 expression (PMID:39031567).
  • Neratinib monotherapy. In the SUMMIT basket trial, 7 ERBB2-mutant EC patients received neratinib. The best response was stable disease in 4 (S310Y ×2, V777L, R678Q) and progression in 3 (V842I, V697L, P761del) (PMID:29420467). The authors conclude that “neratinib alone does not appear to be particularly effective for ERBB2-mut EC” and call for combinations or alternative HER2-directed agents (PMID:39031567).
  • Immunotherapy combinations. Because ERBB2 mutation is linked to MSI-H status, many of these patients would be eligible for immune checkpoint inhibitors. Combining anti-HER2 therapy with an immune checkpoint inhibitor is proposed as a potential strategy (PMID:39031567).
  • Mutation-specific sensitivity (from cited preclinical and clinical data).
    • V842I confers in vitro resistance to trastuzumab and lapatinib; data on neratinib conflict.
    • R678Q is associated with sensitivity to trastuzumab, lapatinib and neratinib.
    • S310F/Y is generally sensitive to anti-HER2 therapy. One EC patient and one cervical cancer patient with S310Y had partial responses to paclitaxel + trastuzumab + everolimus.
    • In this cohort, clinical benefit was seen with R678Q and S310Y, while the V842I tumor progressed on trastuzumab (PMID:39031567).
  • Prognosis. ERBB2 mutation is not a prognostic biomarker in EC. ERBB2 amplification’s adverse effect on PFS and OS is explained by stage, age and molecular subtype, since it disappears on multivariate analysis (PMID:39031567).

Limitations & open questions

  • Design limitations named by the authors.
    • Retrospective, single-institution design.
    • The survival analysis was restricted to patients whose entire treatment and follow-up occurred at MSK, which leaves few ERBB2-mutated cases.
    • Only 6 patients received trastuzumab, all with ERBB2 amplification or HER2 overexpression. No conclusions can therefore be drawn about how specific mutations affect response in ERBB2-mutated/non-amplified EC (PMID:39031567).
  • Variant selection. Only OncoKB-pathogenic or hotspot ERBB2 variants were counted, so variants of unknown significance are excluded. HER2 IHC was available for 41 of 69 cases (PMID:39031567).
  • Internal inconsistencies in the text:
    • R678Q frequency is 25% in the abstract but 16/69 (23%) in Results.
    • The survival-cohort ERBB2-mut/non-amp count is 34 in Methods but 37 in Results.
    • The S310Y trastuzumab response is “stable disease” in Results (Section 3.6) but one of “the only two patients with objective responses” in the Discussion.
    • The Discussion writes “V678Q” where R678Q is meant (PMID:39031567).
  • Open biology question. V842I, R678Q and S310F/Y are also the three most common ERBB2 mutations in colorectal cancer (COADREAD). Whether they preferentially drive carcinogenesis in intestinal and endometrial epithelium in an MSI-H/high-TMB background is still open. Why MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs are enriched for germline/somatic MMR mutations rather than MLH1 hypermethylation is also unexplained, though the authors speculate that ERBB2 activation may favour survival under higher TMB (PMID:39031567; PMID:35849120).
  • Small subgroup. The ERBB2-mut/amp group is too small (n = 11; n = 3 in survival analysis) for firm conclusions about how co-occurring ERBB2 mutation and amplification behave (PMID:39031567).
  • Dataset mapping (cross-paper note). The TCGA comparison uses 529 ECs retrieved from cBioPortal while citing the 2013 TCGA Nature paper (PMID:23636398). The sample count matches ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018, but the paper does not name the studyId.
