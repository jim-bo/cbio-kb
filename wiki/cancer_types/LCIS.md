# Breast Lobular Carcinoma In Situ (LCIS)

## Overview

Lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS) is a non-invasive proliferation of small, dyscohesive epithelial cells filling and expanding the breast lobules, without invasion through the basement membrane. It sits at OncoTree level 2 directly under the Breast ([BREAST](../cancer_types/BREAST.md)) branch, alongside the invasive lobular ([ILC](../cancer_types/ILC.md)) and ductal ([IDC](../cancer_types/IDC.md)) carcinomas with which it shares loss of the cell-adhesion gene [CDH1](../genes/CDH1.md). LCIS frequently coexists with invasive lobular carcinoma and is regarded both as a risk factor for and a non-obligate precursor of invasive lobular disease.

## Cohorts in the corpus

- In a reanalysis of 364 MSK-IMPACT-sequenced primary invasive lobular carcinomas focused on the 25 cases with no [CDH1](../genes/CDH1.md) inactivating genetic alteration (dataset [ilc_msk_2023](../datasets/ilc_msk_2023.md)), coexisting LCIS was present in 18 of 25 (72%) cases, and ductal carcinoma in situ ([DCIS](../cancer_types/DCIS.md)) in 4 of 25 (16%); all four DCIS-positive cases also had LCIS [PMID:38347189](../papers/38347189.md).
