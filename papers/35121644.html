Evaluating the efficacy of a priming dose of cyclophosphamide prior to pembrolizumab to treat metastatic triple negative breast cancer

TL;DR

Anders, Woodcock et al. ran a multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II trial (NCT02768701) at five US centers. The trial tested whether one low “priming” dose of cyclophosphamide (Cy; 300 mg/m² IV), given the day before pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every 3 weeks), could deplete regulatory T cells (Tregs) and improve outcomes. It enrolled 40 patients with pretreated metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (mTNBC; BRCA).

The strategy failed on both primary endpoints: - Peripheral Tregs fell only slightly and non-significantly after Cy (median −3.3%, p=0.19), then rebounded. - Median PFS was 1.8 months, no better than the 1.9-month historical null.

The objective response rate (ORR) was 21% (1 complete response, 7 partial responses, out of 39 patients), similar to single-agent anti-PD-1 therapy. Correlative studies included WES, RNA-seq, TCR/BCR repertoire sequencing and mass cytometry. Response tracked with: - immune-related toxicity (IRT; p=0.02) - B-cell gene signatures in pretreatment tumors - more diverse circulating B-cell receptor (BCR) repertoires - an early rise in circulating plasmablasts during treatment

PD-L1, tumor mutational burden (TMB), PAM50 subtype and somatic mutations did not predict response. The trial’s genomic data back the iAtlas-harmonized cBioPortal study brca_iatlas_anders_2022 PMID:35121644.

Cohort & data

  • Trial: multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II (NCT02768701). Patients were enrolled from November 2016 to February 2018 at UNC-Chapel Hill, University of Pittsburgh, Moses Cone, UNC-Rex Cancer Center and George Washington University. Approved under UNC IRB #16-1025; funded by Merck Sharp & Dohme PMID:35121644.
  • Eligibility:
    • histologically confirmed mTNBC: ER and PR <1%, and HER2 0–1+ by IHC or non-amplified by FISH
    • at least one prior cytotoxic line in the metastatic setting
    • ECOG ≤1
    • stable brain metastases allowed; prior immunotherapy, immunodeficiency and ongoing steroid use excluded
    PMID:35121644
  • Regimen: a single 300 mg/m² IV dose of cyclophosphamide on cycle 1 day 1 (C1D1). The next day (C1D2), patients started 200 mg IV pembrolizumab, repeated every 3 weeks until progression or significant toxicity PMID:35121644.
  • Endpoints:
    • Primary: PFS, and the change in peripheral blood Tregs after Cy.
    • Secondary: ORR, duration of response, OS, treatment-related adverse events (AEs) and correlative science.
    Response was assessed by modified RECIST v1.1 and AEs by NCI CTCAE v4.0 PMID:35121644.
  • Sample size: 36 patients gave 80% power to detect a rise in median PFS from 1.9 months (null) to 2.9 months (alternative) at α=0.05. The trial enrolled 40 to allow for 10% dropout. All 40 were evaluable for toxicity and survival, and 39 for response PMID:35121644.
  • Patients (n=40):
    • median age 54.5 years (range 33–82); 76% White
    • 75% stage 0–III at diagnosis
    • 33 had prior (neo)adjuvant therapy (85% in the text, 83% in Table 1)
    • median 2 prior lines of therapy for metastatic disease (range 1–16)
    • metastatic sites: bone 45%, lung 35%, liver 30%, lymph node 10%, brain 5%
    • ECOG 0 in 60%
    • median time from TNBC diagnosis to first metastasis: 2 years (95% CI 0.6–3.4)
    PMID:35121644
  • Cancer type: metastatic triple-negative invasive breast carcinoma. OncoTree has no TNBC-specific code, and the cBioPortal study is filed under brca.
  • Correlative assays (pretreatment archival FFPE tumor plus serial blood samples; tissue was not required for enrollment):
    • Whole-exome sequencing: WES of tumor, with PBMCs as the matched normal (TruSeq DNA PCR-Free, HiSeq 4000). Reads were aligned to hg38 with BWA v0.7.17. Variants were called with Strelka2 v2.9.10, Cadabra (Abra2 v2.22) and Mutect2 (GATK v4.1.4.0), then filtered against gnomAD and 1000 Genomes. C>T and G>A calls needed a minimum allele fraction of 10% to guard against FFPE artifacts. WES was available for 26 patients. TMB was classed as high, intermediate or low (≥20, 5–20, <5 mut/Mb), or as high vs low at 10 mut/Mb PMID:35121644.
    • RNA-seq: RNA-seq (Illumina TruSeq RNA Access, HiSeq 4000) aligned to GRCh38 (Ensembl 99) with STAR v2.7.3a and quantified with Salmon. Differential expression used DESeq2. The same data fed:
      • PAM50 subtyping, against re-centered microarray centroids
      • CIBERSORTx immune-cell deconvolution
      • GSEA and immune gene signatures
      • the TCGA pan-cancer immune-subtype (C1–C6) classification
      PMID:35121644
    • Immune repertoires: PBMC TCR α/β and BCR IgG/IgM H/K/L repertoires were profiled with Takara SMARTer kits (MiSeq, 2×300 bp) (TCR-seq, BCR-seq). Immune chains were inferred with MiXCR v2.1.9. Tumor repertoire metrics came from tumor RNA-seq. The post-treatment blood sample was the latest one taken after ≥2 cycles of pembrolizumab PMID:35121644.
    • Treg counts: peripheral Tregs (CD45+/FoxP3+/CD4+/CD127−/lo) were counted by flow cytometry at three points: C1D1 before Cy, C1D2 before pembrolizumab, and day 1 of each later cycle PMID:35121644.
    • Mass cytometry: CyTOF of peripheral lymphocytes from 16 patients (8 responders, 8 non-responders) at 43 time points, grouped into early (cycles 1–2), middle (3–5) and late (6+) PMID:35121644.
    • PD-L1: IHC with anti-PD-L1 clone 22C3 (QualTek; see PD-L1 IHC 22C3). Membrane staining was scored as a proportion across all tumor and immune cells, and >1% counted as positive. It was assessed on 13 pretreatment primary tumors and 8 metastatic lesions PMID:35121644.
  • Statistics:
    • Kaplan–Meier estimates for PFS and OS from C1D1
    • Fisher’s exact and Jonckheere–Terpstra tests; Student’s t-test for the effect of Cy on Tregs
    • univariable Cox proportional hazards models of PFS against repertoire diversity metrics
    • Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction at α=0.20
    PMID:35121644
  • Dataset: brca_iatlas_anders_2022 is the cBioPortal study (hg38) reprocessed by CRI iAtlas. cBioPortal’s study metadata, not this paper, lists 31 samples. The paper states only that genomics data are in a public open-access repository.

Key findings

  • Primary endpoint 1, Tregs were not depleted:
    • A single dose of Cy gave a small, non-significant fall in peripheral Tregs (C1D1→C1D2 median change −3.3%, p=0.19).
    • Tregs then rose significantly: +10% from C1D1 to C2D1 (p=0.03) and +22% from C1D2 to C2D1 (p=0.005; the abstract says +21%).
    • Patients whose Tregs fell after Cy had a non-significant trend toward better response than those whose Tregs rose (p=0.08).
    PMID:35121644
  • Primary endpoint 2, PFS did not improve:
    • Median PFS was 1.8 months (95% CI 1.4–2.3). This missed the 2.9-month alternative and did not differ from the 1.9-month null.
    • Median OS was 6.3 months (95% CI 2.8–9.3).
    • At a median follow-up of 29 months, 35 of 40 patients (88%) had died: 1 on treatment and 34 after progression.
    PMID:35121644
  • Response (n=39):
    • ORR (complete plus partial response) was 21%: 1 CR and 7 PRs.
    • 3 patients had stable disease (SD) and 28 (72%) had progressive disease (PD). Of those with PD, 15 progressed within 3 months.
    • The reported clinical benefit rate (CBR) was 10% (95% CI 2%–24%).
    • Median duration of response was 20.4 months (range 4–34). The complete responder stayed in response for at least 19 months.
    PMID:35121644
  • Toxicity:
    • The regimen was generally well tolerated.
    • The most common AEs were fatigue (45%), anemia (33%), nausea (30%), lymphopenia (20%), edema (17%) and raised ALT (17%). The most frequent grade 3–4 AE, fatigue, occurred in 7% of patients.
    • Progression caused 63% of treatment discontinuations. 4 patients (10%) stopped for toxicity and 11 (28%) had dosing interruptions, most often for grade ≥2 liver toxicity.
    PMID:35121644
  • Immune-related toxicity tracked with response:
    • 10 of 40 patients (25%) had IRT of any grade: rash 6, colitis 3, hypothyroidism 3, xerostomia 3. All but one of them were on therapy for more than 6 months.
    • IRT was associated with best response (p=0.02).
    • Prior lines of therapy (p=0.61) and Treg change (C1D1→C2D1 p=0.68; C1D1→C1D2 p=0.10) were not associated with response.
    PMID:35121644
  • PD-L1: response was higher in tumors with a higher percentage of PD-L1-positive cells, but not significantly at either the 1% or 10% cutoff (Wilcoxon p>0.37). PD-L1 was not associated with TMB (p>0.171) PMID:35121644.
  • Genomics did not predict response:
    • No somatic mutation was associated with clinical response.
    • TMB was not associated with clinical benefit or response (Wilcoxon p>0.288). High-TMB tumors were scarce; the only CR had TMB >20 mut/Mb.
    • Most tumors were PAM50 basal-like, and subtype did not predict response or CBR (Fisher p>0.29).
    PMID:35121644
  • Differential expression, responders (CR+PR) vs non-responders (SD+PD):
    • ANKRD30A, SYCE1 and TUBB2B were higher in responders.
    • KRT1, NRAP and KRT2 were higher in non-responders (α<0.2).
    • Across the whole cohort, GSEA and immune gene signatures showed no significant association with clinical benefit or response.
    PMID:35121644
  • B-cell and immune pathway signals in tumors (exploratory):
    • In a GSEA subset of tumors (n=22), CBR was associated with the CXCR4, BCR, IL-2 and NO2-IL12 pathways, and B-cell immune gene signatures were higher in patients with CBR.
    • The authors then excluded tumor-containing lymph nodes (n=6) and re-analyzed the non-nodal tissues (n=25). Clinical benefit correlated with CD56-dim NK-cell and claudin-low signatures. Response correlated with an IgG signature, three B-cell signatures and CD56-dim NK cells.
    • The discussion also lists caspase activation, the NFAT pathway, C-MYC repression, granzyme activity and MHC class II antigen expression as associated with CBR.
    • None of these tumor pathway or signature associations remained significant after FDR correction at α<0.2.
    PMID:35121644
  • Adaptive immune receptor repertoires:
    • Overall TCR (TRA, TRB) and BCR (IgH, IgK, IgL) diversity did not differ by clinical benefit or response. This held in pretreatment tumor, pretreatment blood, post-treatment blood, and the pre- vs post-treatment blood comparison.
    • Patients with CBR or response shared fewer TRB chains with each other than patients who progressed or did not respond. This difference was not significant after correction.
    • Patients with CBR or response were also more likely to keep a preserved peripheral TRB repertoire after ≥2 cycles.
    • In univariable Cox models for PFS, several B-cell diversity features in post-treatment blood were significant at FDR α<0.2: IGL abundance, and IGH Shannon entropy, evenness and abundance.
    • Pretreatment tumor TRA evenness and Chao1 showed weaker associations (p<0.05).
    PMID:35121644
  • Tumor immune microenvironment: CIBERSORTx estimates of immune-cell mixtures did not differ by clinical benefit or response. Gene expression in every tumor sample was strongly representative of the TCGA pan-cancer C4 “lymphocyte depleted” immune subtype PMID:35121644.
  • Peripheral immune cells (CyTOF, 8 responders vs 8 non-responders):
    • Responders had significantly more plasmablasts (CD45+CD14−HLA-DR+CD20+CD19+IgD−CD38+CD27+), but only after cycles 1–2. There was no difference in later cycles.
    • Total CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell frequencies did not differ at any time.
    • Responder CD8+ T cells expressed more PD-1, TIM-3 and CD25 during cycles 1–2.
    PMID:35121644

Genes & alterations

  • TP53: the most frequently mutated gene in pretreatment tumors by WES, in 14 of 26 patients (54%) PMID:35121644.
  • BRCA2: all three patients with BRCA2 mutations had PD as best response PMID:35121644.
  • ARID1A, PIK3CA, BAP1: the cohort carried several uncommon mutations in these genes. The authors note that in tumors other than breast cancer, these genes have been linked to immunosuppressive microenvironments (dampened interferon responses, less T-cell infiltration). None was associated with response in this trial PMID:35121644.
  • ANKRD30A, SYCE1, TUBB2B: higher expression in pretreatment tumors of responders (CR+PR) PMID:35121644.
  • KRT1, NRAP, KRT2: higher expression in pretreatment tumors of non-responders (SD+PD) PMID:35121644.
  • CD274 (PD-L1): 22C3 IHC positivity was numerically higher in responders but not significant at the 1% or 10% cutoff, and was not associated with TMB PMID:35121644.
  • PDCD1 (PD-1): the target of pembrolizumab. Responder peripheral CD8+ T cells expressed more PD-1 during cycles 1–2 PMID:35121644.
  • HAVCR2 (TIM-3) and IL2RA (CD25): both higher on responder peripheral CD8+ T cells during cycles 1–2. CD25 is also part of the CD25+ FOXP3+ Treg phenotype the authors describe in the TNBC microenvironment PMID:35121644.
  • FOXP3: the Treg marker used for peripheral Treg counts (CD45+/FoxP3+/CD4+/CD127−/lo). Low-dose Cy did not significantly deplete FOXP3+ Tregs PMID:35121644.

Clinical implications

  • A single 300 mg/m² priming dose of cyclophosphamide before pembrolizumab did not deplete peripheral Tregs in pretreated mTNBC. It also did not improve PFS over historical controls. The 21% ORR is similar to earlier single-agent PD-1 inhibitor studies (the authors cite an 18.5% ORR in KEYNOTE-012) PMID:35121644.
  • The authors suggest the Treg rebound after Cy may reflect expansion driven by lymphopenia. They gave only one dose of Cy because continued exposure weakened effector T-cell responses in animal models. They also cite the TONIC trial as showing that short Cy exposure did not improve response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade in mTNBC PMID:35121644.
  • IRT was associated with response here (p=0.02). In the cited KEYNOTE-012 and KEYNOTE-086 studies, AEs were not associated with outcome. The authors recommend that future trials combining Treg depletion with checkpoint inhibitors test whether efficacy correlates with immune-related events PMID:35121644.
  • The authors propose B-cell gene signatures in pretreatment tumors and peripheral BCR diversity as candidate biomarkers of anti-PD-1 benefit in mTNBC. This fits the group’s earlier mouse work showing that B cells and antibody production are critical to checkpoint-inhibitor response. The evidence is hypothesis-generating only PMID:35121644.
  • In this heavily pretreated population, not selected for PD-L1, none of the following separated responders from non-responders: PD-L1 IHC, TMB, PAM50 subtype or individual somatic mutations PMID:35121644.

Limitations & open questions

  • Design and size: single-arm design compared against historical controls. Sample sizes were small: 40 enrolled, WES n=26, PD-L1 n=21, RNA-seq subsets n=22–25, CyTOF n=16. Patients were heavily pretreated and not selected for PD-L1 PMID:35121644.
  • Multiple testing: most correlative associations did not survive Benjamini–Hochberg correction at α=0.20, including the tumor pathway and signature enrichments and the TRB overlap result. Only some post-treatment peripheral B-cell diversity features in the PFS Cox models passed FDR, so the correlative findings are exploratory PMID:35121644.
  • No on-treatment biopsies: the authors could not check whether the B-cell and BCR changes seen in blood also occur in the tumor microenvironment PMID:35121644.
  • Lymph node samples: tumor samples taken from lymph nodes may inflate the B-cell signal. A non-nodal subgroup analysis did not remain significant after multiple-testing correction PMID:35121644.
  • TMB: only one patient (the CR) had high TMB, so the trial had little power to test TMB as a biomarker PMID:35121644.
  • Unreconciled figures: the reported CBR (10%, 95% CI 2%–24%) is lower than the ORR (21%), even though CBR is defined as CR+PR+SD lasting ≥6 months and so includes responders. The paper does not explain this PMID:35121644.
  • Internal inconsistencies in the paper:
    • Treg rise from C1D2 to C2D1: +21% in the abstract vs +22% in the results.
    • Prior (neo)adjuvant therapy: 85% in the text vs 83% in Table 1.
    • Prior metastatic lines: the text reports 1–3 lines in n=21 (53%), while Table 1 reports “0 to 3” in n=22 (58%).
    • The TONIC trial is named “Nivolumab After Induction Treatment in Triple-negative Breast Cancer” but is described as testing a “durvalumab response”.
    PMID:35121644
  • Portal data vs paper: the cBioPortal study brca_iatlas_anders_2022 is an iAtlas reprocessing with standardized mutation calling and neoantigen annotation. It lists 31 samples in cBioPortal metadata, vs 26 WES-evaluable patients in the paper. Mutation frequencies from the portal may therefore differ from the paper’s figures (e.g. TP53 in 14/26).
