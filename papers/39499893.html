DNA Damage Response Alterations Predict for Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy Sensitivity in Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer: A Correlative Analysis of the SWOG S1314 Trial

Authors

Gopa Iyer

Catherine M Tangen

Michal Sarfaty

Ashley M Regazzi

I-Ling Lee

Megan Fong

Woonyoung Choi

Colin P N Dinney

Thomas W Flaig

Ian M Thompson

Seth P Lerner

David J McConkey

Jonathan E Rosenberg

Doi

10.1200/PO.24.00287

PMID: 39499893 · DOI: 10.1200/PO.24.00287 · Journal: JCO Precision Oncology (2024)

TL;DR

Iyer, Rosenberg and colleagues sequenced pre-treatment tumors from SWOG S1314 (NCT02177695), a randomized phase II trial. In S1314, cisplatin-eligible patients with cT2–T4a N0 muscle-invasive bladder cancer (BLCA) received neoadjuvant gemcitabine–cisplatin (GC) or dose-dense methotrexate, vinblastine, doxorubicin and cisplatin (ddMVAC), followed by radical cystectomy. The team sequenced 184 transurethral resection (TURBT) specimens with the 505-gene MSK-IMPACT panel. They then tested whether alterations in a pre-specified 9-gene DNA damage response (DDR) panel predicted pathologic response.

Deleterious DDR alterations (truncating variants, or any ERCC2 point mutation) were present in 41/179 patients (23%). Among 151 patients evaluable for pathologic response, two alteration classes were associated with pathologic complete response (pT0): - deleterious DDR alterations: OR 3.24 (95% CI 1.51–6.94), p=0.003 - ERCC2 alterations: OR 3.33 (95% CI 1.35–8.22), p=0.009

Neither class was significantly associated with downstaging to <pT2. Survival results were trends only (deleterious DDR PFS HR 0.54, p=0.053). When variants of unknown significance (VUS) were counted, DDR status did not correlate with pathologic response. The authors call this the largest prospective neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) trial so far to test DDR mutations against chemosensitivity. The same 9-gene panel is now an integral biomarker in the Alliance A031701 trial PMID:39499893.

Cohort & data

  • Trial: SWOG S1314 (NCT02177695) was a randomized phase II cooperative-group study. It enrolled cisplatin-eligible patients with cT2–T4a N0 muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma of the bladder and randomized them 1:1 to GC or ddMVAC, followed by radical cystectomy. The intent-to-treat (ITT) cohort was n=227. The trial’s primary objective was not met: it tested whether the RNA-based Coexpression Extrapolation (COXEN) score predicts pT0 or <pT2 response. Pre-treatment tumor tissue and germline DNA were collected from all patients PMID:39499893.
  • Specimen flow (Fig. 1):
    • 216 pre-treatment TURBT specimens were available. 17 had too little tissue for DNA extraction, and matched germline DNA was extracted for 199.
    • 15 tumor specimens were not processed further, leaving 184 sequenced tumors (the genomic cohort).
    • 5 of the 184 could not be assessed for survival under the S1314 protocol, leaving 179 survival-evaluable patients.
    • 151 patients were evaluable for pathologic response: they either had cystectomy or progressed before it. Patients who received fewer than 3 chemotherapy cycles, or who could not be assessed per protocol, were excluded PMID:39499893.
  • Assay: MSK-IMPACT is an FDA-authorized, CLIA-certified exon-capture NGS panel. It covers all exons and selected introns of 505 cancer genes, and here it was run on paired tumor and germline DNA to filter out SNPs and germline variants. Tumor RNA and DNA were extracted at MD Anderson. Germline DNA was extracted at the SWOG Biospecimen Bank (Nationwide Children’s Hospital) PMID:39499893.
  • Dataset: blca_msk_2024 holds the data. The cBioPortal study describes it as MSK-IMPACT targeted sequencing of 184 MIBC samples with matched normals (hg19).
  • DDR gene set (9 genes): ERCC2, ERCC5, ATM, ATR, BRCA1, BRCA2, FANCC, RECQL4 and RAD51C. These genes were previously implicated in cisplatin sensitivity in the authors’ earlier phase II dose-dense GC trial (the paper’s ref. 10; not in this corpus) PMID:39499893.
  • Alteration categories: The authors tested three definitions against outcomes:
    • any alteration in the 9 genes, regardless of functional impact
    • a deleterious alteration: a predicted truncating variant (nonsense, splice-site or frameshift indel) or any ERCC2 point mutation
    • any ERCC2 alteration PMID:39499893.
  • Statistics:
    • PFS and OS were modeled with Cox proportional hazards regression in the 179 survival-evaluable patients.
    • pT0 and <pT2 were modeled with logistic regression in the 151 path-evaluable patients.
    • All p-values are two-sided and adjusted for the stratification factors (clinical stage and performance status).
    • Pre-specified power: with 151 patients, a 20% prevalence of deleterious alterations and two-sided α=0.05, the study had 80% power to detect an OR of 2.8 for pathologic downstaging PMID:39499893.
  • Demographics (179 survival-evaluable patients, Table 1):
    • median age 61 (Q1–Q3 59–69; range 33–86); 152 male (85%); 156 White (87%)
    • clinical stage: 156 cT2 (87%) and 23 cT3/T4a (13%)
    • performance status 0: 136 (76%)
    • arm: 94 ddMVAC, 85 GC
    The genomic, path-evaluable and ITT populations did not differ substantially PMID:39499893.
  • External comparator: The authors compare against “the urothelial TCGA cohort of 412 MIBC specimens” but give no citation. That cohort size matches the cBioPortal study blca_tcga_pub_2017, which is backed by PMID:28988769.

Key findings

  • Genomic landscape (184 sequenced tumors, Supp. Fig. 1): TERT promoter mutations were the most frequent alteration. Next came TP53, the epigenetic regulators KMT2D, KDM6A and ARID1A, and the DDR genes ATM and ERCC2. The alteration spectrum closely matched TCGA, with one exception: RB1 was altered in 26% of S1314 tumors versus 18% in TCGA PMID:39499893.
  • Tumor mutational burden (TMB), normalized to the fraction of the exome covered by the panel:
    • whole cohort: median 12.4 mut/Mb
    • 83 specimens with any DDR alteration: 18.9 mut/Mb
    • tumors with deleterious DDR alterations: 26 mut/Mb
    • ERCC2-altered vs ERCC2-wild-type tumors: 23.1 vs 10.7 mut/Mb (Fig. 3C)
    PMID:39499893
  • DDR prevalence:
    • The most frequently altered DDR genes were ATM (17%, n=31) and ERCC2 (15%, n=27; 28 alterations in 27 tumors).
    • Deleterious DDR alterations: 41/179 (23%) survival-evaluable patients and 39/151 (25.8%) path-evaluable patients.
    • ERCC2 alterations: 26/179 (14.5%) and 24/151 (15.9%).
    PMID:39499893
  • VUS-inclusive definition fails: When VUS were included alongside deleterious alterations, DDR status did not correlate with pathologic response. This supports the authors’ hypothesis that only putative loss-of-function alterations predict chemosensitivity PMID:39499893.
  • Pathologic response (n=151; overall pT0 51/151 = 34%, <pT2 74/151 = 49%; Table 2):
    • Deleterious DDR alteration vs none: pT0 OR 3.24 (95% CI 1.51–6.94), p=0.003; <pT2 OR 1.74 (0.83–3.65), p=0.15.
    • ERCC2 alteration vs none: pT0 OR 3.33 (1.35–8.22), p=0.009; <pT2 OR 2.33 (0.92–5.89), p=0.073.
    • The Results report a 51% pT0 rate and a 56% <pT2 rate among patients with deleterious DDR alterations, higher than in the overall population.
    PMID:39499893
  • Responders vs non-responders (Fig. 3A): ERCC2 alterations were more than twice as common in responders as in non-responders (22% vs 10%) PMID:39499893.
  • Survival (n=179; 77 PFS events and 54 OS events):
    • Deleterious DDR: PFS HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.29–1.01), p=0.053; OS HR 0.53 (0.25–1.13), p=0.10.
    • ERCC2: PFS HR 0.64 (0.31–1.33), p=0.23; OS HR 0.57 (0.23–1.44), p=0.23. The authors report no evidence that ERCC2 status is associated with survival (Supp. Fig. 3).
    PMID:39499893
  • ERCC2 variant annotation (Fig. 3B): 22 of the 28 ERCC2 alterations had been reported before and linked to impaired nucleotide excision repair (NER) in an in vitro proteoprobe assay (the paper’s ref. 11; not in this corpus) PMID:39499893.
  • ERCC2 alterations in non-responders: Eight non-responders (≥pT2 at cystectomy) carried nine ERCC2 alterations. All eight were cT2 at baseline; 6 received GC and 2 received ddMVAC.
    • Previously linked to NER impairment and cisplatin sensitivity in the proteoprobe assay: N238S (3 patients) and Y72C (1 patient).
    • Not functionally characterized: M724T (in the Y72C patient) and T484A. The related T484M allele did impair NER.
    • Also not characterized in vitro: S74L, A172T and V121spl.
    • A172T had a mutation allele frequency below 10%, possibly a subclonal event.
    • V121spl is predicted to truncate ERCC2, and the consequences of a truncated ERCC2 protein are unknown.
    PMID:39499893

Genes & alterations

  • ERCC2 is the NER helicase and the most frequently altered DDR gene in the 9-gene panel analysis (15%; 28 alterations in 27 tumors).
    • Any ERCC2 alteration was associated with pT0 (OR 3.33, p=0.009) but not significantly with <pT2 (OR 2.33, p=0.073), PFS or OS.
    • ERCC2-altered tumors had higher TMB (23.1 vs 10.7 mut/Mb).
    • Not all ERCC2 mutations confer response. Nine alterations (including N238S, Y72C, T484A, S74L, A172T, V121spl and M724T) were found in non-responders.
    • Earlier work showed that impaired NER tracks with mutations inside the conserved helicase domains; mutations near the C-terminus or between helicase domains did not impair NER.
    • This builds on the extreme-phenotype discovery that ERCC2 mutations were confined to cisplatin responders (PMID:25096233, the paper’s ref. 7).
    PMID:39499893
  • ATM was the most frequently altered DDR gene overall (17%, n=31, all alteration classes). Only truncating ATM variants counted toward the deleterious DDR category PMID:39499893.
  • The other seven DDR panel genes were ERCC5, ATR, BRCA1, BRCA2, FANCC, RECQL4 and RAD51C. Only truncating alterations (nonsense, splice-site, frameshift) counted as deleterious, and results were reported for the pooled category rather than per gene PMID:39499893.
  • RB1 was altered more often in S1314 than in TCGA (26% vs 18%). In the discussion, the RETAIN trial used a composite of DDR and RB1 mutations as its biomarker PMID:39499893.
  • TERT promoter mutations were the most frequent alteration, followed by TP53, KMT2D, KDM6A and ARID1A. Each was altered in at least 10% of specimens PMID:39499893.

Clinical implications

  • Candidate predictive biomarker: Deleterious DDR alterations, and ERCC2 mutations in particular, identify MIBC patients more likely to reach pT0 after cisplatin-based NAC. The authors conclude that functional validation is needed before these alterations can be refined into NAC biomarkers PMID:39499893.
  • Variant classification matters: Counting VUS removed the association with response. Clinical use therefore depends on restricting to putative loss-of-function or functionally validated ERCC2 alleles PMID:39499893.
  • De-intensification rather than selection: The authors think no single biomarker is likely to decide who should receive neoadjuvant cisplatin. Instead, they propose using DDR alterations to identify exquisitely chemosensitive patients who might avoid cystectomy or radiotherapy. The prospective trials they discuss:
    • The 9-gene DDR panel is an integral biomarker in Alliance A031701 (NCT03609216). There, patients with a qualifying deleterious DDR mutation and a clinical complete response after cisplatin-based chemotherapy may be observed without consolidation. The primary endpoint is 90% recurrence-free survival at 3 years.
    • The phase II RETAIN trial (NCT02710734) used ddMVAC with a composite DDR plus RB1 mutation biomarker. It did not meet its non-inferiority endpoint (14% margin at 2 years), though ERCC2-mutant patients on active surveillance showed a trend toward better metastasis-free survival. RETAIN-2 is testing the DDR biomarker with NAC plus checkpoint blockade.
    • In HCRN GU16-257 (GC plus nivolumab), clinical complete response alone had a PPV of 0.97 for bladder-intact survival. Adding DDR alterations did not improve the PPV.
    PMID:39499893
  • Adjuncts: The authors suggest urine-based liquid biopsies and stringent clinical complete-response criteria as possible complements to DDR-based selection. They also suggest urine tumor DNA to separate the effect of NAC from that of complete TURBT, and ctDNA at diagnosis to flag patients with micrometastatic disease PMID:39499893.

Limitations & open questions

  • Design: This is a prospective-retrospective correlative analysis of a trial whose primary (COXEN) objective was not met. Selection and confounding biases are possible PMID:39499893.
  • Gene scope: The 9 DDR genes were pre-specified from prior associations and cover only a subset of DDR pathways. Exon-capture sequencing misses alterations outside the panel. The authors call for broader DDR pathway analysis PMID:39499893.
  • DNA only: No expression-based molecular subtypes were integrated, although some subtypes may predict NAC response. Whole-exome and transcriptome sequencing are underway PMID:39499893.
  • Response not explained by DDR: Most tumors lacked DDR alterations, yet many still downstaged or had complete responses, so other chemosensitivity factors must exist. About 15% of patients reach <pT2 through complete TURBT alone, which confounds response attribution PMID:39499893.
  • Power for survival: DDR alterations are too rare to show whether they alter natural history. PFS and OS associations did not reach significance PMID:39499893.
  • ERCC2 alleles in non-responders: Several ERCC2 alleles found in non-responders lack functional characterization (T484A, S74L, A172T, V121spl, M724T). Whether truncating ERCC2 behaves like the canonical helicase-domain missense mutations is unknown PMID:39499893.
  • Endpoint framing: The abstract reports the deleterious-DDR OR of 3.24 as a higher “pathologic response rate”. Table 2 assigns this OR to pT0; the corresponding <pT2 OR was 1.74 (p=0.15). The Results text states that both ERCC2 and deleterious DDR alterations were associated with a higher OR for pT0 and <pT2, but neither <pT2 estimate reached p<0.05 PMID:39499893.
  • Denominators for cross-paper synthesis: ERCC2 prevalence is reported three ways: 27/184 sequenced (15%), 26/179 survival-evaluable (14.5%) and 24/151 path-evaluable (15.9%). Table 1 also has small arithmetic inconsistencies, such as 85 GC patients listed as 49.5% of 179 and 112 DDR-wild-type patients listed as 77.1% of 151 PMID:39499893.
  • Comparison with other cohorts: Gil-Jimenez et al. (retrospective; not in this corpus) also found better downstaging with ERCC2 mutations but only a trend toward better survival. That matches the pattern here PMID:39499893.
