# E2F3

## Overview

E2F3 is a transcription factor that activates genes required for S-phase entry and DNA replication; it is a primary transcriptional target released by [RB1](../genes/RB1.md) upon CDK-mediated hyperphosphorylation. E2F3 amplification at chromosome 6p22 is a recognized event in retinoblastoma that may contribute to tumorigenesis in the subset of tumors with 6p gain, and has been proposed as a potential driver when RB1 inactivation is incomplete.

## Alterations observed in the corpus

- E2F3 is discussed as a driver candidate in retinoblastoma ([rbl_mskcc_2020](../datasets/rbl_mskcc_2020.md), n=83 enucleated specimens); no recurrent E2F3 coding mutations were identified in this MSK-IMPACT–profiled cohort. E2F3 is listed among previously proposed driver candidates (alongside [DEK](../genes/DEK.md), [RBL2](../genes/RBL2.md), [KIF14](../genes/KIF14.md), [NGFR](../genes/NGFR.md)) whose role is not confirmed at the somatic coding-mutation level in this dataset [PMID:33466343](../papers/33466343.md).
- E2F3 is part of the cell-cycle pathway gene set (with RB1, [CCND1](../genes/CCND1.md), [CCNE1](../genes/CCNE1.md)) in colorectal cancer; it carries a high VUS fraction, and oncogenic cell-cycle pathway alteration had a multivariable OS HR of 2.26 [PMID:35487942](../papers/35487942.md).

## Cancer types (linked)

- **Retinoblastoma ([RBL](../cancer_types/RBL.md)):** E2F3 is a previously proposed candidate based on chromosomal location at 6p22 (6p gain is a known recurrent CNA in retinoblastoma); no recurrent somatic coding mutations in 83 MSK-IMPACT specimens [PMID:33466343](../papers/33466343.md).

## Co-occurrence and mutual exclusivity

- No co-occurrence data from somatic mutations; E2F3 amplification may be linked to the 6p gain CNA observed in RB1-wildtype retinoblastoma cases [PMID:33466343](../papers/33466343.md).

## Therapeutic relevance

- No therapeutic targeting reported in the corpus.

## Open questions

- Whether E2F3 amplification (via 6p gain) contributes to retinoblastoma tumorigenesis independently of or cooperatively with partial RB1 inactivation requires functional testing [PMID:33466343](../papers/33466343.md).
