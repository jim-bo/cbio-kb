**PMID:** 39031567 · **DOI:** [10.1002/1878-0261.13698](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.13698) · **Journal:** Molecular Oncology (2024)

## TL;DR

Brodeur, Chui, Weigelt and colleagues at Memorial Sloan Kettering screened 2,638 endometrial carcinomas ([UCEC](../cancer_types/UCEC.md)) that had clinical tumor-normal [MSK-IMPACT](../methods/msk-impact-panel.md) sequencing. They found 69 tumors (2.6%) with OncoKB-pathogenic [ERBB2](../genes/ERBB2.md) mutations, and 11 of these were also ERBB2-amplified. The most common hotspots were V842I (38%, kinase domain) and R678Q (23–25%, juxtamembrane domain). ERBB2 mutations were clonal in 87% of evaluable tumors. Compared with ERBB2-wildtype ECs, ERBB2-mutated/non-amplified ECs were mostly low-grade endometrioid tumors. They were enriched for the MSI-H molecular subtype (59% vs 24%) and, to a lesser extent, the [POLE](../genes/POLE.md) subtype (11% vs 5.6%). They had much higher tumor mutational burden (median 43.2 vs 6.1 mut/Mb) and very low chromosomal instability. ERBB2-amplified ECs were the opposite: overwhelmingly CN-H/[TP53](../genes/TP53.md)-abnormal and high-grade. Most ERBB2-mutated ECs had low HER2 protein (IHC 0/1+ in 66%). ERBB2 mutation was not prognostic. ERBB2 amplification was linked to worse PFS and OS on univariate analysis only. The authors conclude that ERBB2-mutated EC is a rare subgroup, pathogenically distinct from ERBB2-amplified EC, that may need different HER2-directed strategies such as antibody-drug conjugates or combinations with immunotherapy ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).

## Cohort & data

- **Screening cohort:** 2,638 consented EC patients with clinical FDA-authorized tumor-normal [MSK-IMPACT](../methods/msk-impact-panel.md) sequencing between 01/2014 and 03/2022 (IRB #12-245). Of these, 69 (2.6%) had pathogenic ERBB2 mutations by [OncoKB](../methods/oncokb.md) ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)). The 69 ERBB2-mutated ECs are deposited in cBioPortal as [ucs_msk_2024](../datasets/ucs_msk_2024.md), which cBioPortal names "Endometrial Carcinoma (MSK, Molecular Oncology 2024)" and lists with 69 samples.
- **Comparison cohort:** 1,790 ERBB2-wildtype ECs, including 99 with ERBB2 amplification, drawn from a previously published MSK molecularly classified dataset (01/2014–12/2020; Rios-Doria et al., Gynecol Oncol 2023). Table 1 has N = 1,859 in total: ERBB2-wt/non-amp n = 1,691, ERBB2-mut/non-amp n = 58, ERBB2-wt/amp n = 99, ERBB2-mut/amp n = 11 ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **External validation:** TCGA EC cohort of 529 whole-exome-sequenced ECs ([whole-exome-seq](../methods/whole-exome-seq.md)). ERBB2 mutation, histology and molecular subtype were pulled from cBioPortal, citing the TCGA 2013 study ([PMID:23636398](../papers/23636398.md)). The 529-sample count matches cBioPortal's [ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018](../datasets/ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018.md) rather than the 373-sample [ucec_tcga_pub](../datasets/ucec_tcga_pub.md). This mapping is inferred; the paper does not name a studyId.
- **Cancer types (histology, ERBB2-mut/non-amp, n = 58):** endometrioid [UEC](../cancer_types/UEC.md) 66% (79% of them FIGO grade 1–2), serous [USC](../cancer_types/USC.md) 6.9%, clear cell [UCCC](../cancer_types/UCCC.md) 6.9%, carcinosarcoma [UCS](../cancer_types/UCS.md) 6.9%, mixed/high-grade EC-NOS 10%, undifferentiated/dedifferentiated 3.4%. Histology was re-reviewed by a gynecologic pathologist using WHO 2020 criteria ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Assay and analysis:**
  - Sequencing: MSK-IMPACT covering 341–505 genes ([IMPACT341](../methods/IMPACT341.md), [IMPACT410](../methods/IMPACT410.md), [IMPACT468](../methods/IMPACT468.md), [IMPACT505](../methods/IMPACT505.md)).
  - Copy number and LOH: [FACETS](../methods/facets.md).
  - Clonality: cancer cell fraction by [ABSOLUTE](../methods/absolute.md) v1.0.6. A mutation was called clonal if P(clonal) > 50% or if the lower 95% CI bound of its CCF was > 90%.
  - MSI: [MSIsensor](../methods/msisensor.md), with a score ≥ 10 called MSI-H ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Molecular subtyping:** integrated molecular–IHC classifier, assigning subtypes in this order:
  - POLE: [POLE](../genes/POLE.md) exonuclease-domain hotspot mutation.
  - MSI-H: MSIsensor ≥ 10 and/or MMR-deficient by IHC.
  - CN-H/TP53abn: pathogenic [TP53](../genes/TP53.md) alteration.
  - CN-L/NSMP: none of the above ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **HER2 protein:** HER2 [immunohistochemistry](../methods/immunohistochemistry.md) (clone 4B5, Ventana) on 41 ERBB2-mutated ECs, done on the same block used for sequencing. Scoring followed the EC-specific guidelines based on the Fader et al. trial criteria, as endorsed by CAP. One trastuzumab-treated case had ERBB2 amplification by [FISH](../methods/fish.md) only ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Survival cohort:** n = 1,012 patients with primary treatment at MSK and MSK-IMPACT sequencing of the primary tumor. Groups were ERBB2-wt/non-amp n = 936, ERBB2-mut/non-amp n = 34 in Methods but n = 37 in Results, ERBB2-wt/amp n = 39, and ERBB2-mut/amp n = 3. Methods: [Kaplan–Meier](../methods/kaplan-meier.md) curves and [Cox proportional-hazards](../methods/cox-proportional-hazards.md) models with left truncation to correct for sequencing-date selection bias. Group comparisons used Wilcoxon rank-sum and Fisher's exact tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction, in R 4.1.2 ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).

## Key findings

- **Prevalence and spectrum of ERBB2 mutations.**
  - 69/2,638 ECs (2.6%) carried pathogenic ERBB2 mutations. 11 (16%) also had ERBB2 amplification, and 8 (12%) had more than one pathogenic ERBB2 mutation.
  - Hotspots: V842I 26/69 (38%, kinase domain) and R678Q 16/69 (23%, juxtamembrane domain; the abstract says 25%).
  - Other recurrent kinase-domain mutations: L755S n = 5, D769H n = 3, T862A n = 3, V777M n = 2.
  - Other recurrent mutations outside the kinase domain: furin-like extracellular domain S310F/Y n = 6 and juxtamembrane V697L n = 2 ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Clonality.** Among 61 cases with enough tumor purity to assess, ERBB2 mutations were clonal in 87% (n = 53). The authors read this as an early event in tumorigenesis followed by clonal expansion, and as evidence that ERBB2 mutations are drivers rather than passengers even in a high-TMB/MSI-H background ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **High TMB, low chromosomal instability.**
  - TMB in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs: median 43.2 mut/Mb (range 1.8–436.2). This was higher than in ERBB2-wt/non-amp ECs (median 6.1; range 0.8–667.9; P < 0.001) and ERBB2-wt/amp ECs (median 4.4; range 1.8–16.7; P < 0.001).
  - Fraction of genome altered (FGA) was low in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs (median 0.5%) vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp (5.1%; P < 0.001) and ERBB2-wt/amp ECs (23.4%; P < 0.001).
  - The higher TMB held within subgroups:
    - MSI-H: median 50.3 vs 29.8 mut/Mb, P < 0.001.
    - MSS: median 10.1 vs 5.3 mut/Mb, P < 0.001.
    - FGA did not differ significantly within these subgroups.
  - The 11 ERBB2-mut/amp ECs had low TMB (median 6.1) and FGA (2.5%) ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Molecular subtype enrichment.** Comparing ERBB2-mut/non-amp with ERBB2-wt/non-amp ECs (P < 0.001):

  | Molecular subtype | ERBB2-mut/non-amp | ERBB2-wt/non-amp |
  | :-- | :-- | :-- |
  | MSI-H | 59% | 24% |
  | POLE | 11% | 5.6% |
  | CN-L/NSMP | 13% | 33% |
  | CN-H/TP53abn | 17% | 37% |

  ERBB2-wt/amp ECs were 92% CN-H/TP53abn. Of the 11 ERBB2-mut/amp ECs, 10 were CN-H/TP53abn (8 serous, 1 grade 3 endometrioid, 1 carcinosarcoma); the remaining case was an MSI-H clear cell carcinoma ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Clinicopathologic contrasts.**
  - ERBB2-mut/non-amp vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp: similar histology distribution (P = 0.12), BMI (P = 0.08) and stage (P = 0.22); median age 60 vs 63 (P = 0.041).
  - ERBB2-mut/non-amp vs ERBB2-wt/amp: significant differences in age, stage, histology and molecular subtype (all P < 0.001). ERBB2-amplified patients were older (median 66 years), more often stage IV (38% vs 7.1%), and enriched for serous/mixed carcinomas and carcinosarcoma ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Co-mutations.**
  - Most frequent in ERBB2-mutated ECs: [ARID1A](../genes/ARID1A.md) 65%, [PTEN](../genes/PTEN.md) 57% and [PIK3CA](../genes/PIK3CA.md) 54%, a pattern typical of endometrioid and clear cell carcinoma.
  - High-grade EC alterations were also present: [TP53](../genes/TP53.md) 46%, [FBXW7](../genes/FBXW7.md) 35%, [PPP2R1A](../genes/PPP2R1A.md) 17% ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs.** These were enriched for V842I/R678Q hotspots: 25/32 (78%) vs 14/33 (42%) in other subtypes, P = 0.005. The mechanism of MMR deficiency across the 32 tumors:
  - [MLH1](../genes/MLH1.md) promoter hypermethylation: 9 (28%).
  - Germline results among 21 tested non-hypermethylated cases:
    - Lynch syndrome: 9/21 (43%), from germline [MLH1](../genes/MLH1.md) n = 1, [MSH2](../genes/MSH2.md) n = 4 and [MSH6](../genes/MSH6.md) n = 4.
    - Germline [MUTYH](../genes/MUTYH.md): 1 (4.8%), with somatic MSH2 mutations.
    - Somatic MMR-gene mutations: 12/21 (57%).
  - The authors note that germline/somatic MMR mutations make up "up to 72%" of MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs. That is the opposite of the general MMR-deficient EC population, where MLH1 hypermethylation accounts for about 70%. This matches earlier MSK data reporting ERBB2 mutations in 3% of MLH1-hypermethylated vs 29% (germline) and 13% (somatic) MMR-deficient ECs ([PMID:35849120](../papers/35849120.md); [PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **HER2 protein expression (n = 41).**
  - Score distribution: IHC 0 in 27% (n = 11), 1+ in 39% (n = 16), 2+ in 27% (n = 11), 3+ in 7.3% (n = 3).
  - All 3+ tumors, and 3/11 (27%) 2+ tumors, had both ERBB2 mutation and amplification.
  - HER2 2+/3+ was associated with TP53 mutation (11/14, 79% vs 9/27, 33% in 0/1+; P = 0.009) and with the CN-H/TP53abn subtype (10/14, 71% vs 1/27, 3.7%; P < 0.001).
  - The common mutations (V842I, R678Q, S310F/Y) occurred across all IHC scores, with no apparent relationship between specific mutation and HER2 level ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Survival.** In the 1,012-patient survival cohort: median follow-up 21.8 months (range 0.6–214.5), 83 deaths.

  | Group vs ERBB2-wt/non-amp | Median PFS (months) | PFS HR (95% CI) | Median OS (months) | OS HR (95% CI) |
  | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
  | ERBB2-mut/non-amp | not reached | 0.51 (0.19–1.36) | not reached | 0.39 (0.05–2.78) |
  | ERBB2-wt/amp | 12.8 (95% CI 7.8–18.4) | 4.32 (2.84–6.57), P < 0.001 | 31.9 | 4.35 (2.16–8.76), P < 0.001 |

  - PFS and OS could not be estimated for the 3 ERBB2-mut/amp patients.
  - On multivariate analysis adjusting for age, stage and molecular subtype, ERBB2 status was no longer significant:
    - PFS: P = 0.52; mutated HR 0.89 (0.33–2.43); amplified HR 1.28 (0.83–1.99).
    - OS: P = 0.69; mutated HR 0.54 (0.07–3.89); amplified HR 1.24 (0.60–2.56).
    - CN-H/TP53abn stayed prognostic vs POLE/MSI-H (PFS HR 2.86; OS HR 3.91).
  - Among ERBB2-mutated ECs, V842I or R678Q tumors did not differ in survival from those with other ERBB2 mutations ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Trastuzumab-treated patients (n = 6).**
  - All received [trastuzumab](../drugs/trastuzumab.md) plus chemotherapy in the recurrent setting, every 3 weeks, 1–19 doses (median 8).
  - 5/6 were ERBB2-amplified by MSK-IMPACT. The sixth (EC22) had low-level amplification by FISH only (ERBB2/CEP17 ratio 2.1; copy number 3.5).
  - Best responses: complete response in 1 (R678Q), stable disease in 2 (V697L, S310Y), progressive disease in 3 (V842I, D277H, E265Q).
  - Median time to progression 164 days (range 20–456) ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **TCGA validation.** 15/529 (2.8%) TCGA ECs carried pathogenic ERBB2 mutations; one case had both V842I and L755S. The recurrent mutations were again R678Q (n = 5), V842I (n = 4) and L755S (n = 3). 11/15 (73%) were endometrioid and 11/15 (73%) were MSI-H ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md); TCGA data from [PMID:23636398](../papers/23636398.md)).

## Genes & alterations

- [ERBB2](../genes/ERBB2.md): pathogenic mutations in 2.6% of ECs.
  - Hotspots: V842I (38%), R678Q (23%), S310F/Y (8.7%), plus L755S, D769H, T862A, V777M and V697L.
  - Clonal in 87% of evaluable cases.
  - Co-occurs with ERBB2 amplification in 16% of mutated cases (11/69).
  - HER2 IHC is mostly 0/1+. ERBB2 mutation and ERBB2 amplification define distinct EC subgroups: mutation goes with MSI-H/high TMB and low FGA, amplification with CN-H/TP53abn and high FGA ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [POLE](../genes/POLE.md): its exonuclease-domain hotspot mutation defines the POLE molecular subtype, which was over-represented in ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs (11% vs 5.6%) ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [TP53](../genes/TP53.md): its pathogenic alteration defines the CN-H/TP53abn subtype, which was 92% of ERBB2-wt/amp but only 17% of ERBB2-mut/non-amp ECs. TP53 was mutated in 46% of ERBB2-mutated ECs and associated with HER2 IHC 2+/3+ (P = 0.009) ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [ARID1A](../genes/ARID1A.md) (65%), [PTEN](../genes/PTEN.md) (57%) and [PIK3CA](../genes/PIK3CA.md) (54%): the most frequent co-mutations in ERBB2-mutated ECs, consistent with endometrioid/clear cell biology ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [FBXW7](../genes/FBXW7.md) (35%) and [PPP2R1A](../genes/PPP2R1A.md) (17%): high-grade EC-associated co-mutations seen in ERBB2-mutated ECs ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [MLH1](../genes/MLH1.md), [MSH2](../genes/MSH2.md) and [MSH6](../genes/MSH6.md): mechanisms of MMR deficiency in the 32 MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs.
  - MLH1 promoter hypermethylation in 28%.
  - Lynch germline mutations in 9/21 tested non-hypermethylated cases (MLH1 n = 1, MSH2 n = 4, MSH6 n = 4).
  - Somatic MMR mutations in 12/21.
  - One case had isolated MSH6 loss by IHC; another had MSH2/MSH6 loss with somatic MSH2 mutations ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- [MUTYH](../genes/MUTYH.md): one MSI-H ERBB2-mutated EC (4.8% of 21) had a germline MUTYH mutation together with somatic MSH2 mutations ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).

## Clinical implications

- **Diagnostics and targeting.** Because ERBB2-mutated and ERBB2-amplified ECs barely overlap, the authors argue that "more ECs patients may potentially benefit from novel anti-HER2 therapies." Most ERBB2-mutated ECs are HER2 IHC 0/1+, so they would not be picked up by current HER2 overexpression/amplification-based selection. Current NCCN guidance restricts [trastuzumab](../drugs/trastuzumab.md) to advanced/recurrent serous EC with HER2 overexpression/ERBB2 amplification ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Antibody-drug conjugates.** HER2 ADCs are proposed as particularly promising for ERBB2-mutated EC, because they work in HER2-low/negative tumors and across extracellular- and kinase-domain mutations. The agents named are [ado-trastuzumab emtansine](../drugs/ado-trastuzumab-emtansine.md) (T-DM1) and [trastuzumab deruxtecan](../drugs/trastuzumab-deruxtecan.md) (T-DXd). The authors cite a 55% objective response rate for T-DXd in ERBB2-mutant [NSCLC](../cancer_types/NSCLC.md), independent of HER2 expression ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Neratinib monotherapy.** In the SUMMIT basket trial, 7 ERBB2-mutant EC patients received [neratinib](../drugs/neratinib.md). The best response was stable disease in 4 (S310Y ×2, V777L, R678Q) and progression in 3 (V842I, V697L, P761del) ([PMID:29420467](../papers/29420467.md)). The authors conclude that "neratinib alone does not appear to be particularly effective for ERBB2-mut EC" and call for combinations or alternative HER2-directed agents ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Immunotherapy combinations.** Because ERBB2 mutation is linked to MSI-H status, many of these patients would be eligible for immune checkpoint inhibitors. Combining anti-HER2 therapy with an immune checkpoint inhibitor is proposed as a potential strategy ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Mutation-specific sensitivity (from cited preclinical and clinical data).**
  - V842I confers in vitro resistance to trastuzumab and [lapatinib](../drugs/lapatinib.md); data on neratinib conflict.
  - R678Q is associated with sensitivity to trastuzumab, lapatinib and neratinib.
  - S310F/Y is generally sensitive to anti-HER2 therapy. One EC patient and one cervical cancer patient with S310Y had partial responses to [paclitaxel](../drugs/paclitaxel.md) + trastuzumab + [everolimus](../drugs/everolimus.md).
  - In this cohort, clinical benefit was seen with R678Q and S310Y, while the V842I tumor progressed on trastuzumab ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Prognosis.** ERBB2 mutation is not a prognostic biomarker in EC. ERBB2 amplification's adverse effect on PFS and OS is explained by stage, age and molecular subtype, since it disappears on multivariate analysis ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).

## Limitations & open questions

- **Design limitations named by the authors.**
  - Retrospective, single-institution design.
  - The survival analysis was restricted to patients whose entire treatment and follow-up occurred at MSK, which leaves few ERBB2-mutated cases.
  - Only 6 patients received trastuzumab, all with ERBB2 amplification or HER2 overexpression. No conclusions can therefore be drawn about how specific mutations affect response in ERBB2-mutated/non-amplified EC ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Variant selection.** Only OncoKB-pathogenic or hotspot ERBB2 variants were counted, so variants of unknown significance are excluded. HER2 IHC was available for 41 of 69 cases ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Internal inconsistencies in the text:**
  - R678Q frequency is 25% in the abstract but 16/69 (23%) in Results.
  - The survival-cohort ERBB2-mut/non-amp count is 34 in Methods but 37 in Results.
  - The S310Y trastuzumab response is "stable disease" in Results (Section 3.6) but one of "the only two patients with objective responses" in the Discussion.
  - The Discussion writes "V678Q" where R678Q is meant ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Open biology question.** V842I, R678Q and S310F/Y are also the three most common ERBB2 mutations in colorectal cancer ([COADREAD](../cancer_types/COADREAD.md)). Whether they preferentially drive carcinogenesis in intestinal and endometrial epithelium in an MSI-H/high-TMB background is still open. Why MSI-H ERBB2-mutated ECs are enriched for germline/somatic MMR mutations rather than MLH1 hypermethylation is also unexplained, though the authors speculate that ERBB2 activation may favour survival under higher TMB ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md); [PMID:35849120](../papers/35849120.md)).
- **Small subgroup.** The ERBB2-mut/amp group is too small (n = 11; n = 3 in survival analysis) for firm conclusions about how co-occurring ERBB2 mutation and amplification behave ([PMID:39031567](../papers/39031567.md)).
- **Dataset mapping (cross-paper note).** The TCGA comparison uses 529 ECs retrieved from cBioPortal while citing the 2013 TCGA Nature paper ([PMID:23636398](../papers/23636398.md)). The sample count matches [ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018](../datasets/ucec_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018.md), but the paper does not name the studyId.
