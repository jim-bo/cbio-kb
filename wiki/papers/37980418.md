**PMID:** 37980418 · **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-023-43249-4 · **Journal:** Nature Communications (2023)

## TL;DR

Zhang, Yang, Hu et al. profile the genomic landscape and tumor immune microenvironment of 408 solitary fibrous tumors ([SFT](../cancer_types/SFT.md)) across a discovery cohort (SYSUCC, n=131) and three independent validation cohorts (FAHSYSU, CHCAMS cohort 1, CHCAMS cohort 2), using a 1021-gene targeted NGS panel and quantitative IHC/multiplex immunofluorescence. They identify an actionable *[IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md)* p.R132S hotspot mutation in 6.9% (9/131) of SFTs and a macrophage-dominant, PD-L1-high immune infiltrate in 24.4% (32/131) of cases as candidate targets for [IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md) inhibitors and combination immune/targeted therapy, and build a four-variable integrated risk model (mitotic count, Ki-67+ cell density, CD163+ cell density, *[MTOR](../genes/MTOR.md)* mutation) that outperforms the WHO classification, the mDemicco model, and the G-score for predicting progression-free survival (PFS) in both primary non-CNS and CNS SFTs with negative tumor margins [PMID:37980418](../papers/37980418.md).

## Cohort & data

- **408 histologically confirmed SFTs** ([SFT](../cancer_types/SFT.md)) across four cohorts: SYSUCC (discovery, n=131, resected 2008–2020, Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center), FAHSYSU (n=115, 2013–2021), CHCAMS cohort 1 (n=101, 2017–2021), CHCAMS cohort 2 (n=61, 2013–2016) — collectively the [sft_sysucc_2023](../datasets/sft_sysucc_2023.md) study.
- **[STAT6](../genes/STAT6.md) IHC positivity** (diagnostic surrogate for the *[NAB2](../genes/NAB2.md)*–*[STAT6](../genes/STAT6.md)* fusion): 91.60% (120/131) SYSUCC, 98.26% (113/115) FAHSYSU, 96.04% (97/101) CHCAMS1, 98.36% (60/61) CHCAMS2; RT-PCR in 11 IHC-negative SYSUCC cases confirmed *[NAB2](../genes/NAB2.md)*–*STAT6* fusion transcripts in 5 more, bringing overall SYSUCC molecular/IHC confirmation to 95.42% (125/131).
- **Targeted NGS**: the [geneplus-1021-gene-panel](../methods/geneplus-1021-gene-panel.md) (Geneplus-Beijing, hybrid-capture, ~1.1 Mb, GenePlus 2000 platform, 2×100bp paired-end, aligned to hg38 with [bwa](../methods/bwa.md); SNVs/indels called with [mutect](../methods/mutect.md) and annotated with [annovar](../methods/annovar.md); CNVs with CONTRA) was run on the SYSUCC discovery cohort (n=131) only.
- **Risk-model cohort**: primary non-CNS SFTs with negative tumor margins (NTM) — SYSUCC discovery n=101; validation in FAHSYSU n=71, CHCAMS1 n=84, CHCAMS2 n=55 (pooled n=311 across all four cohorts); additionally validated in 36 primary CNS SFTs with NTM, and tested (unsuccessfully) in 31 relapsed non-CNS SFTs with NTM.
- **Immune profiling**: multiplex IHC (STAT6, Ki-67, [CD68](../genes/CD68.md), CD163, HLA-DPB1, PD-L1, CD3, [CD4](../genes/CD4.md), CD8, [FOXP3](../genes/FOXP3.md), CD11c, CD20) quantified with the HALO 2.3 digital pathology system on the SYSUCC cohort (n=131); [multiplexed-immunofluorescence](../methods/multiplexed-immunofluorescence.md) performed on 3 representative [SFT](../cancer_types/SFT.md) cases to co-localize PD-L1 with macrophage/T-cell markers.
- **MTOR/IDH1 confirmation**: [sanger-sequencing](../methods/sanger-sequencing.md) used to validate *[MTOR](../genes/MTOR.md)* mutations across all three validation cohorts (n=277) and *IDH1* p.R132S in 7 SYSUCC specimens.

## Key findings

- **Actionable IDH1 hotspot**: *IDH1* p.R132S was found in 6.9% (9/131) of SYSUCC SFTs by NGS, verified by Sanger sequencing. It was highly enriched in malignant-WHO-classification, high-nuclear-pleomorphism, and high-cellularity SFTs, but was not associated with PFS.
- **Genomic landscape**: across the 1021-gene panel, the most frequently altered genes in the SYSUCC discovery cohort were *[ZFHX3](../genes/ZFHX3.md)* (25%), *[KMT2C](../genes/KMT2C.md)* (21%), *[TERT](../genes/TERT.md)* (19%), *[SLX4](../genes/SLX4.md)* (17%), *[FAT1](../genes/FAT1.md)* (16%), *[KMT2D](../genes/KMT2D.md)* (16%), *[ARID1B](../genes/ARID1B.md)* (14%), *[CDH23](../genes/CDH23.md)* (13%), and *[NOTCH1](../genes/NOTCH1.md)* (9%); 17.6% (23/131) of SFTs carried clinically actionable alterations.
- **Prognostic mutations**: *MTOR* mutation (3.05–8.70% across cohorts) was associated with shorter PFS (p=3.00E-05, HR 8.31, 95% CI 0.36–189.53); *[TP53](../genes/TP53.md)* mutation (7%, more common in malignant/male/high-mitotic-count SFTs) with shorter PFS (p=0.045, HR 2.81, 95% CI 0.56–14.09); *[ERCC5](../genes/ERCC5.md)* mutation (4%) with shorter PFS (p=0.0024). *[TERT](../genes/TERT.md)* promoter mutations (19.08%, 25/131) were associated with malignant histology, necrosis, larger tumor size, and older age, but not with PFS in this cohort (p=0.18, HR 1.74, 95% CI 0.67–4.50). NOTCH-pathway alterations (*[NOTCH1](../genes/NOTCH1.md)*, *[NOTCH2](../genes/NOTCH2.md)*, *[NOTCH3](../genes/NOTCH3.md)*, *[CREBBP](../genes/CREBBP.md)*) were also related to PFS.
- **Actionable low-frequency alterations mapped to drug classes** (Fig. 2b): PARP-pathway gene alterations in 6.9% (9/131) — *[FANCF](../genes/FANCF.md)* p.R284Lfs*38, *[RAD54L](../genes/RAD54L.md)* p.C179Wfs*6, *[FANCA](../genes/FANCA.md)* p.H913Qfs*29, *[FANCC](../genes/FANCC.md)* p.Y430*, *[ATM](../genes/ATM.md)* p.R2691C, *[RECQL4](../genes/RECQL4.md)* p.V767Gfs*42, *[NBN](../genes/NBN.md)* p.R89*, *[CHEK2](../genes/CHEK2.md)* p.H371Y, *[WRN](../genes/WRN.md)* p.R732* — matched to PARP inhibitors ([niraparib](../drugs/niraparib.md), [olaparib](../drugs/olaparib.md), [rucaparib](../drugs/rucaparib.md), [talazoparib](../drugs/talazoparib.md)); mTOR-pathway alterations in 3.1% (4/131) — *[PIK3CA](../genes/PIK3CA.md)* p.E545A, *[NF2](../genes/NF2.md)* c.675+1G>A, *[NF2](../genes/NF2.md)* p.K253Rfs*43, *[NF1](../genes/NF1.md)* p.Q1086* — matched to mTOR inhibitors ([everolimus](../drugs/everolimus.md), [sirolimus](../drugs/sirolimus.md), [temsirolimus](../drugs/temsirolimus.md)); one case (SFT-111, *[NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md)* p.Q61K) within a 3-case (2.1%) RAS/MEK-pathway group was matched to the MEK inhibitors [fcn-159](../drugs/fcn-159.md) and [pd0325901](../drugs/pd0325901.md); single cases (0.8% each) of *[RB1](../genes/RB1.md)* p.S318Nfs*13 (CDK4/6-inhibitor target), *[PIK3CA](../genes/PIK3CA.md)* p.E545A (PI3K-inhibitor target), and *[BRAF](../genes/BRAF.md)* p.G469R (BRAF-inhibitor / tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor target) were also flagged.
- **Immune microenvironment**: tumor-infiltrating immune cells in SFTs were predominantly macrophages (median CD68+ 42.70 cells/mm², CD163+ 549.50 cells/mm², HLA-DPB1+ 2029.20 cells/mm²; Kruskal-Wallis p=2.20E-16 vs. lymphocyte populations). High density of CD163+ cells (p=0.0026, HR 2.93, 95% CI 1.32–6.52) and HLA-DPB1+ cells (p=2.50E-06, HR 4.93, 95% CI 1.99–12.18) were each independently associated with shorter PFS; CD68+ macrophage density was also significantly correlated with shorter PFS, whereas high CD20+ B-cell density was associated with longer PFS. Neither CD3+ nor CD8+ T-cell density was associated with PFS.
- **PD-L1 expression**: 13.74% (18/131) of SFTs had high PD-L1 expression in tumor cells and 10.69% (14/131) in immune cells (chiefly CD68+/HLA-DPB1+/CD163+ macrophages); 24.4% (32/131) of SFTs showed high PD-L1 in either compartment. High PD-L1 in immune cells was associated with WHO classification, nuclear pleomorphism, and tumor site, but not with PFS. Two SFT cases had both *IDH1* p.R132S and high PD-L1 expression.
- **Integrated risk model**: LASSO regression and random survival forest selected four variables from clinical, histopathological, immunohistochemical, and molecular candidates: mitotic count (≥4/10 HPF), Ki-67+ cell density (≥454.7 cells/mm²), CD163+ cell density (≥929.3 cells/mm²), and *MTOR* mutation. Patients are scored into low (score=0), intermediate (0<score≤3.37), and high-risk (score>3.37) groups. In the SYSUCC discovery cohort (n=101) these groups comprised 62.38%/30.69%/6.93% of patients with 3-year PFS of 100%/89%/43% (p=7.49E-12); results were reproduced in FAHSYSU (3-year PFS 100%/95%/48%, p=4.81E-05), CHCAMS1 (3-year PFS 100%/75%/76%, p=0.039), and CHCAMS2 (5-year PFS 100%/83%/18%, p=3.30E-06).
- **Model performance vs. published models**: the integrated model's C-index and AUC exceeded those of the WHO classification, the mDemicco model, and the G-score in all four cohorts — e.g., in SYSUCC: C-index 0.911 (95% CI 0.846–0.976) vs. 0.787/0.857/0.855, and AUC 0.921 (95% CI 0.868–0.974) vs. 0.790/0.832/0.865, respectively. Pooled across all four cohorts (n=311), the integrated model stratified 50.80%/35.69%/13.50% of patients into low/intermediate/high risk with 5-year PFS of 100%/73%/21% (p=2.00E-16), while the comparator models showed poor inter-model correlation and less-consistent stratification. In the 36-case CNS SFT/NTM cohort, the integrated model's C-index (0.901) and AUC (0.867) exceeded WHO grade (0.802 and 0.756, respectively). The model did **not** stratify the 31-case relapsed non-CNS SFT/NTM cohort.

## Genes & alterations

- **[NAB2](../genes/NAB2.md)–[STAT6](../genes/STAT6.md)**: the defining SFT gene fusion; used diagnostically via STAT6 nuclear IHC (positive in 91.6–98.4% of cases across the four cohorts) rather than direct DNA/RNA fusion detection, because the panel used lacked RNA-based NGS.
- **[IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md)** p.R132S: 6.9% (9/131) of SYSUCC SFTs; enriched in malignant, high-pleomorphism, high-cellularity tumors; not prognostic for PFS; proposed actionable target for IDH1 inhibitors ([ivosidenib](../drugs/ivosidenib.md), [vorasidenib](../drugs/vorasidenib.md)).
- **[MTOR](../genes/MTOR.md)**: mutated in 3.05–8.70% of SFTs across the four cohorts; associated with shorter PFS (p=3.00E-05) and incorporated as the sole molecular variable in the integrated risk model.
- **[TP53](../genes/TP53.md)**: 7% mutated; associated with shorter PFS (p=0.045) and more common in malignant, male, high-mitotic-count SFTs.
- **[ERCC5](../genes/ERCC5.md)**: 4% mutated; associated with shorter PFS (p=0.0024).
- **[TERT](../genes/TERT.md)** promoter: 19.08% (25/131) mutated; associated with malignant histology, necrosis, large tumor size, and older age, but not PFS.
- **NOTCH pathway** (*[NOTCH1](../genes/NOTCH1.md)*, *[NOTCH2](../genes/NOTCH2.md)*, *[NOTCH3](../genes/NOTCH3.md)*, *[CREBBP](../genes/CREBBP.md)*): alterations related to PFS.
- **DNA-repair/PARP-pathway genes** (*[FANCF](../genes/FANCF.md)*, *[RAD54L](../genes/RAD54L.md)*, *[FANCA](../genes/FANCA.md)*, *[FANCC](../genes/FANCC.md)*, *[ATM](../genes/ATM.md)*, *[RECQL4](../genes/RECQL4.md)*, *[NBN](../genes/NBN.md)*, *[CHEK2](../genes/CHEK2.md)*, *[WRN](../genes/WRN.md)*): 6.9% (9/131) combined, proposed PARP-inhibitor targets.
- **mTOR-pathway genes** (*[PIK3CA](../genes/PIK3CA.md)*, *[NF2](../genes/NF2.md)*, *[NF1](../genes/NF1.md)*): 3.1% (4/131) combined, proposed mTOR-inhibitor targets in addition to *MTOR* itself.
- **[NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md)** p.Q61K: within a 3-case (2.1%) RAS/MEK-pathway group proposed for MEK-inhibitor targeting.
- **[RB1](../genes/RB1.md)** p.S318Nfs*13 and **[BRAF](../genes/BRAF.md)** p.G469R: single cases (0.8% each) flagged as CDK4/6-inhibitor and BRAF-inhibitor/tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor targets, respectively.
- **Most frequently altered genes** (frequency, not necessarily prognostic): *[ZFHX3](../genes/ZFHX3.md)* (25%), *[KMT2C](../genes/KMT2C.md)* (21%), *TERT* (19%), *[SLX4](../genes/SLX4.md)* (17%), *[FAT1](../genes/FAT1.md)* (16%), *[KMT2D](../genes/KMT2D.md)* (16%), *[ARID1B](../genes/ARID1B.md)* (14%), *[CDH23](../genes/CDH23.md)* (13%), *NOTCH1* (9%).

## Clinical implications

- **IDH1-directed therapy**: the authors argue *IDH1* p.R132S is a tractable target given approved IDH1 inhibitors ([ivosidenib](../drugs/ivosidenib.md)) in IDH1-mutant cholangiocarcinoma and acute myeloid leukemia, and propose extending IDH1/2 inhibition (including [vorasidenib](../drugs/vorasidenib.md)) as an additional treatment option for high-grade SFTs.
- **Combination immunotherapy**: given predominant macrophage infiltration, PD-L1 positivity concentrated in CD68+/HLA-DPB1+/CD163+ macrophages, and reported activity of anti-angiogenics (e.g., [pazopanib](../drugs/pazopanib.md), cited from prior ESMO-EURACAN-GENTURIS-endorsed phase II trials with ~50% overall response rate) and [CSF1R](../genes/CSF1R.md) inhibition in other tumor types, the authors propose biomarker-guided combinations of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 blockade with anti-angiogenic, anti-macrophage (CSF1R-inhibitor), or anti-IDH1 therapy as a future treatment strategy for SFTs — extrapolating in part from a published [nivolumab](../drugs/nivolumab.md)-[sunitinib](../drugs/sunitinib.md) combination trial in other soft-tissue sarcomas.
- **Risk stratification**: the four-variable integrated model (mitotic count, Ki-67+ density, CD163+ density, *MTOR* mutation) is proposed to guide surveillance intensity and treatment aggressiveness for both primary non-CNS and CNS SFTs with negative tumor margins, outperforming the WHO classification/grade, mDemicco model, and G-score on C-index and AUC in all tested cohorts. Incorporation of *MTOR* mutation status is described as increasingly feasible given the growing routine use of targeted NGS testing.
- **Not validated for relapsed disease**: the model failed to stratify relapsed (recurrent/metastatic) non-CNS SFTs with NTM (n=31), so its clinical use is currently limited to primary tumors with negative margins.

## Limitations & open questions

- No matched normal DNA was sequenced, which the authors note could have improved mutation-detection specificity.
- The targeted panel lacked RNA-based NGS, so some fusion genes — notably *NAB2*–*STAT6* itself — could not be directly detected at the sequence level; diagnosis relied on STAT6 IHC (plus RT-PCR follow-up in IHC-negative cases).
- Most cohort specimens were resected within the past several years, so long-term follow-up outcomes are not yet available.
- The integrated risk model did not significantly stratify relapsed (recurrent/metastatic) non-CNS SFTs with NTM (n=31), which the authors attribute to the added biological complexity of relapsed disease.
- The authors state the practical clinical implications of the model (surveillance intensity, treatment escalation) still require prospective evaluation.
- Figure 2b presents a detailed case-by-case mapping of low-frequency actionable mutations to specific drug classes; several rows of this figure (including a PIK3CA/AKT-pathway group of 2 cases, 1.5%) could not be reliably transcribed from the extracted PDF text due to OCR column-interleaving artifacts — readers should consult the original figure for the complete matrix.
- No prospective or retrospective clinical outcomes with any of the proposed targeted/immune therapies (IDH1, PARP, mTOR, MEK, or PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) are reported in this study; the therapeutic proposals are based on mutation-drug matching rather than treatment response data.
