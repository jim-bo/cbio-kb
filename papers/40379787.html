TL;DR

Gupta, Gazzo et al. mine a clinicogenomic cohort of 3,880 tumor-normal-sequenced breast cancer samples (3,117 patients) from Memorial Sloan Kettering to show that APOBEC3 mutagenesis — driven by the deaminases APOBEC3A (A3A) and APOBEC3B (A3B) — is the single most prevalent dominant mutational process in breast cancer, is enriched in metastatic/post-treatment tumors, and is causally linked to acquired resistance against endocrine therapy, CDK4/6 inhibitors and HER2-targeted agents. Using doxycycline-inducible A3A/A3B overexpression models, APOBEC3-knockout HER2+ cell lines, and whole-genome sequencing of paired primary-metastatic patient samples, they show that active APOBEC3 mutagenesis drives characteristic APOBEC3-context alterations — including RB1 loss-of-function, ESR1 E380Q, and PIK3CA helical-domain hotspots — that accelerate resistance and shorten progression-free survival. Critically, APOBEC3 activity is often detectable before treatment exposure (60% of later APOBEC3-dominant paired samples were already APOBEC3-dominant earlier; 95% showed A3A/A3B protein by IHC), positioning APOBEC3 signatures as a pretreatment biomarker of evolvability rather than a purely acquired, treatment-induced process.

Cohort & data

  • Primary clinicogenomic cohort: 3,880 high-quality breast cancer samples from 3,117 patients profiled by MSK-IMPACT tumor-normal targeted sequencing (341–505 gene panel versions, 2014–2021; data freeze June 2022), after excluding samples with sequencing-estimated tumor purity <20% from an initial 5,831 retrieved. Receptor-subtype composition: 2,133 (68.4%) HR+/HER2−, 276 (8.9%) HR+/HER2+, 149 (4.8%) HR−/HER2+, 559 (17.9%) TNBC (triple-negative, no distinct OncoTree code). Backing dataset: breast_msk_2025.
  • Mutational signature calling: The single multivariate analysis (SigMA) tool deconvoluted dominant mutational signature per sample (APOBEC3 = SBS2+SBS13, HRD = SBS3+SBS8, Clock = SBS1+SBS5, Other). SigMA was benchmarked against downsampled whole-exome sequencing from TCGA primary BRCA (n=1,019) and Bertucci et al. metastatic breast cancer WES (n=617), and whole-genome sequencing from Nik-Zainal et al. primary breast cancer (n=560), simulated to the IMPACT468 panel footprint.
  • Deep WGS validation: 5 paired primary/metastatic patient tumor samples (MSK-BR-WGS series, median 102x tumor / 44x normal coverage) plus WGS of 29 breast cancer cell lines.
  • Functional models: doxycycline-inducible HA-tagged wild-type or catalytically dead (A3A-E72Q, A3B-E255Q) A3A/A3B overexpression in T47D and MCF7 (HR+, endogenously APOBEC3-low) cells; endogenous APOBEC3-driven BT-474 (WT vs A3A-KO) and MDA-MB-453 (WT vs A3A-KO) HER2+ models.
  • Immunohistochemistry: A3A- and A3A/B/G-specific antibody staining on 130 tissue samples.
  • Longitudinal paired-sample analysis: 449 HR+/HER2− patients with 2 sequential MSK-IMPACT samples and 43 with ≥3, genotyped with Waltz to identify acquired versus shared mutations.
  • Treatment outcome analysis: progression-free survival (PFS) on first-line endocrine monotherapy (n=111), first-line endocrine therapy + CDK4/6 inhibitor (n=549), anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in TMB-high tumors (n=39), and PI3Kα-selective inhibitor (n=21).

Key findings

  • APOBEC3 was the most prevalent dominant mutational signature across the 3,880-sample cohort and was enriched from primary to metastatic disease: 15.7%→28.7% of HR+/HER2−, 18.5%→33.1% of HR+/HER2+, 30.2%→20.6% of HR−/HER2+, and 8.4%→16.9% of TNBC samples (primary vs. metastatic).
  • SigMA reliably detected APOBEC3-dominant samples from targeted-panel data, with high sensitivity/specificity/accuracy against WES/WGS ground truth and exposure correlations of 0.75–0.79.
  • APOBEC3-dominant HR+/HER2− metastatic tumors had significantly higher fraction of genome altered (FGA) than unmatched primaries, but significantly lower median FGA and whole-genome-doubling rate than non-APOBEC3-dominant tumors of the same sample type. APOBEC3-dominant HR+ and TNBC tumors had significantly higher median TMB than other dominant-signature groups, and APOBEC3 was the dominant process in most TMB-high (≥10 mutations/Mb) HR+/HER2− and HR+/HER2+ tumors — the current tumor-agnostic threshold for anti-PD-1 immunotherapy.
  • Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC, n=489) was associated more frequently with the APOBEC3 signature than invasive ductal carcinoma, regardless of sample type.
  • Doxycycline-induced wild-type A3A or A3B overexpression in APOBEC3-null T47D/MCF7 cells directly produced an APOBEC3 mutational signature (41.3–92.5% of acquired SNVs in A3A clones; 6.7–12.5% in A3B clones), whereas catalytically dead mutants did not; WT-overexpressing cells also acquired kataegis, omikli, indels, CNAs and structural variants.
  • In first-line endocrine monotherapy for HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer (MBC) (n=111), APOBEC3-dominant tumors had numerically shorter median PFS than tumors with other dominant signatures (8.6 vs. 15.6 months; HR 1.4, 95% CI 0.9–2.2, P=0.12). In first-line endocrine therapy + CDK4/6 inhibitor (n=549), both APOBEC3-dominant (HR 1.5, 95% CI 1.2–1.8, P=2.4×10⁻⁴) and HRD-dominant (HR 1.8, 95% CI 1.4–2.2, P=6.4×10⁻⁷) tumors were independently associated with shorter median PFS versus tumors with other dominant signatures.
  • Causal resistance experiments: fulvestrant-treated A3A-WT T47D cells acquired resistance significantly faster than catalytically dead A3A-E72Q controls (P=1.44×10⁻⁹); 2/3 A3B-WT MCF7 replicates acquired fulvestrant resistance versus 0/3 A3B-E255Q (P=9.13×10⁻¹⁰); A3A-WT T47D cells gained a growth advantage under abemaciclib (P=1.19×10⁻¹²) and palbociclib; BT-474 cells with endogenous APOBEC3 activity acquired lapatinib resistance faster than A3A-knockout cells (P=6.78×10⁻¹⁰), with similar selective advantages under neratinib, the AKT inhibitor MK2206, and the antibody–drug conjugate trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd).
  • Permutation-based gene enrichment in metastatic/post-treatment HR+/HER2− APOBEC3-dominant tumors identified oncogenic mutations in PIK3CA, CDH1, and KMT2C (P_permutation<0.1, mutated in >5% of samples), plus lower-frequency enrichment of NF1 (2.8%) and ZFHX3 (1.1%).
  • In longitudinally sampled HR+/HER2− patients, APOBEC3-context acquired alterations were enriched in ARID1A and ZFHX3 (q=0.08 each) in treatment-resistant, APOBEC3-dominant paired samples; the proportion of APOBEC3-context mutations exclusive to later-stage samples (51.9%) exceeded those shared with earlier samples (32.3%), consistent with an active, ongoing mutagenic process.
  • Site-specific enrichment analysis found the APOBEC3-context ESR1 E380Q mutation strongly enriched in APOBEC3-dominant, HR+/HER2− post-treatment samples (q=7.8×10⁻¹²), whereas classic activating ESR1 ligand-binding-domain hotspots (L536X, Y537X, D538X — none APOBEC3-context) were comparatively rare (8%) in APOBEC3-dominant tumors. APOBEC3-context PIK3CA helical-domain hotspots E545K (P=2×10⁻¹⁰) and E542K (P=4.5×10⁻⁶) were similarly enriched in APOBEC3-dominant post-treatment samples.
  • RB1 Q217* was the most frequent RB1 SNV in the clinical cohort and was enriched in APOBEC3-dominant cases; engineered T47D A3A-WT cells selected under abemaciclib acquired an RB1 X703_splice truncating mutation with complete RB1 protein loss and a ~31-fold shift in abemaciclib IC50 (84.5 nM sensitive vs. 2,624 nM resistant).
  • MDA-MB-453 wild-type cells (endogenous A3A activity) developed abemaciclib resistance through a chromothripsis event at the YAP1 locus (chromosome 11), elevating YAP1 and its downstream target CDK6 expression — a previously described CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance mechanism.
  • Deep case study of patient MSK-BR-WGS-05 (endocrine therapy including tamoxifen and letrozole): 95% of acquired mutations were assigned to the APOBEC3 signature; the metastasis acquired pathogenic PIK3CA E545K and RAD51C P21S mutations and an AURKA amplification while retaining the primary tumor’s FGFR1 amplification and PIK3R1 deep deletion; a chromosome-19 kataegis region co-localized with a CIC deep deletion present in both the primary and metastatic samples.
  • Among 39 TMB-high HR+/HER2− MBC patients treated with anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, all 14 treated in earlier (first/second) lines with APOBEC3-dominant tumors showed disease control. Double (cis) PIK3CA mutations were more frequent in APOBEC3-dominant tumors regardless of TMB category, and in a small cohort (n=21, <4 prior lines) treated with a PI3Kα-selective inhibitor, median PFS was numerically longer for APOBEC3-dominant (9.1 months) versus non-APOBEC3-dominant (4.1 months) tumors (P=0.4).
  • In the paired early/late HR+/HER2− cohort (n=439), >60% of samples that were APOBEC3-dominant at the later timepoint were already APOBEC3-dominant at the earlier timepoint (median exposure 64%, IQR 39–85%), and 95% of tumors showed A3A or A3B protein expression by IHC — evidence that APOBEC3 activity largely predates treatment rather than being solely treatment-induced.

Genes & alterations

  • APOBEC3A / APOBEC3B — the two deaminases whose catalytically active (but not catalytically dead) overexpression reproduces the APOBEC3 mutational signature, kataegis, and structural instability in ER+ and HER2+ breast cancer cell lines, and causally accelerates resistance to fulvestrant, CDK4/6 inhibitors, and HER2-targeted therapies.
  • ESR1 — the APOBEC3-context E380Q mutation is strongly enriched in APOBEC3-dominant, post-treatment HR+/HER2− tumors (q=7.8×10⁻¹²); classic non-APOBEC3-context activating ligand-binding-domain hotspots (L536X/Y537X/D538X) are comparatively rare in APOBEC3-dominant tumors.
  • PIK3CA — APOBEC3-context helical-domain hotspots E545K and E542K are enriched in APOBEC3-dominant post-treatment tumors; double (cis) PIK3CA mutations are more common in APOBEC3-dominant tumors, suggesting increased sensitivity to PI3Kα-selective inhibitors.
  • RB1 — Q217* is the most frequent RB1 SNV and is enriched in APOBEC3-dominant tumors; an engineered APOBEC3-context RB1 truncating mutation acquired under abemaciclib selection caused complete RB1 protein loss and ~31-fold reduced abemaciclib sensitivity.
  • ARID1A, ZFHX3 — transcription factors previously linked to endocrine-therapy resistance; APOBEC3-context acquired alterations in both genes are enriched in treatment-resistant, APOBEC3-dominant paired longitudinal samples (q=0.08 each).
  • NF1, KMT2C, CDH1 — oncogenic mutations enriched by permutation testing in metastatic/post-treatment HR+/HER2− APOBEC3-dominant tumors.
  • FGFR1, PIK3R1, RAD51C, AURKA, CIC — alterations tracked through the MSK-BR-WGS-05 primary-to-metastasis case: FGFR1 amplification and PIK3R1 deep deletion were maintained from primary to metastasis; RAD51C P21S and an AURKA amplification were newly acquired at metastasis; CIC underwent deep deletion co-located with a chromosome-19 kataegis region present at both timepoints.
  • YAP1 / CDK6 — a chromothripsis event at the YAP1 locus drove elevated YAP1 and downstream CDK6 expression in abemaciclib-resistant MDA-MB-453 cells, a known CDK4/6-inhibitor resistance mechanism.
  • TP53, ERBB2, PTEN, MAP3K1, JAK2, CBL — additional genes from the cohort oncoprint and enrichment analyses: TP53 was altered in 45% of the overall cohort; ERBB2 (HER2) amplification defines the HER2+ receptor subtypes studied in the resistance models; PTEN and MAP3K1 alterations were numerically (though not significantly) higher in APOBEC3-dominant resistant samples; JAK2 amplification and CBL deep deletion were among the acquired CNAs in the MSK-BR-WGS-05 metastatic sample.

Clinical implications

  • APOBEC3-dominant status, callable from routine MSK-IMPACT targeted sequencing via the SigMA tool, is associated with shorter PFS on both first-line endocrine monotherapy and endocrine therapy + CDK4/6 inhibitor combinations in HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer, nominating it as a candidate prognostic/predictive biomarker for early treatment escalation.
  • The co-occurrence of APOBEC3 dominance with high TMB identifies a subset of MBC potentially suited to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, with the authors noting that earlier-line use (rather than later-line, treatment-refractory use) may yield better disease control.
  • Enrichment of PIK3CA helical-domain hotspots and double (cis) PIK3CA mutations in APOBEC3-dominant tumors suggests these patients may be preferentially sensitive to PI3Kα-selective inhibitors, and could be identified before overt resistance develops.
  • Because APOBEC3 activity and its downstream alterations (RB1 loss, ESR1 E380Q, PIK3CA hotspots) are frequently detectable in pretreatment or early samples, the authors propose APOBEC3 signatures as a pretreatment biomarker of tumor evolvability that could inform earlier use of combination therapy or closer monitoring, rather than being purely a marker acquired during treatment.
  • No APOBEC3-targeting therapeutic currently exists; the clinical strategies discussed (PI3Kα inhibitors, anti-PD-1 blockade) are indirect, biomarker-guided approaches that exploit downstream consequences of APOBEC3 mutagenesis rather than the enzymes themselves.

Limitations & open questions

  • The clinicogenomic cohort was profiled with targeted panel sequencing (MSK-IMPACT) rather than WGS/WES, providing less complete mutational information; the authors note WGS will likely be needed to fully characterize the clinical scope of APOBEC3 mutagenesis.
  • The cohort is skewed toward HR+ tumors, limiting the ability to assess how APOBEC3 mutagenesis affects disease progression in HR−/HER2+ and triple-negative breast cancer.
  • The large proportion of metastatic cases limits analysis of APOBEC3’s role in relapse from early-stage disease; the authors call for larger primary cohorts with long-term follow-up (including non-relapsing cases) to assess APOBEC3’s contribution to metastatic relapse and adjuvant-therapy resistance.
  • The timing of APOBEC3 mutagenesis (persistent versus staggered/episodic activity) remains debated in the broader field; this study’s paired-sample data (60% early/late concordance, 95% A3A/A3B IHC positivity) support early, pre-existing activity but do not fully resolve episodic dynamics.
  • The PI3Kα-inhibitor (n=21) and anti-PD-1 (n=39) clinical subgroup analyses were small and not statistically significant (P=0.4; no significant PFS difference by APOBEC3 status), so the authors describe these as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of a treatment-selection biomarker.
