**PMID:** 24030381 · **DOI:** 10.1182/blood-2013-08-518886 · **Journal:** Blood (2013), 122(22):3616–3627

## TL;DR

Papaemmanuil, Campbell and colleagues, for the ICGC Chronic Myeloid Disorders Working Group, sequenced 111 cancer genes in 738 patients with myelodysplastic syndromes ([MDS](../cancer_types/MDS.md)) or closely related neoplasms, including chronic myelomonocytic leukemia ([CMML](../cancer_types/CMML.md)) and MDS/MPN. They used tumor-only, whole-genome-amplified DNA with no matched germline. Combining sequencing with cytogenetics, 78% of patients had at least one oncogenic lesion. Oncogenic mutations were found in 43 genes, led by [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) (24%), [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) (22%) and [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) (14%). The authors found 46 significant pairwise gene–gene associations, with co-occurrence and mutual exclusivity centered on spliceosome genes and epigenetic modifiers. Using allele fractions, they inferred that splicing and DNA-methylation mutations occur early and chromatin and signaling mutations occur later. This led them to propose a hypothesis of genetic "predestination", in which early driver mutations, typically in RNA-splicing genes, shape the later trajectory of clonal evolution. Clonal and subclonal driver mutations carried the same prognostic weight. Leukemia-free survival fell steadily as the number of driver lesions rose, from a median of 49 months with 1 lesion to 4 months with ≥6. This effect held after accounting for IPSS ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).

## Cohort & data

- **N = 738 patients** sequenced; **595 had outcome data**; median follow-up 12 months (range 0–155). 415 (56%) were male, and mean age was 68 ± 13 years ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Disease classification** (WHO 2008, except that RCMD-RS was kept as a separate WHO 2002 category). Counts are from Table 1:
  - **RA:** 139 (19%)
  - **RARS:** 92 (12%)
  - **RARS-T:** 17 (2%) ([MDSMPNRST](../cancer_types/MDSMPNRST.md))
  - **RCMD:** 126 (17%)
  - **RCMD-RS:** 59 (8%)
  - **RAEB:** 167 (23%) ([MDSEB](../cancer_types/MDSEB.md))
  - **5q-:** 20 (3%) ([MDSID5Q](../cancer_types/MDSID5Q.md))
  - **[CMML](../cancer_types/CMML.md):** 70 (9%)
  - **MDS-MPN:** 3 (0.4%)
  - **MDS-U:** 10 (1%)
  - **MDS-AML (progressive disease, [AML](../cancer_types/AML.md)):** 35 (5%)

  RARS and RCMD-RS together make up the ring-sideroblast [MDS](../cancer_types/MDS.md) group ([MDSRS](../cancer_types/MDSRS.md)). Overall, 603 patients had [MDS](../cancer_types/MDS.md) proper ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Baseline features:**
  - Hemoglobin 9.9 ± 2.0 g/dL, neutrophils 3.0 ± 3.4 ×10⁹/L, platelets 191 ± 181 ×10⁹/L.
  - Ring sideroblasts >15% in 173/529 (33%); marrow blasts >5% in 202 (27%).
  - Karyotype normal in 425, abnormal in 213, and failed or not done in 100.
  - During follow-up, 66/595 (11%) transformed to leukemia and 234/595 (39%) died ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **DNA source:** peripheral-blood granulocytes (n = 431) or bone-marrow mononuclear cells (n = 307). Germline DNA was generally not available ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Assay:**
  - All DNA underwent [whole-genome-amplification](../methods/whole-genome-amplification.md).
  - An RNA-bait capture panel of 111 genes was then sequenced ([targeted-dna-seq](../methods/targeted-dna-seq.md)), using 96-well barcoded libraries on 2 lanes of an Illumina HiSeq. Genes were chosen for prior implication in myeloid disease, recurrence in common cancers, in-house candidate status, or location in common copy-number-altered regions.
  - Calling used in-house algorithms against an unrelated reference sample, followed by a custom stringent variant-selection pipeline. This yielded 2260 high-confidence variants.
  - Variants were then triaged into "driver mutations", "possible oncogenic" or "unknown significance", based on literature-defined expected mutation patterns ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Cytogenetics:** conventional bone-marrow [karyotyping](../methods/karyotyping.md). Copy-number profiles were also derived from sequencing read counts ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Validation controls (6 datasets):**
  - Matched tumor/constitutional [whole-exome-seq](../methods/whole-exome-seq.md) for 10 WGA samples.
  - 18 technical replicates.
  - Prior [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) and [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) calls.
  - WGA plus panel sequencing of 22 normal DNAs.
  - The same pipeline applied to 56 normal blood exomes.
  - Exome or whole-genome data from 317 constitutional DNA samples ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Statistics:**
  - Pairwise associations: Fisher tests with multiple-testing correction.
  - Univariate tests: log-rank on leukemia-free survival.
  - Multivariate models: [cox-proportional-hazards](../methods/cox-proportional-hazards.md) models with multiple imputation of missing data and stability selection. Variables were selected with [lasso-regression](../methods/lasso-regression.md), and accuracy was averaged over 5-fold cross-validation.
  - Clonal relationships: Pearson goodness-of-fit tests on variant allele fractions, followed by [phylogenetic-tree-reconstruction](../methods/phylogenetic-tree-reconstruction.md). Global temporal ordering used a [bradley-terry-model](../methods/bradley-terry-model.md) ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Data deposition:** sequence data were deposited at the European Genome-Phenome Archive. No accession is given in the article text ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **cBioPortal dataset:**
  - This paper is one of three publications cited by the cBioPortal study [mds_mskcc_2020](../datasets/mds_mskcc_2020.md) ("Myelodysplastic (MSK, 2020)"; 4231 samples; hg19). The other two are [PMID:27276561](../papers/27276561.md) (Papaemmanuil et al., NEJM 2016) and [PMID:30333627](../papers/30333627.md) (Tyner et al., Nature 2018).
  - The portal sample count therefore does not correspond to this paper's 738-patient cohort.

## Key findings

- **Validation of tumor-only calling:**
  - In 10 patients with matched exomes, all 21 somatic variants in the 111 genes were detected and 20/21 passed filters (95.2% sensitivity). Every variant annotated as a driver was confirmed somatic.
  - No oncogenic variants were called in 78 constitutional samples (22 WGA-screened normals plus 56 normal blood exomes).
  - All 147 previously known [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) mutations were recovered, plus 11 that had been missed earlier because of poor coverage. For [TET2](../genes/TET2.md), 20/21 known mutations (95%) were recovered in 184 patients, plus 3 additional ones.
  - WGA did not significantly distort variant allele fractions ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Mutational landscape:**
  - Oncogenic mutations were found in 43 genes, led by [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) (24%), [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) (22%) and [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) (14%).
  - Only 4 genes were mutated in >10% of patients and 3 more in 5–10%. The remaining 36 genes were each mutated in <5% of patients, but together they contributed 33.5% of all mutations ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Copy number from sequencing:**
  - Credible copy-number profiles were obtained for 629/738 patients (85%), and abnormalities were detected in 101 (13%).
  - Detected changes included deletions of 5q, 11q, 20q and 17p, monosomy 7, trisomies 8 and 21, and isochromosome X.
  - Sequencing also found uniparental disomy that cytogenetics cannot see ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Driver yield:**
  - 549/738 patients (74%; 95% CI 71–77%) had ≥1 oncogenic point mutation or MDS-related copy-number change detectable by sequencing. Cytogenetics alone found abnormalities in 33%, and sequencing plus cytogenetics raised the yield to 78%.
  - 43% of patients had 2–3 oncogenic lesions, and 10% had 4–8 ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Gene–gene interactions:** 46 gene pairs were significant at FDR <10% ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
  - Spliceosome-gene mutations were mutually exclusive, and [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) and [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md) were mutually exclusive.
  - Some exclusive pairs cannot be explained by redundancy within one pathway. [EZH2](../genes/EZH2.md) and [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) mutations were never found together (q = 0.04), and [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md) was mutually exclusive with [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md).
  - By contrast, [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md) was strongly co-mutated with [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) (OR 6.7, 95% CI 4.9–9.3; q = 0.0004).
  - [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) and [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) had strikingly different sets of co-mutated genes. [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) was linked to ring-sideroblast MDS ([MDSRS](../cancer_types/MDSRS.md)), while [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) was enriched in [CMML](../cancer_types/CMML.md).
- **Clonal architecture:** among 313 patients with ≥2 drivers ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)):
  - 62% had only clonal drivers.
  - In 4%, the subclonal fractions were too low to resolve phylogeny.
  - 34% had strong evidence of both clonal and subclonal drivers, which allowed pairwise precedences to be defined. 107 patients contributed informative precedences.
- **Temporal ordering:**
  - A Bradley–Terry ranking, with [ASXL1](../genes/ASXL1.md) as the reference point, showed that RNA-splicing and DNA-methylation mutations occur early. Chromatin-modification and signaling mutations tend to occur later.
  - These were trends rather than [absolute](../methods/absolute.md) rules. The authors proposed the genetic "predestination" hypothesis on this basis ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Single-gene prognosis:**
  - Of 24 genes mutated in >5 patients, 8 were associated with significantly worse leukemia-free survival (LFS). One gene, [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md), was associated with better LFS (supplemental Figure 8; the 8 adverse genes are not listed in the main text).
  - These results replicated prior studies ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Clonal = subclonal for prognosis:** six genes with published survival effects had ≥5 patients carrying subclonal mutations: [TET2](../genes/TET2.md), [ASXL1](../genes/ASXL1.md), [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md), [EZH2](../genes/EZH2.md), [CBL](../genes/CBL.md) and [RUNX1](../genes/RUNX1.md). For all six, LFS did not differ significantly between clonal and subclonal mutations ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Driver number predicts outcome:**
  - LFS worsened with the combined number of oncogenic mutations and cytogenetic lesions (P < 0.0001). The trend held with gene mutations only (P = 0.002) and remained significant independent of [TP53](../genes/TP53.md) or [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) status.
  - Median LFS was 49, 42, 27, 18 and 4 months for 1, 2, 3, 4–5 and ≥6 lesions. The rate of transformation to acute leukemia rose monotonically with driver number (P < 0.0001).
  - Driver count added prognostic information after stratification by IPSS (P = 0.0004) ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Driver-negative patients:**
  - 22% of patients had no known oncogenic point mutation or cytogenetic aberration. Their LFS and transformation rates resembled those of patients with 1–2 drivers.
  - DNA source (marrow vs. blood) did not systematically affect the driver distribution ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Prognostic modeling ([ipss](../methods/ipss.md) vs. genomics):**
  - The time-dependent AUC at 90 months was 0.76 for IPSS and 0.80 for all standard clinical variables. Adding point mutations gave 0.82, a marginal and nonsignificant gain.
  - The standard and genomic curves broadly overlapped, which suggests the two carry partly redundant prognostic information.
  - IPSS-R could not be calculated because of missing data ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Genotype–phenotype:**
  - [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) mutations (positive correlation) and del(5q) (negative correlation) were the strongest genetic predictors of hemoglobin.
  - [WT1](../genes/WT1.md), [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md), [STAG2](../genes/STAG2.md) and [NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md) mutations, and complex karyotype, correlated strongly with marrow blast percentage. [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md) predicted a low blast fraction ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Long tail of rare drivers:** 20% of mutations in patients with a single driver fell in genes mutated in <2% of cases. The same was true for 30–40% of additional (3rd, 4th, and later) mutations ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).

## Genes & alterations

- **[SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md):**
  - Most frequently mutated gene (24%) and an early event.
  - The only gene associated with better LFS.
  - Mutually exclusive with [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) and [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md). Linked to ring sideroblasts ([MDSRS](../cancer_types/MDSRS.md)) and predicts a low marrow blast fraction.
  - Its co-mutation partners differ sharply from those of [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md) ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[TET2](../genes/TET2.md):**
  - Mutated in 22%; an early DNA-methylation-pathway driver.
  - Mutually exclusive with [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md), which the authors link to shared disordered DNA hydroxymethylation.
  - The strongest positive genetic predictor of hemoglobin. Clonal and subclonal mutations carried equivalent LFS risk ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md):**
  - Mutated in 14%; an early splicing driver, enriched in [CMML](../cancer_types/CMML.md).
  - Strongly co-mutated with [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md) (OR 6.7). Never co-mutated with [EZH2](../genes/EZH2.md) (q = 0.04) and mutually exclusive with [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md).
  - Clonal and subclonal mutations had equivalent LFS ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[ASXL1](../genes/ASXL1.md):**
  - Reference point for the temporal-ordering model. Clonal and subclonal mutations had equivalent LFS.
  - 10 variants at previously uncharacterized residues (classified VUS) marked significantly worse LFS than ASXL1-wild-type cases (P = 0.03, log-rank), tracking the curve for known oncogenic [ASXL1](../genes/ASXL1.md) mutations.
  - The authors cite an Asxl1-loss plus Nras mouse model that produced more aggressive disease, as support for the observed ASXL1–[NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md) co-mutation ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md):**
  - Co-mutated with [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md), and mutually exclusive with [TET2](../genes/TET2.md) and [SF3B1](../genes/SF3B1.md).
  - Correlated with higher marrow blast percentage ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[EZH2](../genes/EZH2.md):** a chromatin-modifier driver that is never co-mutated with [SRSF2](../genes/SRSF2.md). Clonal and subclonal mutations had equivalent LFS ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[CBL](../genes/CBL.md) and [RUNX1](../genes/RUNX1.md):** both had published survival effects, and for each, clonal and subclonal mutations carried equivalent LFS risk ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[WT1](../genes/WT1.md), [STAG2](../genes/STAG2.md), [NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md):** mutations correlated strongly with marrow blast percentage. [NRAS](../genes/NRAS.md) is co-mutated with [ASXL1](../genes/ASXL1.md) ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[TP53](../genes/TP53.md):** the prognostic effect of driver number remained significant independent of [TP53](../genes/TP53.md) mutation status ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[IRF1](../genes/IRF1.md), [CUX1](../genes/CUX1.md):**
  - Oncogenic [IRF1](../genes/IRF1.md) mutations were found; the group had previously seen IRF1 in 1 RARS patient.
  - [CUX1](../genes/CUX1.md), recently reported in [AML](../cancer_types/AML.md), was also mutated in this cohort ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[EP300](../genes/EP300.md), [CREBBP](../genes/CREBBP.md), [PTEN](../genes/PTEN.md):** well-known cancer genes not previously implicated in MDS. Mutations were rare (<2%) but showed the nonsense, splice-site and frameshift pattern typical of other cancers ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **[SETBP1](../genes/SETBP1.md), [SMC1A](../genes/SMC1A.md), [SMC3](../genes/SMC3.md):** not on the 111-gene bait set, because they were described after it was designed. The authors suggest these genes could explain part of the 22% of driver-negative patients ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Cytogenetic lesions:**
  - del(5q) was the strongest negative genetic predictor of hemoglobin.
  - Complex karyotype correlated with high blast percentage.
  - Uniparental disomy was detectable from sequencing data alone ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).

## Clinical implications

- **Prognosis:** the number of driver mutations plus cytogenetic lesions is one of the strongest predictors of LFS and of transformation to [AML](../cancer_types/AML.md). It adds independent information beyond IPSS (P = 0.0004) ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Subclonal detection matters:** subclonal drivers carried the same LFS risk as clonal drivers in 6 prognostic genes. The authors therefore argue that detecting subclonal mutations could identify high-risk patients early. They also propose sequencing sequential blood samples, for example annually, to catch emerging subclones before higher-risk disease becomes clinically apparent ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Panel breadth:** many drivers are rare. A sizeable share of mutations fall in genes mutated in <2% of patients, so diagnostic screening should sequence a comprehensive set of well-characterized genes ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Tumor-only clinical sequencing is feasible:** the authors present this as the first targeted resequencing of a clinical cohort without matched constitutional DNA. With a stringent pipeline, driver calls were accurate ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Genomic prognostic scores need scale:** adding mutations to standard variables raised the AUC only marginally (0.80 to 0.82, not significant). The authors [estimate](../methods/estimate.md) that an IPSS-R-scale cohort (IPSS-R used 7012 patients) may be needed for robust genomically informed prognostic schemes ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **No drug-response findings:** patients were censored when they started disease-modifying therapy (allogeneic transplant, aggressive chemotherapy, or hypomethylating agents). No drug-response associations were analyzed ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).

## Limitations & open questions

- **No matched germline DNA:** germline polymorphisms could be misannotated as drivers. Validation controls (95.2% sensitivity; no oncogenic calls in 78 constitutional samples) mitigate but do not eliminate this risk ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Whole-genome amplification** could represent mutations non-uniformly, introduce artifacts, or distort allele burden. Control data showed no significant VAF bias ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **"Predestination" is a hypothesis:** it rests on cross-sectional inference, and the authors state that it needs longitudinal analysis of serial samples from large cohorts ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Conservative annotation:** many variants were left as "unknown significance". The ASXL1 VUS analysis is exploratory; confirming these variants would require showing they are somatic, recurrent and independently prognostic ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Panel gaps:** the bait set predates [SETBP1](../genes/SETBP1.md), [SMC1A](../genes/SMC1A.md) and [SMC3](../genes/SMC3.md). The 22% driver-negative fraction may therefore be overstated ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Clinical data gaps:**
  - IPSS-R could not be calculated because of missing data.
  - Missing covariates were multiply imputed.
  - Ring-sideroblast data were missing for 209 patients.
  - Median follow-up was only 12 months ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Copy-number calling from panel reads is preliminary:** the authors suggest adding germline SNP targets to increase sensitivity ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Internal count discrepancy:** the Methods text says 13 patients were "of undefined MDS category or classified as MDS-MPN (including RARS-T)". Table 1 lists RARS-T 17, MDS-MPN 3 and MDS-U 10, which sum to 30 and give the 738 total ([PMID:24030381](../papers/24030381.md)).
- **Cross-paper synthesis:**
  - The authors justify tumor-only driver calling by pointing to the well-established somatic landscape of large genomics studies, including TCGA AML ([PMID:23634996](../papers/23634996.md)). Their in-house calling algorithms were previously described in the Sanger breast-cancer study ([PMID:22722201](../papers/22722201.md)).
  - The early-splicing and early-epigenetic ordering here can be compared with the clonal-ordering and chromatin–spliceosome findings in AML from the same group ([PMID:27276561](../papers/27276561.md)).
