Atezolizumab monotherapy for metastatic urothelial carcinoma: final analysis from the phase II IMvigor210 trial

TL;DR

This is the final efficacy and safety report of IMvigor210, a global single-arm phase II trial of atezolizumab monotherapy in metastatic urothelial carcinoma (BLCA and UTUC). Atezolizumab is an anti-PD-L1 (CD274) antibody and was given at 1200 mg IV every 21 days. The trial had two cohorts:

  • Cohort 1 (n=119) was untreated and cisplatin-ineligible.
  • Cohort 2 (n=310) had been treated with platinum-based chemotherapy.

At the 1 June 2023 cutoff, median survival follow-up was 96.4 months in cohort 1 and 46.2 months in cohort 2. In cohort 1, the independently reviewed (IRF) RECIST 1.1 objective response rate (ORR) was 23.5%. Median duration of response (DOR) was 59.1 months, median OS was 16.3 months and 5-year OS was 21.6%. In cohort 2, the IRF ORR was 16.5% (27.0% in PD-L1 IC2/3), median OS was 7.9 months and 4-year OS was 15.3%. Early cohort 1 data had suggested that benefit did not depend on PD-L1 status. With longer follow-up, however, PD-L1 IC2/3 tumours had higher 4- and 5-year landmark OS rates than IC0/IC1, although these subgroup analyses were not statistically tested. Treatment-related grade 3/4 adverse events occurred in 21.8% (cohort 1) and 18.7% (cohort 2) of patients, with one treatment-related death (sepsis) and no new safety signals. cBioPortal credits this paper, together with the cohort 1 primary publication PMID:27939400, for the study blca_iatlas_imvigor210_2017 PMID:39642637.

Cohort & data

  • Trial design. IMvigor210 was a global, single-arm, phase II study. This paper registers cohort 1 as NCT02951767 and cohort 2 as NCT02108652. The cohort 1 primary publication PMID:27939400 cited NCT02108652 for its cohort. The final analysis is described as exploratory: its aim was to check that long-term efficacy and safety were consistent with the earlier data, including the primary analyses PMID:39642637.
  • Eligibility. Patients had inoperable locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (UC) of the renal pelvis, ureters, bladder or urethra. They also needed measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 and a tumour sample for PD-L1 testing.
    • Cohort 1 patients had no prior treatment for metastatic UC. Neoadjuvant or adjuvant platinum was allowed if recurrence came more than 12 months after it ended. Patients had to be cisplatin-ineligible, based on GFR >30 to <60 ml/min, grade ≥2 hearing loss or peripheral neuropathy, or ECOG PS 2.
    • Cohort 2 patients had progressed during or after platinum-based chemotherapy. Patients who progressed within 12 months of (neo)adjuvant platinum counted as second-line.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Treatment. Patients received atezolizumab 1200 mg IV every 21 days. Treatment continued until investigator-assessed radiographic progression (cohort 1), loss of clinical benefit (cohort 2) or unacceptable toxicity. Dose reductions were not permitted. Tumours were assessed at baseline, every 9 weeks for 12 months and every 12 weeks thereafter PMID:39642637.
  • Accrual and disposition.
    • Cohort 1: 167 screened and 123 enrolled (9 June 2014 to 30 March 2015). 4 were not treated, leaving 119 treated. The last patient’s last visit was 28 February 2023. All 119 discontinued, most often for progressive disease (81), an adverse event (16) or patient withdrawal (16).
    • Cohort 2: 494 screened and 315 enrolled (13 May 2014 to 19 November 2014). 5 were not treated, leaving 310 treated. The last patient’s last visit was 25 February 2019. Reasons for discontinuation included progressive disease (249), an adverse event (21), patient withdrawal (16) and sponsor study termination (13). Those 13 patients continued atezolizumab in rollover study BO39633 or through commercial supply.
    • Two patients first assigned to cohort 1 and one first assigned to cohort 2 were moved to the other cohort after eligibility was reassessed.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Baseline (cohort 1 / cohort 2).
    • Male: 80.7% / 77.7%.
    • PD-L1 IC2/3: 26.9% / 32.3%.
    • Bladder (or urethra) primary: 71.4% / 75.8%. Eligibility also allowed renal pelvis and ureter primaries (UTUC).
    • Cohort 1: 69.7% were cisplatin-ineligible because of renal impairment.
    • Cohort 2: 81.0% had one or more prior systemic regimens in the inoperable or metastatic setting.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Exposure.
    • Cohort 1: median treatment duration 3.4 months (range 0–59.4). 21.0% were treated for >1 year. The median was 6 doses (range 1–83), and 32.8% received ≥10 doses.
    • Cohort 2: median treatment duration 2.8 months (range 0–52.9). 20.0% were treated for >1 year. The median was 5 doses (range 1–76), and 31.6% received ≥10 doses.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Follow-up. Median survival follow-up was 96.4 months (range 0.2–103.4) in cohort 1 and 46.2 months (range 0.2 [censored]–54.9) in cohort 2. Not every endpoint uses the 2023 cutoff. Cohort 2 ORR and DOR use a 12 July 2017 cutoff and cohort 2 PFS uses a 4 July 2016 cutoff (Table 1 footnotes) PMID:39642637.
  • PD-L1 assay. PD-L1 on tumour-infiltrating immune cells (IC) was scored prospectively at a central site (HistoGeneX, Brussels) with the VENTANA SP142 immunohistochemistry assay (pd-l1-ihc-sp142).
    • Cut-offs were IC2/3 ≥5% of tumour area covered by PD-L1-stained immune cells, IC1 ≥1% and <5%, and IC0 <1%.
    • Cohort 1 had 39 IC0, 48 IC1 and 32 IC2/3 patients.
    • Cohort 2 had 103 IC0, 107 IC1 and 100 IC2/3 patients.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Endpoints and statistics.
    • Cohort 1 primary endpoint: IRF-confirmed ORR per RECIST 1.1.
    • Cohort 2 co-primary endpoints: IRF ORR per RECIST 1.1 and investigator-assessed modified RECIST (mRECIST) ORR.
    • Secondary endpoints: investigator ORR, DOR, PFS, OS and safety (NCI CTCAE v4.0).
    • Statistical methods: ORR CIs by Clopper–Pearson. DOR, PFS and OS by Kaplan-Meier, with Brookmeyer–Crowley CIs for medians and the Greenwood formula for 4- and 5-year OS.
    • Time to onset of response (TTOR) was a post hoc analysis.
    • Analyses of DOR, PFS and OS by PD-L1 status were exploratory and not statistically tested.
    • PMID:39642637
  • cBioPortal dataset. The linked study is blca_iatlas_imvigor210_2017, titled “Metastatic Bladder Urothelial Carcinoma (IMvigor210 Phase II Trial, ESMO Open. 2024) - iAtlas Harmonized”. Its cBioPortal metadata (not this paper) describes whole-exome sequencing and neoantigen data from 347 metastatic bladder UCs with matched normals, reprocessed by CRI iAtlas on hg38. The study is co-credited to PMID:27939400. This paper reports no sequencing, neoantigen or gene-expression data. Its only biomarker is SP142 PD-L1 IHC PMID:39642637.

Key findings

  • Cohort 1 response (IRF, RECIST 1.1).
    • ORR was 23.5% (28/119; 95% CI 16.2–32.2), and 10.1% had a complete response (CR).
    • By PD-L1 status: IC0 20.5% (8/39; 9.3–36.5), IC1 22.9% (11/48; 12.0–37.3) and IC2/3 28.1% (9/32; 13.8–46.8). CR rates were 7.7%, 8.3% and 15.6%.
    • Investigator-assessed ORR was 26.9% (32/119; 19.2–35.8).
    • PMID:39642637
  • Cohort 1 durability.
    • Median IRF TTOR was 2.1 months (95% CI 2.1–3.9).
    • Median IRF DOR was 59.1 months (37.5–93.8) overall, with 16/28 responders having an event. By subgroup it was 40.8 months (12.8–53.5) in IC0, 66.3 months (30.4–NE) in IC1 and 93.8 months (11.1–NE) in IC2/3.
    • Median investigator-assessed DOR was 53.2 months (18.4–84.8).
    • PMID:39642637
  • Cohort 1 PFS. Median IRF PFS was 4.0 months (2.1–4.3) overall: 3.4 months in IC0, 2.1 in IC1 and 4.1 in IC2/3. Median investigator PFS was 4.2 months (2.3–5.8) PMID:39642637.
  • Cohort 1 OS.
    • 96/119 patients (80.7%) had died.
    • Median OS was 16.3 months (10.4–24.5) overall, 20.2 months (6.7–28.7) in IC0, 16.3 months (9.2–33.6) in IC1 and 12.3 months (6.0–49.8) in IC2/3.
    • 1-year OS was 58.1% overall.
    • 4-year OS was 29.0% (20.5–37.5): 21.6% in IC0, 30.8% in IC1 and 36.0% in IC2/3.
    • 5-year OS was 21.6% (13.7–29.5): 15.8% in IC0, 22.6% in IC1 and 27.0% (10.3–43.7) in IC2/3.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Cohort 2 response.
    • IRF RECIST 1.1 ORR was 16.5% (51/310; 12.5–21.1), with a 7.1% CR rate.
    • By PD-L1 status: IC0 8.7% (9/103; 4.1–15.9), IC1 14.0% (15/107; 8.1–22.1) and IC2/3 27.0% (27/100; 18.6–36.8). CR rates were 1.9%, 5.6% and 14.0%.
    • mRECIST ORR was 19.7% (15.4–24.6) overall and 28.0% (19.5–37.9) in IC2/3.
    • These response data use the 12 July 2017 cutoff.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Cohort 2 durability and PFS. Median IRF DOR was 24.8 months (13.8–30.4) overall and 29.7 months (13.8–NE) in IC2/3. The median TTOR, reported earlier, was 2.1 months (2.0–2.2). Median IRF PFS was 2.1 months (2.1–2.1), using the 4 July 2016 cutoff PMID:39642637.
  • Cohort 2 OS.
    • 253/310 patients (81.6%) had died.
    • Median OS was 7.9 months (6.7–9.3) overall, 6.5 months (4.4–8.3) in IC0, 6.7 months in IC1 and 11.9 months (9.0–22.8) in IC2/3.
    • 1-year OS was 36.9% overall and 49.9% in IC2/3.
    • 4-year OS was 15.3% (11.1–19.6) overall, 7.3% in IC0, 9.4% in IC1 and 30.3% (20.6–40.0) in IC2/3.
    • Follow-up was too short for 5-year OS in cohort 2.
    • PMID:39642637
  • PD-L1 relationship changed with follow-up. Early cohort 1 data suggested efficacy regardless of PD-L1 status, because median OS did not rise with PD-L1 expression. With extended follow-up, PD-L1 IC2/3 was associated with higher 4- and 5-year landmark OS rates than IC0/IC1. The authors note that this agrees with IMvigor130 PMID:39642637. In cohort 1, IC2/3 median OS (12.3 months) is still the lowest of the three subgroups, the same value the primary publication reported PMID:27939400.
  • Safety summary (cohort 1 / cohort 2).
    • Any adverse event (AE): 96.6% / 98.1%.
    • Treatment-related AE: 74.8% / 71.3%.
    • Grade 3/4 AE: 53.8% / 62.3%.
    • Grade 3/4 treatment-related AE: 21.8% / 18.7%.
    • Serious AE: 41.2% / 50.0%.
    • Grade 5 AE: 4.2% / 1.0%. The only treatment-related grade 5 event was 1 sepsis death in cohort 1, in a patient with an unidentified source of infection.
    • AE leading to discontinuation: 13.4% / 6.8%.
    • AE leading to dose modification or interruption: 37.0% / 34.8%.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Common treatment-related AEs (cohort 1 / cohort 2). Fatigue 33.6% / 31.0%, pruritus 13.4% / 13.2%, diarrhoea 12.6% / 8.7%, decreased appetite 10.1% / 11.0% and nausea 6.7% / 13.9% PMID:39642637.
  • Immune-mediated AEs of special interest (AESI; cohort 1 / cohort 2).
    • Any AESI: 41.2% / 33.5%. Grade 3/4 AESI: 10.9% / 7.4%. Serious AESI: 5.9% / 5.2%.
    • AESI requiring systemic corticosteroids: 10.9% / 8.1%.
    • AESI leading to withdrawal: 1.7% / 1.3%.
    • By type: rash 25.2% / 21.6%, hepatitis (diagnosis plus lab abnormalities) 14.3% / 10.0%, hypothyroidism 9.2% / 4.2%, infusion-related reactions 4.2% / 1.0%, colitis 3.4% / 1.3% and pneumonitis 2.5% / 3.5%.
    • No AESI deaths occurred in either cohort.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Change since the cohort 1 primary publication. Comparing that report at 17.2 months’ median follow-up PMID:27939400 with this final analysis at 96.4 months PMID:39642637:
    • ORR rose from 23% (27/119) to 23.5% (28/119).
    • CR rate rose from 9% to 10.1%.
    • Median DOR went from not reached to 59.1 months.
    • Median OS went from 15.9 to 16.3 months.
    • Treatment-related AEs rose from 66% to 74.8%, and grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs from 16% to 21.8%.
    • AE-driven discontinuation rose from 8% to 13.4%.

Genes & alterations

  • CD274 (PD-L1) is atezolizumab’s target and the only biomarker in this paper. It was measured as protein on tumour-infiltrating immune cells with the SP142 IHC assay, and the tumour-cell score was not used.
    • In cohort 2, ORR rose with IC score: 8.7% in IC0, 14.0% in IC1 and 27.0% in IC2/3. Median OS was 6.5, 6.7 and 11.9 months, and 4-year OS 7.3%, 9.4% and 30.3%.
    • In cohort 1, the ORR gradient was shallower (20.5%, 22.9%, 28.1%) and median OS ran the other way (20.2, 16.3 and 12.3 months). The 4- and 5-year landmark OS rates, however, were highest in IC2/3 (36.0% and 27.0%, vs 21.6% and 15.8% in IC0).
    • None of these PD-L1 subgroup comparisons were formally tested.
    • PMID:39642637
  • PDCD1 (PD-1) comes up only as the target class of approved checkpoint inhibitors and of an investigational anti-PD-1/LAG-3 bispecific (tobemstomig). It was not measured PMID:39642637.
  • The paper reports no somatic mutation, copy-number, TMB or expression data. Genomic readouts for IMvigor210 cohort 1, such as FoundationOne tumour mutation load and the T-effector signature, are in PMID:27939400. The WES and neoantigen data in blca_iatlas_imvigor210_2017 are not described here.

Clinical implications

  • Durable benefit in a subset. The authors conclude that atezolizumab monotherapy gave clinically meaningful efficacy with durable responses in a subset of metastatic UC patients. The evidence is a cohort 1 median IRF DOR of 59.1 months and 5-year OS of 21.6%. They call this one of the longest datasets for atezolizumab and the longest follow-up of first-line checkpoint-inhibitor (CPI) monotherapy in metastatic UC PMID:39642637.
  • Regulatory history. The authors state that IMvigor210 led to the first approvals of CPI monotherapy with atezolizumab, in both the first-line cisplatin-ineligible and second-line settings PMID:39642637.
  • PD-L1 as a selector. Extended follow-up suggests that PD-L1 IC2/3 status goes with better long-term (landmark) survival, even though median OS in cohort 1 did not track PD-L1. The authors argue that well-selected first-line patients with ultimately non-curable disease might benefit from CPI monotherapy “if a suitable validated biomarker is found in the future”. They point to its better tolerability than cytotoxic chemotherapy PMID:39642637.
  • Safety. Tolerability was consistent with earlier analyses, with no new safety signals, including in patients treated for more than 1 year. The authors say this likely supports CPI-based combination regimens in first-line and later settings. For cross-trial context they give AE-driven discontinuation rates of 9% in IMvigor130 and 7% in IMvigor211, against 13.4% and 6.8% here PMID:39642637.
  • Evolving first-line landscape (context from the discussion).
    • Approved options they list are avelumab switch maintenance after platinum induction (JAVELIN Bladder 100, NCT02603432), enfortumab-vedotin plus pembrolizumab (EV-302/KN-A39, NCT04223856) and nivolumab plus cisplatin/gemcitabine (CheckMate 901, NCT03036098).
    • The IMvigor130 atezolizumab-monotherapy arm (n=360) had a final median OS of 15.2 months. The authors caution that it cannot be compared directly with IMvigor210, because IMvigor130 also enrolled cisplatin-eligible patients.
    • PMID:39642637
  • Earlier-disease trials mentioned.
    • IMvigor011: adjuvant atezolizumab in ctDNA-positive high-risk muscle-invasive UC.
    • NIAGARA: cisplatin/gemcitabine ± durvalumab, which met its event-free survival endpoint and its OS secondary endpoint.
    • Other perioperative trials: KEYNOTE-866, KEYNOTE-B15/EV-304, KEYNOTE-905/EV-303, NCT03661320 and VOLGA (durvalumab ± tremelimumab plus enfortumab vedotin).
    • ALBAN: atezolizumab plus bcg in BCG-naive high-risk non-muscle-invasive disease.
    • S1806: chemoradiotherapy ± atezolizumab (NCT03775265).
    • A phase II trial of tobemstomig ± tiragolumab (anti-TIGIT) versus atezolizumab in platinum-ineligible metastatic UC.
    • PMID:39642637

Limitations & open questions

  • Design. Per the authors, IMvigor210 was a single-arm trial with ORR, not PFS or OS, as its primary endpoint, which limits extrapolation. Cohort 1 was first designed as an exploratory subgroup of 30 patients and later expanded to about 100. The authors present the findings as coming from “a small, non-randomized phase II trial” PMID:39642637.
  • Tension with the randomized confirmatory trial. The authors call cohort 2 consistent with the phase III IMvigor211 trial, which used the same hierarchical PD-L1 testing design (IC2/3, then IC1/2/3, then ITT). IMvigor211, however, did not meet its OS primary endpoint. The single-arm survival figures here therefore cannot establish benefit over chemotherapy PMID:39642637.
  • The PD-L1 landmark-OS signal is exploratory and imprecise. The PD-L1 subgroup analyses of DOR, PFS and OS had no statistical testing. The cohort 1 IC2/3 subgroup is small (n=32), with wide CIs: 5-year OS 27.0% (10.3–43.7) and DOR 93.8 months (11.1–NE). This paper’s IC2/3 landmark-OS advantage sits alongside the earlier cohort 1 finding that PD-L1 IC status did not enrich response or median OS PMID:27939400, PMID:39642637. Both observations can hold at once, since median and landmark OS measure different parts of the survival curve. Whether SP142 IC status identifies long-term survivors in cisplatin-ineligible disease therefore remains open.
  • Mixed cutoffs. Cohort 2 ORR, DOR and PFS were not updated to the 2023 cutoff (they use 12 July 2017 and 4 July 2016). Only cohort 2 OS and safety reflect final follow-up PMID:39642637.
  • Text vs table inconsistency. The results text gives cohort 2 IC1 median OS as 6.7 months with a 95% CI of 22.5–39.1 months. That interval cannot contain its own median. Table 1 gives 6.7 (5.4–9.2), and 22.5–40.2 is the Table 1 CI for the IC1 1-year OS rate PMID:39642637.
  • PFS differs from the primary publication. Cohort 1 median IRF PFS is 4.0 months (2.1–4.3) here, while the cohort 1 primary publication reported 2.7 months (2.1–4.2) PMID:27939400, PMID:39642637. The final analysis does not explain the difference, which may reflect a different assessor or definition between reports.
  • Trial registration. This paper lists NCT02951767 for cohort 1, whereas the cohort 1 primary publication cited NCT02108652 PMID:27939400, PMID:39642637. Anyone joining trial records should be aware of both identifiers.
  • Clinical subgroups not updated. The final analysis does not update the subgroup results from PMID:27939400, for example the 39% ORR in upper-tract (UTUC) primaries or the effect of Bajorin risk factors. The long-term fate of the upper-tract signal is unknown.
  • The paper and the dataset cover different ground. The cBioPortal study credited to this paper holds WES and neoantigen data on 347 tumours (per cBioPortal metadata). This paper reports only clinical outcomes and SP142 IHC, and it does not describe the sequencing, sample selection or the link between the 347 samples and the 429 treated patients (119 + 310). The paper’s data-sharing statement says individual-level data are available through Vivli and should not be linked to external sources because of re-identification risk PMID:39642637.
