Sotigalimab and/or nivolumab with chemotherapy in first-line metastatic pancreatic cancer: clinical and immunologic analyses from the randomized phase 2 PRINCE trial

TL;DR

The PRINCE trial (NCT03214250) is a randomized, open-label phase 1b/2 study of first-line metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PAAD) testing gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel chemotherapy combined with the anti-PD-1 antibody nivolumab, the CD40-agonist antibody sotigalimab, or both. In 105 efficacy-evaluable patients, the primary endpoint (1-year OS versus a 35% historical control) was met for nivo/chemo (57.7%, P = 0.006, n = 34) but not for sotiga/chemo (48.1%, P = 0.062, n = 36) or sotiga/nivo/chemo (41.3%, P = 0.233, n = 35); ORR, PFS and DCR did not clearly separate the arms. Extensive multi-omic profiling of paired blood and tumor samples identified largely non-overlapping, treatment-arm-specific correlates of survival — a diverse pre-treatment circulating T cell response (especially CD4 subsets) for nivo/chemo, and cross-presenting dendritic cells plus circulating and tumor CD4 T cell activity for sotiga/chemo — while the triple-combination arm showed no clear predictive biomarker and evidence of possible immune over-activation. Genomic profiling by whole-exome sequencing (WES) confirmed treatment arms were balanced for KRAS, SMAD4 and TP53 mutation frequencies, with a single MSI-high tumor and a single pathogenic BRCA2 variant identified.

Cohort & data

  • Trial: PRINCE, a Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy (PICI) pancreas cancer consortium study conducted at 7 US academic centers; randomized, open-label, multi-center, seamless phase 1b/2 design (NCT03214250) PMID:35662283.
  • Population: Treatment-naive first-line metastatic PAAD (mPDAC), ECOG 0-1.
  • Enrollment: 130 assessed for eligibility; 99 randomly allocated in phase 2 (37 nivo/chemo, 31 sotiga/chemo, 31 sotiga/nivo/chemo); 6 randomized patients were not dosed and excluded from analysis.
  • Efficacy population (n=105): 34 nivo/chemo, 36 sotiga/chemo, 35 sotiga/nivo/chemo — comprising 93 randomized-and-dosed phase 2 patients plus 12 dose-limiting-toxicity (DLT)-evaluable patients enrolled at the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) during the non-randomized phase 1b portion (6 each added to sotiga/chemo and sotiga/nivo/chemo).
  • Safety population (n=108): 36 nivo/chemo, 37 sotiga/chemo, 35 sotiga/nivo/chemo.
  • Follow-up: Median 24.2 months (IQR 20.5-26.3) at the 24 March 2021 data cutoff; based on 78 (74%) deaths.
  • Treatment arms: gemcitabine (1,000 mg/m²) + nab-paclitaxel (125 mg/m²) IV days 1, 8, 15 of a 28-day cycle (“chemo”) combined with: nivolumab 240 mg IV days 1 and 15 (“nivo/chemo”); sotigalimab (anti-CD40 agonist, RP2D 0.3 mg/kg) IV day 3 (“sotiga/chemo”); or both (“sotiga/nivo/chemo”).
  • Biospecimens: Serial peripheral blood (PBMC + serum) collected at screening, C1D1, C1D15, C2-4D1, and end of treatment; paired pre-treatment and on-treatment (~cycle 2) tumor biopsies (fresh-frozen or FFPE).
  • Multi-omic profiling: Whole-exome and transcriptome sequencing of tumor/normal pairs via ImmunoID NeXT (Personalis); bulk tumor RNA-seq (liver-metastasis biopsies only, 64% of biopsies, to avoid tissue-of-origin confounding); seven-color multiplexed immunofluorescence (mIF) of tumor tissue; 28-color multiparameter flow cytometry (BD Symphony A5, “X50”) and mass cytometry (CyTOF) of PBMCs; Olink proximity-extension serum proteomics (172 analytes across two Target96 panels).
  • Genomics: 63 (60%) patients had pre-treatment tumor of sufficient quality for WES; treatment arms were balanced for somatic mutation frequencies in KRAS, SMAD4 and TP53.
  • Dataset: Deposited as the cBioPortal/iAtlas-harmonized study paad_iatlas_prince_2022; de-identified clinical, RNA-seq and cell-proportion summary data are also on GitHub (ParkerICI/prince-trial-data).

Key findings

  • Primary endpoint (1-year OS vs. 35% historical control, gem/nab-paclitaxel benchmark): Met for nivo/chemo — 57.7% (one-sided 95% lower CI 41.7%, P = 0.006); not met for sotiga/chemo — 48.1% (lower CI 33.7%, P = 0.062); not met for sotiga/nivo/chemo — 41.3% (lower CI 27.0%, P = 0.233) PMID:35662283.
  • Median OS: 16.7 months (95% CI 9.8-18.4) for nivo/chemo; 11.4 months (7.2-20.1) for sotiga/chemo; 10.1 months (7.9-13.2) for sotiga/nivo/chemo.
  • Secondary endpoints (nivo/chemo, sotiga/chemo, sotiga/nivo/chemo, respectively): median PFS 6.4 / 7.3 / 6.7 months; investigator-assessed ORR 50% / 33% / 31%; DCR 74% / 78% / 69%; median DOR 7.4 / 5.6 / 7.9 months. No clear separation between arms on ORR or PFS; study was not powered for between-arm comparison and no multiplicity adjustment was applied.
  • Post hoc subgroup analysis: The nivo/chemo survival benefit was not attributable solely to imbalances in initial stage, prior chemotherapy, MSI status, KRAS wild-type status (balanced across arms), or BRCA status. The single MSI-high tumor (nivo/chemo arm) had OS of only 8.1 months, despite MSI-H generally predicting immunotherapy benefit.
  • Safety: 106/108 (98%) patients had ≥1 treatment-related adverse event (TRAE); adverse events of special interest occurred in 92 (87%). Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurred in 0%, 9/37 (24%) and 12/35 (34%) of nivo/chemo, sotiga/chemo and sotiga/nivo/chemo patients respectively (no grade 4/5 CRS). Two on-study deaths were assessed as possibly drug-related (acute hepatic failure, sotiga/chemo; intracranial hemorrhage, sotiga/nivo/chemo).
  • Pharmacodynamics common to all arms: Increases in circulating proliferating (Ki-67+) and activated (HLA-DR+) non-naive CD4/CD8 T cells, and increases in serum IFN-γ, CXCL9 and CXCL10; earlier kinetics in nivo-containing arms. Soluble PD-1 rose only in nivo-containing arms.
  • Correlates of survival, nivo/chemo: Higher pre-treatment circulating CD38+ effector-memory (EM) CD8 T cells (log-rank P = 0.047), PD-1+CD39+ EM1 CD4 T cells (P = 0.0037) and follicular helper (CXCR5+PD-1+) CD4 T cells (P < 0.0001) associated with longer OS; tumor TNF-α/NFκB signature was uniquely associated with shorter survival in this arm; lower intratumoral iNOS+ macrophage infiltration associated with longer survival.
  • Correlates of survival, sotiga/chemo: Higher pre-treatment circulating CD1c+CD141+ cross-presenting dendritic cells (P = 0.004), higher on-treatment CD141+ DCs and conventional DCs (P = 0.019-0.043), higher pre-treatment PD-1+Tbet+ (P = 0.016) and Tbet+Eomes+ (P = 0.013) non-naive CD4 T cells, and higher pre-treatment circulating HLA-DR+CCR7+ B cells associated with longer OS. Tumor Th1/Th2/IFN-γ gene-expression signatures associated with longer survival; TGF-β and E2F/glycolysis signatures with shorter survival; non-proliferating (Ki-67-) CD4 T cell (conventional and Foxp3+ regulatory) infiltrate associated with longer survival.
  • Correlates of survival, sotiga/nivo/chemo: No consistent benefit and largely non-overlapping/absent biomarkers versus the monotherapy-immunotherapy arms; lower activated CD38+ non-naive CD4 and CD8 T cells and lower on-treatment CCR7+CD11b+CD27- B cells associated with longer survival, consistent with a hypothesized state of excessive/exhausting T cell activation from dual immunotherapy.

Genes & alterations

  • KRAS — Somatic mutation frequency balanced across the three treatment arms by WES (n=63 profiled tumors). Post hoc subgroup analysis found no evidence that KRAS wild-type status (balanced across arms) explained the nivo/chemo survival benefit.
  • SMAD4 — Somatic mutation frequency balanced across arms by WES; no alteration-specific survival association reported.
  • TP53 — Somatic mutation frequency balanced across arms by WES; no alteration-specific survival association reported.
  • BRCA2 — One pathogenic tumor indel variant (BRCA2 c.5946delT) identified, in a patient on sotiga/nivo/chemo who had a partial response but withdrew consent at 2.8 months (1-year OS status unknown).
  • MSI status (not gene-level in this paper) — One tumor (nivo/chemo arm) was MSI-high; that patient’s OS was 8.1 months, notably short despite the MSI-H genotype.

Clinical implications

  • Nivo/chemo met its pre-specified survival bar (1-year OS 57.7% vs. 35% historical) in an all-comer first-line mPDAC population, but ORR/PFS did not clearly differ from the other arms and the trial lacked a concurrent chemotherapy-only control, so causal attribution of the OS gain to nivolumab is not definitive.
  • Sotiga/chemo and sotiga/nivo/chemo did not meet the primary endpoint, and the authors explicitly state the data do not support further all-comer trials of these regimens in mPDAC.
  • Biomarker-selected trial design recommended for future studies. T cell subsets (nivo/chemo) and DC/CD4 T cell subsets (sotiga/chemo) are proposed as candidate patient-selection biomarkers for prospective, minimally invasive (blood-based) biomarker-enriched trial designs; predictive value has not yet been validated prospectively or against a chemotherapy-only control arm.
  • CD4, not CD8, T cells implicated as the operative compartment. Circulating antigen-experienced CD8 T cells and tumor-infiltrating CD8 T cells — canonical IO biomarkers in other solid tumors — were not associated with OS in either immunotherapy arm; tumor infiltrates were predominantly CD4 T cells, and CD4 T cell subsets drove most survival associations.
  • Dual immunotherapy (CD40 agonist + PD-1 blockade) showed no benefit and a signal of possible antagonism — attenuated pharmacodynamic effects and a distinct CD38+ T cell/regulatory-B-cell phenotype associated with shorter survival, consistent with preclinical reports that CD40 agonism can impair PD-1 blockade responses via regulatory B cell induction.

Limitations & open questions

  • No concurrent chemotherapy-only control arm (by design, to accelerate accrual); OS was instead benchmarked against a historical rate (35%) and a post hoc synthetic control arm derived from PRINCE’s top-enrolling sites (45% 1-year OS), and against a subsequent phase 3 trial reporting ~40-45% 1-year OS with gem/nab-paclitaxel — narrowing, but not eliminating, the apparent OS benefit of adding nivolumab or sotigalimab.
  • Not powered for between-arm comparison; no adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied to the exploratory biomarker P values, which the authors state should be used only for ranking candidate biomarkers, not interpreted as confirmatory statistics.
  • Small, single-consortium cohort (7 US academic centers), raising possible referral/selection bias; efficacy population sizes were 34-36 per arm.
  • Tumor gene-expression analyses restricted to liver-metastasis biopsies (64% of biopsies) to avoid tissue-of-origin confounding on bulk RNA-seq, limiting generalizability to other metastatic sites; mIF, in contrast, was not restricted by biopsy site.
  • Small paired pre-/on-treatment biopsy numbers (n = 3-6 per arm for some mIF comparisons) limit confidence in pharmacodynamic tumor findings.
  • Biomarker validation requires prospective testing with a chemotherapy control arm to distinguish predictive (treatment-specific) from purely prognostic biomarkers; the authors note the low overlap between arm-specific biomarkers as indirect evidence against a pure chemotherapy-prognosis effect, but this is not definitive.
  • Open question: Why the single MSI-high tumor in this cohort had comparatively short OS (8.1 months) despite MSI-H generally predicting immunotherapy benefit in other cancers.
  • Open question: The mechanistic basis for antagonism (rather than synergy) between CD40 agonism and PD-1 blockade in the triple-combination arm, including whether the implicated CCR7+CD11b+CD27- regulatory B cell population is causally responsible.
