Chromoplexy Is a Frequent Early Clonal Event in EWSR1-Rearranged Round Cell Sarcomas That Can Be Detected Using Clinically Validated Targeted Sequencing Panels

Authors

Josephine K Dermawan

Emily Slotkin

William D Tap

Paul Meyers

Leonard Wexler

John Healey

Fabio Vanoli

Chad M Vanderbilt

Cristina R Antonescu

Doi

10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-23-2573

PMID: 38335254 · DOI: 10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-23-2573 · Journal: Cancer Research (2024)

TL;DR

Dermawan, Antonescu and colleagues at MSKCC asked whether chromoplexy (chained, multigene chromosomal rearrangements) can be detected with a clinically validated targeted DNA panel. They ran the MSK-IMPACT pipeline’s DELLY structural-variant (SV) calls on 277 EWSR1-rearranged round cell sarcomas: 173 Ewing sarcomas (ES) (147 EWSR1::FLI1, 26 EWSR1::ERG) and 104 EWSR1::WT1 desmoplastic small round cell tumors (DSRCT). Chromoplexy was detected in 31% of ES (24% EWSR1::FLI1 vs 42% EWSR1::ERG) and 19% of DSRCT. EWSR1 sat at the core of 76–93% of these events and was rearranged with many non-canonical partners, with a major breakpoint cluster at EWSR1 exons 8–13. Longitudinal sequencing and breakpoint allele fractions indicated that chromoplexy is an early clonal event that likely arises together with the driver fusion and persists through progression. Chromoplexy was significantly associated with metastatic disease at presentation in both ES and DSRCT, but not with overall or progression-free survival. The authors describe this as the first study of chromoplexy in DSRCT and the first to show it can be detected with clinically validated targeted NGS panels PMID:38335254.

Cohort & data

  • Discovery cohort: 277 EWSR1-rearranged small blue round cell tumors identified from the MSK-IMPACT database, with fusions confirmed by targeted DNA (MSK-IMPACT) or RNA (MSK-Archer) sequencing: 173 ES (147 EWSR1::FLI1, 26 EWSR1::ERG) and 104 EWSR1::WT1 DSRCT PMID:38335254.
  • Control cohort: 50 TMPRSS2::ERG-fused prostate adenocarcinomas (PRAD), checked for non-canonical SVs involving TMPRSS2 PMID:38335254.
  • External validation cohort: matched tumor-normal whole-exome sequencing from 26 ES patients, downloaded from the NCI_EwingSarcoma dbGaP repository (phs000804; phs000804.v1.p1 in the data-availability statement). The paper attributes this to Crompton et al. 2014 (its ref. 3). SVs were re-called with Delly v2 PMID:38335254.
  • Dataset: es_dsrct_msk_2023 holds the MSK-IMPACT data. Analysis code is at github.com/dermawaj/chromoplexy_EWSR1 PMID:38335254.
  • DNA assay: MSK-IMPACT, a matched tumor-normal hybridization-capture panel (the panel version is not specified). Samples needed at least 100X coverage per exon in at least 98% of targeted exons. Per-sample coverage ranged from 122 to 1835X (median 574X, mean 602X). Reads were aligned to GRCh37/hg19. SNVs and indels were called with MuTect and VarDict, requiring tumor VAF ≥1% and more than 5× the matched-normal frequency PMID:38335254.
  • RNA assay: MSK-Archer, a 123-gene anchored multiplex PCR panel using the Archer FusionPlex protocol on FFPE RNA PMID:38335254.
  • SV calling and chromoplexy definition:
    • DELLY called SVs from tumor and matched-normal reads. Filters: mapping quality ≥20, length >500 bp, and at least 5 supporting paired-end or split reads.
    • Breakpoint allele fraction (BAF) was the number of reads spanning the breakpoint divided by the total reads of the involved genes at that breakpoint.
    • For targeted sequencing, chromoplexy was defined as more than one SV in the same sample sharing at least one gene partner.
    • Chaining (open chains or closed loops) was confirmed with the genome-graph “gWalk” function in gGnome.
    • A deletion bridge was called when a deletion spanned, or sat next to, one or both rearrangement breakpoints PMID:38335254.
  • Copy number:
    • Arm-level gains and losses were called with ASCETS, keeping events present in at least 15% of samples.
    • Fraction of genome altered (FGA) was the fraction of the genome with log2 gain >0.5 or loss <−0.5 PMID:38335254.
  • Clinical correlates:
    • Variables tested: age, stage at presentation (localized vs metastatic), PFS and OS, FGA, chr8 gain, TP53 and STAG2 mutations in ES, and ARID1A mutations in DSRCT.
    • Survival was analyzed with Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests, including only patients with at least 1 year of follow-up PMID:38335254.

Key findings

  • Chromoplexy frequency. Chromoplexy with EWSR1 at its core was found in:
    • 46 ES (31%): 35 of the EWSR1::FLI1 cases (24%) and 11 of the EWSR1::ERG cases (42%), chi-square P = 0.03.
    • 20 DSRCT (19%) PMID:38335254.
  • EWSR1 is the hub. EWSR1 accounted for 76–93% of chromoplectic events, compared with ERG (7%) and WT1 (24%). FLI1 is not on the panel, so most FLI1-only SVs would be missed. Besides the canonical fusion, EWSR1 was highly promiscuous within individual tumors. Its non-canonical partners across the genome included the cancer-related genes ATR, EGFL7, ETS1, EP300, EZR, FOSB, NAB2 and ZNF331 PMID:38335254.
  • Non-canonical fusions are not transcribed. Of the 66 cases with chromoplexy, 20 (30%) also had MSK-Archer RNA sequencing. The non-canonical EWSR1 fusions were not expressed at the mRNA level, even when low-confidence fusion calls were included PMID:38335254.
  • Method control in prostate cancer. Non-canonical TMPRSS2 translocations and inversions were found in 17 of 50 (34%) TMPRSS2::ERG prostate carcinomas. This reproduces the chained-rearrangement pattern previously described in prostate cancer (PMID:23622249) PMID:38335254.
  • SV classes.
    • Interchromosomal translocations made up most chromoplectic events: 91% in EWSR1::FLI1, 92% in EWSR1::ERG and 94% in EWSR1::WT1 tumors.
    • Intrachromosomal inversions were next: 7.5%, 4% and 6% respectively.
    • Deletions made up about 1% in the FLI1 and ERG groups.
    • The inter- vs intrachromosomal mix did not differ between the unbalanced EWSR1::ERG fusion and the balanced EWSR1::FLI1 and EWSR1::WT1 fusions PMID:38335254.
  • EWSR1 breakpoint cluster. A major breakpoint cluster was found on chr22, centered on the EWSR1 locus, where breakpoints were less than 1 kb apart. No such cluster was seen on chr11 or chr21. Of EWSR1 events, 4–6% were inversions, with no difference across the three fusion types. EWSR1 (NM_013986) breakpoints were enriched at exons 8–13 whether the partner was canonical or non-canonical. Exon 8 of NM_013986 corresponds to exon 7 of NM_015243, a frequent breakpoint in EWSR1 fusions PMID:38335254.
  • Early, persistent clonal event.
    • Longitudinal sequencing covered 7 ES (median 10.5 months between samples, range 3–34) and 5 DSRCT (median 13 months, range 7–55). Chromoplexy was already present in the primary (or first-sequenced) tumor and stayed detectable at recurrence or metastasis.
    • In contrast, in more than half of these cases additional mutations or copy-number changes were acquired only in the later tumor.
    • BAF distributions of canonical and non-canonical EWSR1 SVs did not differ (Kolmogorov–Smirnov test). Purity-normalized BAFs stayed stable across sequential samples. The authors conclude that chromoplectic events likely arose at the same time as the driver fusion, in the same clonal event PMID:38335254.
  • No link to age or secondary alterations.
    • Chromoplexy did not correlate with age at diagnosis in ES or DSRCT.
    • In ES it was not associated with more TP53 or CDKN2A alterations, with chr8 gain, or with %CNV/FGA.
    • In EWSR1::ERG ES, chromoplexy was mutually exclusive with STAG2 mutations.
    • In DSRCT, chromoplexy was mutually exclusive with ARID1A alterations.
    • EWSR1::FLI1 and EWSR1::ERG ES did not differ in number of secondary alterations or in FGA PMID:38335254.
  • Architecture: open chains, closed loops, deletion bridges.
    • Most events were open chains, including 3-way and 5-way EWSR1 translocations. Occasional closed loops formed, for example triangles linking EWSR1, WT1 and a third gene.
    • Deletion bridges disrupted regions next to breakpoints:
      • 11q12–q24 (next to FLI1), containing ATM: 8 cases.
      • 22q11–q13, containing EWSR1 and CHEK2: 5 cases.
      • 1p35–p36 and 3p13: 1 case each.
      • 13q33–34: 2 cases. The Figure 5B legend labels 13q33 with IRS2 PMID:38335254.
  • External WES validation. Among the 26 dbGaP ES exomes:
    • 2 cases had chains of interchromosomal translocations (including EWSR1::FLI1) and intrachromosomal inversions linked by deletion bridges.
    • A third case had chromoplectic translocations involving 10 chromosomes PMID:38335254.
  • Chromoplexy and metastasis at presentation.
    • ES: metastatic at presentation in 14 of 45 (31%) cases with chromoplexy vs 13 of 126 (10%) without, chi-square P = 0.004.
    • DSRCT: 3 of 20 (15%) vs 2 of 74 (2.7%), chi-square P = 0.03.
    • In ES, TP53 or STAG2 mutations, CDKN2A deletions and chr8 gain were not associated with metastatic status PMID:38335254.
  • Survival.
    • ES by fusion type (median follow-up 8.9 years for EWSR1::FLI1 and 5.7 years for EWSR1::ERG):
      • 5-year OS 70% vs 62% (log-rank P = 0.23).
      • 5-year PFS 42% vs 47% (P = 0.76).
    • DSRCT (median follow-up 4.0 years): 5-year OS 35% and PFS 13%.
    • Metastatic vs localized ES at presentation: worse OS (median 4.6 vs 8.9 years, log-rank P = 0.0047).
    • Among localized cases, chromoplexy did not change OS or PFS in ES or DSRCT.
    • Chromoplexy was not enriched in aggressive ES, defined as progression within 2 years. It was present in 2 of 11 (18%) patients with more than 10-year OS and 11 of 76 (14%) with less than 5-year survival.
    • Among localized ES, chr8 gain predicted worse OS (median 6.6 vs 11.3 years, log-rank P = 0.034) but not PFS (3.1 vs 4.1 years, P = 0.11) PMID:38335254.

Genes & alterations

  • EWSR1: the driver fusion gene and hub of chromoplexy, involved in 76–93% of chromoplectic events. It has a major breakpoint cluster, with breakpoints concentrated in exons 8–13 (NM_013986) for both canonical and non-canonical partners. Inversions within chr22 cluster around exon 8 PMID:38335254.
  • FLI1: partner in EWSR1::FLI1 ES (n = 147), where chromoplexy occurred in 24% of cases. FLI1 is not on MSK-IMPACT, so FLI1-only SVs are under-detected. An 11q12–q24 deletion bridge sits next to FLI1 PMID:38335254.
  • ERG: partner in EWSR1::ERG ES (n = 26; unbalanced fusion), where chromoplexy occurred in 42%, versus 24% for FLI1 (P = 0.03). ERG accounted for 7% of chromoplectic events. ERG is also the partner in the TMPRSS2::ERG prostate control PMID:38335254.
  • WT1: partner in EWSR1::WT1 DSRCT (n = 104). Chromoplexy was found in 19% of DSRCT, and WT1 accounted for 24% of events. Closed-loop triangles linking EWSR1, WT1 and a third gene were seen PMID:38335254.
  • TMPRSS2: non-canonical TMPRSS2 SVs were found in 17 of 50 (34%) TMPRSS2::ERG prostate carcinomas, validating the method PMID:38335254.
  • TP53 and CDKN2A: no association with chromoplexy in ES. This contradicts Anderson et al. 2018 (Science, not in corpus), which reported more TP53 alterations in ES with chromoplexy. Neither gene was associated with metastatic presentation PMID:38335254.
  • STAG2: mutations were mutually exclusive with chromoplexy in EWSR1::ERG ES and were not associated with metastatic presentation PMID:38335254. STAG2 and TP53 are established prognostic secondary alterations in ES (PMID:25223734).
  • ARID1A: alterations were mutually exclusive with chromoplexy in DSRCT PMID:38335254.
  • ATM (11q22, 8 cases), CHEK2 (22q12, 5 cases) and IRS2 (13q33): lie in deletion bridges next to chromoplectic breakpoints. The authors suggest these deletions could cause loss of tumor-suppressor function PMID:38335254.
  • ATR, EGFL7, ETS1, EP300, EZR, FOSB, NAB2, ZNF331: cancer-related genes among the non-canonical EWSR1 partners in chromoplectic SVs. None of these rearrangements was expressed by RNA sequencing PMID:38335254.

Clinical implications

  • Detectable on routine panels. Clinically validated targeted DNA panels such as MSK-IMPACT can capture chromoplexy through their standard SV caller (DELLY). This could add information beyond the SNV and CNA calls currently reported for translocation-associated sarcomas PMID:38335254.
  • Linked to metastatic presentation. Chromoplexy was significantly enriched in ES (P = 0.004) and DSRCT (P = 0.03) that were metastatic at diagnosis. Metastatic presentation itself predicted worse OS in ES (median 4.6 vs 8.9 years, P = 0.0047) PMID:38335254.
  • Not independently prognostic. Chromoplexy was not associated with OS or PFS in ES or DSRCT, including among localized cases, and was not enriched in aggressive (progression within 2 years) ES. Its prognostic value is described as uncertain PMID:38335254.
  • Chr8 gain. In localized ES, chr8 gain predicted worse OS (median 6.6 vs 11.3 years, P = 0.034) PMID:38335254.
  • No therapeutic implications are claimed.

Limitations & open questions

  • Panel blind spots. A targeted panel only captures SVs in which at least one partner gene is on the panel. FLI1 is not on MSK-IMPACT, and the authors say many chromoplectic rearrangements would have been missed. This is called a major limitation and may explain some differences from earlier WGS studies PMID:38335254.
  • Relaxed definitions. Chromoplexy was defined operationally as more than one SV sharing a gene partner. Deletion bridges did not have to span both breakpoints. Both choices are looser than WGS-based definitions (Anderson et al. 2018; prostate cancer work in PMID:23622249) PMID:38335254.
  • Conflict with Anderson et al. 2018. That study reported more TP53 alterations and higher relapse with chromoplexy in ES, and loop rearrangements in all EWSR1::ERG tumors vs 48% of EWSR1::FLI1 tumors. This paper found no TP53 association and no survival association, although it also found chromoplexy enriched in EWSR1::ERG tumors (42% vs 24%) PMID:38335254.
  • Biology unresolved. Chromoplectic rearrangements were not transcribed in the 20 cases with RNA data. It remains unclear whether the partners are random or pathway-biased, and whether they mainly cause loss of tumor-suppressor function through deletion bridges. Germline predisposition, including germline TP53, was not assessed and is proposed for future work PMID:38335254.
  • Small longitudinal subset. Clonal timing rests on 7 ES and 5 DSRCT patients with serial samples, plus BAF comparisons PMID:38335254.
  • Internal inconsistencies to note when synthesizing:
    • ES chromoplexy is reported as 46 cases (31%), but 46/173 is about 27%.
    • The metastatic-presentation analysis uses 45 chromoplexy-positive ES and 94 DSRCT, so the denominators differ from the full cohort.
    • The Discussion names ATM and CHEK1 as deleted tumor suppressors, while the Results and Figure 5B describe CHEK2 (22q12) in deletion bridges PMID:38335254.
  • Validation cohort provenance. The WES cohort is identified only by dbGaP phs000804 and the Crompton et al. 2014 citation. It is not mapped here to a cBioPortal study PMID:38335254.
