TL;DR

Cowzer, Harding and colleagues analyzed 1,254 patients with biliary tract cancer (BTC) whose tumors were sequenced with MSK-IMPACT at Memorial Sloan Kettering (April 2014 to September 2022). The cohort comprised 767 intrahepatic cholangiocarcinomas (IHCH), 210 extrahepatic cholangiocarcinomas (EHCH) and 277 gallbladder cancers (GBC). They mapped the genomic landscape and OncoKB actionability of each anatomic subtype, compared outcomes with genomically matched targeted therapy against unmatched therapy, and used paired pre- and post-treatment samples to look for resistance mechanisms. 32.2% of patients had an OncoKB level 1/2 alteration (iCCA 40%, eCCA 15%, GBC 22%). Emerging targets included KRAS alterations (17%), MTAP deletions (12.8%), MDM2 amplification (6.5%) and MET amplification (1.5%). In second line, matched targeted therapy was linked to longer progression-free survival than 5-FU-based chemotherapy (median 6.6 vs 3.3 months; P = .005). Overall survival was not significantly different (median 17.0 vs 11.0 months; HR 0.8; P = .16). In the paired samples, ERBB2-driven tumors often lost the ERBB2 alteration. Tumors driven by IDH1, FGFR2, BRAF and NTRK kept their driver. Acquired resistance involved RAS, MEK, MET, MYC and CDKN2A alterations. The data are released as the cBioPortal study biliary_tract_msk_2026 (PMID:42360806).

Cohort & data

  • Patients and samples: 1,254 patients (1,285 samples, including paired pre-treatment and progression samples plus plasma ctDNA) enrolled on MSK’s prospective clinical genomic profiling protocol (NCT01775072; MSK IRB 12-245) between April 2014 and September 2022. Samples with tumor purity below 10% or no somatic alterations were excluded (n = 111). For the landscape and outcome analyses, one sample per patient was used, preferring primary tumors and higher-quality samples (PMID:42360806).
  • Cancer types: biliary tract cancer, split into intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (IHCH) 767 (61.2%), extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (EHCH) 210 (16.8%) and gallbladder cancer (GBC) 277 (22.1%). iCCA and eCCA both fall under cholangiocarcinoma (PMID:42360806).
  • Demographics (Table 1): median age 65 (range 21–89). 651 female (52%) and 603 male (48%); GBC was 68.6% female. Self-reported race: Caucasian 931 (74.2%), Asian 129 (10.2%), African American 79 (6.2%). By ADMIXTURE genetic ancestry, 60.1% were European. 879 primary (70%) vs 375 metastatic (30%) samples were sequenced (PMID:42360806).
  • Dataset: biliary_tract_msk_2026 on cBioPortal (summary-level data). Deidentified patient-level data are available on request (PMID:42360806).
  • Assay: tumor and matched-normal MSK-IMPACT hybridization-capture NGS on four panel versions: IMPACT341 (n = 55), IMPACT410 (n = 200), IMPACT468 (n = 663) and IMPACT505 (n = 336). Analyses were adjusted for differences in gene content between panel versions. Selected cases also had cfDNA profiling with MSK-ACCESS (assay described in PMID:34145282) (PMID:42360806).
  • Annotation: oncogenicity and actionability came from OncoKB (OncoKB annotation). The March 2017 (v1.8) and July 2023 (v4.7) releases were compared to measure how actionability grew over time. The potentially actionable cohort (n = 514) included tumors with an OncoKB level 1, 2 or 3A alteration, or a selected level 3B alteration: BRAF non-V600E, BRCA1, BRCA2, FGFR1, FGFR3, IDH2 or MET (PMID:42360806).
  • Clinically eligible cohort (n = 413): the actionable cohort minus 46 patients with missing survival data and 55 with incomplete follow-up. 176 (43%) received matched targeted therapy based on NGS; 237 (57%) did not (the “unmatched” group). To reduce lead-time bias, OS was measured from the start of second-line therapy. The treated group was limited to patients who received matched therapy in second line (n = 100) and the untreated group to patients who received two or more lines of systemic therapy (n = 113) (PMID:42360806).
  • Genomic methods:
    • TMB: nonsynonymous mutations per Mb.
    • Copy number and clonality: FACETS/facets-suite for purity-corrected copy number and cancer cell fraction (CCF). A mutation was called subclonal when the upper 95% CI bound of its CCF was below 1 and P(CCF < 0.5) was above 0.95.
    • Pathways: TCGA curated pathway templates (see PMID:29625050).
    • MSI: MSIsensor score ≥10 classified 16 tumors as MSI. MiMSI (PMID:39746944) was run on all TMB ≥10 tumors (n = 72) and reclassified 10 more as MSI. 2 tumors were hypermutant (TMB ≥30) but MSS by both methods (PMID:42360806).
  • Statistics: Mann-Whitney U and Fisher’s exact tests; Kaplan-Meier with log-rank tests; Cox proportional hazards for multivariable OS/PFS; Benjamini-Hochberg correction; R 4.3.2. PFS ran from treatment start to investigator-assessed progression or death (PMID:42360806).

Key findings

Genomic landscape by anatomic subtype

  • 1,226/1,254 tumors (97.8%) were MSS and 26/1,254 (2.1%) MSI-H. MSI-H rates were similar across subtypes: iCCA 12/767 (1.5%), GBC 6/277 (2.2%), eCCA 8/210 (3.8%) (PMID:42360806).
  • Median TMB was 3.3 mut/Mb overall (range 0–74.6) and 3.0 mut/Mb in MSS tumors (range 0–29). 48 MSS tumors were TMB-high (≥10 mut/Mb). Among MSS tumors, GBC had higher median TMB than iCCA (4.1 vs 2.6 mut/Mb; P = .002) and higher median fraction genome altered (0.495 vs 0.43; P = .014) (PMID:42360806).
  • Whole-genome doubling was more common in eCCA (34.2%; P = .002) and GBC (32%; P = .001) than in iCCA (20.7%) (PMID:42360806).
  • The most frequently altered genes in MSS tumors were TP53 (36%), CDKN2A (21%), ARID1A (18%), KRAS (17%) and IDH1/2 (16%) (PMID:42360806).
  • IDH1, FGFR2, BAP1 and PBRM1 alterations were enriched in iCCA compared with both eCCA and GBC. TP53, SMAD4 and MDM2 alterations were less frequent in iCCA. KRAS was most frequent in eCCA, and ERBB2 was more frequent in GBC than in iCCA. Pathway-level trends matched the gene-level ones. The authors also noted age-related differences in FGFR2- and MDM2-altered tumors (PMID:42360806).
  • KRAS alleles: G13D was enriched in GBC and showed the highest rate of mutant-allele selection. G12V occurred only in iCCA and eCCA. Most “other” KRAS variants (A146T/V, L19F, V14I) were subclonal (PMID:42360806).
  • Clonality: most OncoKB driver and passenger mutations were clonal, with passengers slightly more often subclonal. ERBB2 mutations in GBC were subclonal in 41.2% of tumors, while ERBB2 amplifications were mostly clonal in all subtypes. GBC samples with subclonal ERBB2 mutations were enriched for ERBB3 and NF1 alterations and for epigenetic, NRF2 and PI3K pathway alterations (PMID:42360806).

Actionability

  • 59% of patients had at least one OncoKB alteration. 32.2% had a level 1/2 alteration: intrahepatic 40%, extrahepatic 15%, gallbladder 22%. In Table 1, the highest level was I in 348 (27.8%), II in 57 (4.5%), IIIA in 14 (1.1%) and IIIB in 219 (17.5%) (PMID:42360806).
  • The share of BTC with OncoKB level 1/2 alterations rose clearly between the 2017 and 2023 OncoKB releases. Most tumors with an actionable alteration had only one, usually a missense mutation (PMID:42360806).
  • Emerging targets: KRAS G12D (5.4%) and G12C (1.1%); MTAP deletion (12.8% of the n = 336 tumors profiled for it); MDM2 amplification (6.5%); MET amplification (1.5%) (PMID:42360806).

  • Matched therapy was given mostly in second line (n = 100; reported as 62.5%). Uptake rose from 2014 to 2021, tracking new FDA approvals and guideline endorsements (PMID:42360806).

  • Main reasons for not receiving matched therapy (n = 237):

    • No FDA-approved agent available within 6 months of death: 83 (35.0%).
    • Died before becoming eligible: 38 (16.0%).
    • Still responding to first-line therapy when the data were analyzed: 29 (12.2%).
    • Age, sex, anatomic site and oncogenic alterations did not differ between matched and unmatched patients (PMID:42360806).

Outcomes

  • PFS: in second line, matched targeted therapy (n = 99) beat 5-FU-based chemotherapy (n = 25): median 6.6 months (95% CI 4.2–8.6) vs 3.3 months (95% CI 2.1–8.1); P = .005. Clinical characteristics were balanced between the groups (PMID:42360806).

  • OS: matched vs unmatched showed no significant difference: median 17.0 months (95% CI 12.0–27.0) vs 11.0 months (8.5–14.0); HR 0.8; P = .16. Median follow-up was 13.8 vs 9.0 months (PMID:42360806).

    • Excluding immune checkpoint inhibitor–treated MSI/TMB-H patients: 16.0 vs 11.0 months; P = .28.
    • Restricting to OncoKB level 1/2 alterations: 17.0 vs 13.0 months; P = .34.
    • Age, sex, anatomic site, FGA and sample type were not associated with survival in univariate analysis (PMID:42360806).

  • BRAF was the only molecular subgroup in which targeted therapy was associated with longer OS. No clinical or genomic feature stratified PFS in any subgroup (Table S10) (PMID:42360806).

De novo and acquired resistance (paired samples)

  • 23 patients treated with matched therapy had sequential samples: 21 MSS (6 IDH1/IDH2, 8 ERBB2, 5 FGFR2 fusion, 2 NTRK fusion, 1 BRAF V600E) and 2 MSI (PMID:42360806).
  • Mutations were most concordant between paired samples in BRAF inhibitor–treated patients. Every FGFR2-, IDH1- and NTRK-driven tumor still carried its oncogenic driver at resistance (PMID:42360806).
  • ERBB2 group: at progression, 2 patients had lost their driver ERBB2 mutation (S310F, R678Q) and 3 had lost ERBB2 amplification; review of the post-treatment BAMs confirmed these losses. The Discussion puts ERBB2 alteration loss at 4 of 8 cases progressing on first-line HER2-targeted therapy. New alterations at progression included:
    • Tumors that lost ERBB2: MET and MYC amplification.
    • Tumors that kept ERBB2 (n = 4): KRAS and MYC amplifications (PMID:42360806).
  • Case 1 (GBC, age 69, de novo metastatic to liver and peritoneum):
    • Baseline: HER2 IHC 3+ with ERBB2 amplification (19.3-fold).
    • Progressed on FOLFOX and then gemcitabine/cisplatin.
    • Partial response to the HER2 bispecific antibody zanidatamab: 100% tumor shrinkage by RECIST 1.1, CA19-9 fell from 11,016 to 19, and treatment lasted 24 months.
    • A single escape lesion in the liver showed no uptake on HER2 PET. On biopsy it was HER2 IHC 2+ with no ERBB2 amplification, and had new MYC (23.2-fold) and MET (8.2-fold) amplifications.
    • Off-label crizotinib then shrank the tumor (PMID:42360806).
  • Case 2 (metastatic GBC, age 41):
    • Baseline: HER2 IHC 3+, ERBB2 amplification (17.3-fold) and ERBB2 R678Q.
    • Partial response to zanidatamab (84% RECIST reduction) lasting 14 months.
    • At progression, HER2 PET was avid (SUV 14.5). Biopsy confirmed HER2 IHC 3+ and retained ERBB2 amplification (10-fold).
    • Trastuzumab deruxtecan then gave sustained tumor shrinkage, ongoing at 16+ months (PMID:42360806).
  • Other drivers:
    • BRAF V600E: serial tumor and liquid biopsies showed oncogenic RAS and MEK SNVs emerging during treatment and at progression.
    • FGFR2 fusions: a second-site FGFR2 N549K mutation arose after FGFR2 inhibitor therapy.
    • NTRK rearrangement: MET amplification appeared at progression on a TRK inhibitor.
    • IDH1-mutant iCCA: no IDH isoform switching was seen in other patients treated with ivosidenib, although the group had previously reported one such case (PMID:42360806).

Genes & alterations

  • IDH1 / IDH2: IDH1 alterations were enriched in iCCA, and IDH1/2 were altered in 16% of MSS tumors. 73/150 IDH1-mutant patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 4.2 months. In IDH1-mutant tumors, co-occurring TP53 and RAS-RAF pathway alterations were associated with worse OS. The driver was always retained at resistance, and no isoform switching was seen with ivosidenib. IDH2 counted as level 3B actionable (PMID:42360806).
  • FGFR2: fusions were enriched in iCCA. 37/72 fusion-positive patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 7.4 months. TP53 pathway co-alterations were associated with worse OS. The fusion was retained at resistance, and a second-site N549K mutation arose after FGFR2 inhibition (PMID:42360806).
  • ERBB2: alterations were more frequent in GBC. ERBB2 mutations in GBC were subclonal in 41.2% of tumors, while amplifications were mostly clonal. 22/54 amplified patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 8.2 months, and SMAD4 co-alterations were associated with worse OS. It was the only RTK driver that was lost at progression on HER2-targeted therapy, in 4 of 8 paired cases, including S310F and R678Q mutation losses (PMID:42360806).
  • BRAF: 25 BRAF-mutant patients: class I 16, class II 5, class III 4. It was the only subgroup where targeted therapy was associated with longer OS (37.0 vs 9.5 months; HR 0.17; P = .013). BRAF non-V600E counted as level 3B actionable. In one BRAF V600E patient, RAS and MEK SNVs emerged at progression (PMID:42360806).
  • NTRK1: 2/5 fusion-positive patients got matched therapy. NTRK-driven tumors kept the fusion at resistance, and one developed MET amplification on a TRK inhibitor (PMID:42360806).
  • KRAS: altered in 17% of tumors and most frequent in eCCA. G12D was found in 5.4% and G12C in 1.1%, both named as emerging targets. G13D was enriched in GBC with the highest mutant-allele selection, G12V occurred only in cholangiocarcinomas, and minor alleles were mostly subclonal. KRAS amplification appeared at progression in ERBB2-retaining tumors (PMID:42360806).
  • MTAP: deleted in 12.8% of the 336 tumors profiled for it. It was not assessable across the whole cohort because the panel changed over time (PMID:42360806).
  • MDM2: amplified in 6.5% (an emerging target) and less frequent in iCCA than in eCCA or GBC (PMID:42360806).
  • MET: amplified in 1.5% and a level 3B target (1/14 patients matched). MET amplification recurred at progression on HER2- and TRK-targeted therapy (PMID:42360806).
  • MYC: amplification appeared at progression on HER2-targeted therapy, both in tumors that lost ERBB2 and in tumors that kept it (PMID:42360806).
  • TP53: the most frequently altered gene in MSS tumors (36%) and less frequent in iCCA. TP53 or TP53-pathway co-alterations were linked to worse OS in IDH1-mutant and FGFR2-fusion subgroups (PMID:42360806).
  • SMAD4: less frequent in iCCA than in eCCA or GBC. In ERBB2-amplified tumors, SMAD4 alterations were associated with worse OS (PMID:42360806).
  • CDKN2A: altered in 21% of MSS tumors. The abstract lists CDKN2A among alterations associated with acquired resistance (PMID:42360806).
  • ARID1A (18% of MSS tumors), BAP1 and PBRM1 (both enriched in iCCA) (PMID:42360806).
  • ERBB3 / NF1: enriched in GBC samples carrying subclonal ERBB2 mutations (PMID:42360806).
  • BRCA1, BRCA2, FGFR1, FGFR3: counted as selected level 3B actionable genes. 2/4 BRCA1-mutant patients got matched therapy (PMID:42360806).

Clinical implications

  • Actionable (level 1/2) alterations were found in 15–40% of cases depending on anatomic subtype. The authors say this supports molecular profiling as standard of care for all BTC subsets, not just iCCA (PMID:42360806).
  • 22% of patients with a guideline-actionable alteration never got targeted therapy because of poor clinical status or death. 8% of the whole cohort had too little tumor purity for NGS. The authors argue for sequencing earlier in the disease course, re-biopsy, and/or cfDNA profiling (PMID:42360806).
  • Matched targeted therapy gave a modest second-line PFS advantage over fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy. The absolute OS gain was about 5 months but not statistically significant, which the authors say matches other retrospective series. They conclude that targeted monotherapy has “a modest benefit that is largely restricted to a small subset of patients” and call for prospective randomized and combination studies (PMID:42360806).
  • For HER2-driven BTC, retained ERBB2 amplification and HER2 overexpression after first-line HER2-targeted therapy may be rescued by a second HER2-targeted agent, as in the trastuzumab deruxtecan case. The authors call this hypothesis-generating. Recurrent MET amplification at progression suggests a role for MET targeting and for combining MET and HER2 inhibition; basket trials such as NCT06084481 are ongoing (PMID:42360806).
  • RAS alterations are common in BTC and emerged as an escape mechanism. The authors suggest testing RAS allele-specific and pan-RAS inhibitors in these settings (PMID:42360806).
  • Co-alteration contexts (TP53/RAS-RAF with IDH1, TP53 pathway with FGFR2, SMAD4 with ERBB2) are proposed as markers of inferior outcome within actionable subgroups (PMID:42360806).

Limitations & open questions

  • Limitations named by the authors: single-center design, missing data, lead-time and immortal-time bias in the outcome comparison (partly addressed by starting OS at second line), and genomic heterogeneity that targeted NGS cannot capture (PMID:42360806).
  • The assay changed over time (341 → 505 genes), so MTAP deletion was assessed in only 336 tumors. Its prognostic and resistance role still needs study (PMID:42360806).
  • The subgroup and paired-sample analyses are small: n = 4–40 for OS in the subgroups, and 23 patients with serial samples. Several confidence intervals reach NR. The BRAF OS result rests on 4 treated patients in the OS analysis (PMID:42360806).
  • Internal inconsistencies to note before reuse:
    • Denominators for matched uptake differ between the Results text (e.g., IDH1 73/150) and Table 2 (IDH1 PFS n = 72/165).
    • The reported 95% CI for IDH1-treated median OS in Table 2 (13.0 months; 18.0–21.2) does not contain the median.
    • The “OS adjusted in MV model” HRs in Table 2 do not say which covariate the HR belongs to, and none is significant.
    • ERBB2 loss is described both as 2 mutation losses plus 3 amplification losses and as 4 of 8 cases.
    • The second-line share of matched therapy is given as 100 patients (62.5%), which does not equal 100/176 (PMID:42360806).
  • The abstract’s “59% with at least one OncoKB alteration” does not match the Table 1 sum of levels I–IIIB (about 51%). The 59% may include level 4 or other annotations; the paper does not say (PMID:42360806).
  • Cross-paper synthesis:
    • Earlier MSK-IMPACT biliary cohorts, chol_msk_2018 (PMID:29848569) and ihch_msk_2021 (PMID:33765338), were drawn from the same institution and assay. The paper does not report how many patients overlap with them.
    • The adverse effect of TP53/RAS-RAF co-alterations in IDH1-mutant iCCA here fits the finding in PMID:33765338 that TP53, KRAS and CDKN2A alterations are independent adverse prognostic markers in iCCA. The two cohorts used different endpoints and populations.
  • Open question: does ERBB2 loss at progression reflect selection of pre-existing subclonal ERBB2-negative populations? The 41.2% subclonal ERBB2 mutation rate in GBC points that way, but the paper does not test it directly (PMID:42360806).
