**PMID:** 42360806 · **DOI:** 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-26-0428 · **Journal:** Clinical Cancer Research (2026)

## TL;DR

Cowzer, Harding and colleagues analyzed 1,254 patients with biliary tract cancer (BTC) whose tumors were sequenced with [MSK-IMPACT](../methods/msk-impact-panel.md) at Memorial Sloan Kettering (April 2014 to September 2022). The cohort comprised 767 intrahepatic cholangiocarcinomas ([IHCH](../cancer_types/IHCH.md)), 210 extrahepatic cholangiocarcinomas ([EHCH](../cancer_types/EHCH.md)) and 277 gallbladder cancers ([GBC](../cancer_types/GBC.md)). They mapped the genomic landscape and [OncoKB](../methods/oncokb.md) actionability of each anatomic subtype, compared outcomes with genomically matched targeted therapy against unmatched therapy, and used paired pre- and post-treatment samples to look for resistance mechanisms. 32.2% of patients had an OncoKB level 1/2 alteration (iCCA 40%, eCCA 15%, GBC 22%). Emerging targets included [KRAS](../genes/KRAS.md) alterations (17%), [MTAP](../genes/MTAP.md) deletions (12.8%), [MDM2](../genes/MDM2.md) amplification (6.5%) and [MET](../genes/MET.md) amplification (1.5%). In second line, matched targeted therapy was linked to longer progression-free survival than 5-FU-based chemotherapy (median 6.6 vs 3.3 months; P = .005). Overall survival was not significantly different (median 17.0 vs 11.0 months; HR 0.8; P = .16). In the paired samples, [ERBB2](../genes/ERBB2.md)-driven tumors often lost the ERBB2 alteration. Tumors driven by [IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md), [FGFR2](../genes/FGFR2.md), [BRAF](../genes/BRAF.md) and NTRK kept their driver. Acquired resistance involved RAS, MEK, MET, MYC and CDKN2A alterations. The data are released as the cBioPortal study [biliary_tract_msk_2026](../datasets/biliary_tract_msk_2026.md) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

## Cohort & data

- **Patients and samples:** 1,254 patients (1,285 samples, including paired pre-treatment and progression samples plus plasma ctDNA) enrolled on MSK's prospective clinical genomic profiling protocol (NCT01775072; MSK IRB 12-245) between April 2014 and September 2022. Samples with tumor purity below 10% or no somatic alterations were excluded (n = 111). For the landscape and outcome analyses, one sample per patient was used, preferring primary tumors and higher-quality samples ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Cancer types:** [biliary tract cancer](../cancer_types/BILIARY_TRACT.md), split into [intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (IHCH)](../cancer_types/IHCH.md) 767 (61.2%), [extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (EHCH)](../cancer_types/EHCH.md) 210 (16.8%) and [gallbladder cancer (GBC)](../cancer_types/GBC.md) 277 (22.1%). iCCA and eCCA both fall under [cholangiocarcinoma](../cancer_types/CHOL.md) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Demographics (Table 1):** median age 65 (range 21–89). 651 female (52%) and 603 male (48%); GBC was 68.6% female. Self-reported race: Caucasian 931 (74.2%), Asian 129 (10.2%), African American 79 (6.2%). By [ADMIXTURE](../methods/admixture.md) genetic ancestry, 60.1% were European. 879 primary (70%) vs 375 metastatic (30%) samples were sequenced ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Dataset:** [biliary_tract_msk_2026](../datasets/biliary_tract_msk_2026.md) on cBioPortal (summary-level data). Deidentified patient-level data are available on request ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Assay:** tumor and matched-normal [MSK-IMPACT](../methods/msk-impact-panel.md) hybridization-capture NGS on four panel versions: [IMPACT341](../methods/IMPACT341.md) (n = 55), [IMPACT410](../methods/IMPACT410.md) (n = 200), [IMPACT468](../methods/IMPACT468.md) (n = 663) and [IMPACT505](../methods/IMPACT505.md) (n = 336). Analyses were adjusted for differences in gene content between panel versions. Selected cases also had cfDNA profiling with [MSK-ACCESS](../methods/msk-access.md) (assay described in [PMID:34145282](../papers/34145282.md)) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Annotation:** oncogenicity and actionability came from [OncoKB](../methods/oncokb.md) ([OncoKB annotation](../methods/oncokb-annotation.md)). The March 2017 (v1.8) and July 2023 (v4.7) releases were compared to measure how actionability grew over time. The **potentially actionable cohort (n = 514)** included tumors with an OncoKB level 1, 2 or 3A alteration, or a selected level 3B alteration: [BRAF](../genes/BRAF.md) non-V600E, [BRCA1](../genes/BRCA1.md), [BRCA2](../genes/BRCA2.md), [FGFR1](../genes/FGFR1.md), [FGFR3](../genes/FGFR3.md), [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md) or [MET](../genes/MET.md) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Clinically eligible cohort (n = 413):** the actionable cohort minus 46 patients with missing survival data and 55 with incomplete follow-up. 176 (43%) received matched targeted therapy based on NGS; 237 (57%) did not (the "unmatched" group). To reduce lead-time bias, OS was measured from the start of second-line therapy. The treated group was limited to patients who received matched therapy in second line (n = 100) and the untreated group to patients who received two or more lines of systemic therapy (n = 113) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Genomic methods:**
  - TMB: nonsynonymous mutations per Mb.
  - Copy number and clonality: [FACETS](../methods/facets.md)/facets-suite for purity-corrected copy number and cancer cell fraction (CCF). A mutation was called subclonal when the upper 95% CI bound of its CCF was below 1 and P(CCF < 0.5) was above 0.95.
  - Pathways: TCGA curated pathway templates (see [PMID:29625050](../papers/29625050.md)).
  - MSI: [MSIsensor](../methods/msisensor.md) score ≥10 classified 16 tumors as MSI. [MiMSI](../methods/mimsi.md) ([PMID:39746944](../papers/39746944.md)) was run on all TMB ≥10 tumors (n = 72) and reclassified 10 more as MSI. 2 tumors were hypermutant (TMB ≥30) but MSS by both methods ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Statistics:** Mann-Whitney U and Fisher's exact tests; [Kaplan-Meier](../methods/kaplan-meier.md) with log-rank tests; [Cox proportional hazards](../methods/cox-proportional-hazards.md) for multivariable OS/PFS; Benjamini-Hochberg correction; R 4.3.2. PFS ran from treatment start to investigator-assessed progression or death ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

## Key findings

**Genomic landscape by anatomic subtype**

- 1,226/1,254 tumors (97.8%) were MSS and 26/1,254 (2.1%) MSI-H. MSI-H rates were similar across subtypes: iCCA 12/767 (1.5%), GBC 6/277 (2.2%), eCCA 8/210 (3.8%) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Median TMB was 3.3 mut/Mb overall (range 0–74.6) and 3.0 mut/Mb in MSS tumors (range 0–29). 48 MSS tumors were TMB-high (≥10 mut/Mb). Among MSS tumors, GBC had higher median TMB than iCCA (4.1 vs 2.6 mut/Mb; P = .002) and higher median fraction genome altered (0.495 vs 0.43; P = .014) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Whole-genome doubling was more common in eCCA (34.2%; P = .002) and GBC (32%; P = .001) than in iCCA (20.7%) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- The most frequently altered genes in MSS tumors were [TP53](../genes/TP53.md) (36%), [CDKN2A](../genes/CDKN2A.md) (21%), [ARID1A](../genes/ARID1A.md) (18%), [KRAS](../genes/KRAS.md) (17%) and IDH1/2 (16%) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- [IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md), [FGFR2](../genes/FGFR2.md), [BAP1](../genes/BAP1.md) and [PBRM1](../genes/PBRM1.md) alterations were enriched in iCCA compared with both eCCA and GBC. TP53, [SMAD4](../genes/SMAD4.md) and [MDM2](../genes/MDM2.md) alterations were less frequent in iCCA. KRAS was most frequent in eCCA, and [ERBB2](../genes/ERBB2.md) was more frequent in GBC than in iCCA. Pathway-level trends matched the gene-level ones. The authors also noted age-related differences in FGFR2- and MDM2-altered tumors ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **KRAS alleles:** G13D was enriched in GBC and showed the highest rate of mutant-allele selection. G12V occurred only in iCCA and eCCA. Most "other" KRAS variants (A146T/V, L19F, V14I) were subclonal ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Clonality:** most OncoKB driver and passenger mutations were clonal, with passengers slightly more often subclonal. ERBB2 mutations in GBC were subclonal in 41.2% of tumors, while ERBB2 amplifications were mostly clonal in all subtypes. GBC samples with subclonal ERBB2 mutations were enriched for [ERBB3](../genes/ERBB3.md) and [NF1](../genes/NF1.md) alterations and for epigenetic, NRF2 and PI3K pathway alterations ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

**Actionability**

- 59% of patients had at least one OncoKB alteration. 32.2% had a level 1/2 alteration: intrahepatic 40%, extrahepatic 15%, gallbladder 22%. In Table 1, the highest level was I in 348 (27.8%), II in 57 (4.5%), IIIA in 14 (1.1%) and IIIB in 219 (17.5%) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- The share of BTC with OncoKB level 1/2 alterations rose clearly between the 2017 and 2023 OncoKB releases. Most tumors with an actionable alteration had only one, usually a missense mutation ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Emerging targets:** KRAS G12D (5.4%) and G12C (1.1%); MTAP deletion (12.8% of the n = 336 tumors profiled for it); MDM2 amplification (6.5%); MET amplification (1.5%) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

- Matched therapy was given mostly in second line (n = 100; reported as 62.5%). Uptake rose from 2014 to 2021, tracking new FDA approvals and guideline endorsements ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Main reasons for not receiving matched therapy (n = 237):
  - No FDA-approved agent available within 6 months of death: 83 (35.0%).

**Outcomes**

- **PFS:** in second line, matched targeted therapy (n = 99) beat 5-FU-based chemotherapy (n = 25): median 6.6 months (95% CI 4.2–8.6) vs 3.3 months (95% CI 2.1–8.1); P = .005. Clinical characteristics were balanced between the groups ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **OS:** matched vs unmatched showed no significant difference: median 17.0 months (95% CI 12.0–27.0) vs 11.0 months (8.5–14.0); HR 0.8; P = .16. Median follow-up was 13.8 vs 9.0 months ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
  - Excluding immune checkpoint inhibitor–treated MSI/TMB-H patients: 16.0 vs 11.0 months; P = .28.
  - Restricting to OncoKB level 1/2 alterations: 17.0 vs 13.0 months; P = .34.
  - Age, sex, anatomic site, FGA and sample type were not associated with survival in univariate analysis ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Subgroup outcomes (Table 2).** PFS and OS are medians in months. OS compares treated vs untreated patients.

- BRAF was the only molecular subgroup in which targeted therapy was associated with longer OS. No clinical or genomic feature stratified PFS in any subgroup (Table S10) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

**De novo and acquired resistance (paired samples)**

- 23 patients treated with matched therapy had sequential samples: 21 MSS (6 IDH1/IDH2, 8 ERBB2, 5 FGFR2 fusion, 2 NTRK fusion, 1 BRAF V600E) and 2 MSI ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Mutations were most concordant between paired samples in BRAF inhibitor–treated patients. Every FGFR2-, IDH1- and NTRK-driven tumor still carried its oncogenic driver at resistance ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **ERBB2 group:** at progression, 2 patients had lost their driver ERBB2 mutation (S310F, R678Q) and 3 had lost ERBB2 amplification; review of the post-treatment BAMs confirmed these losses. The Discussion puts ERBB2 alteration loss at 4 of 8 cases progressing on first-line HER2-targeted therapy. New alterations at progression included:
  - Tumors that lost ERBB2: MET and MYC amplification.
  - Tumors that kept ERBB2 (n = 4): [KRAS](../genes/KRAS.md) and [MYC](../genes/MYC.md) amplifications ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Case 1** (GBC, age 69, de novo metastatic to liver and peritoneum):
  - Baseline: HER2 IHC 3+ with ERBB2 amplification (19.3-fold).
  - Progressed on [FOLFOX](../drugs/folfox.md) and then [gemcitabine](../drugs/gemcitabine.md)/[cisplatin](../drugs/cisplatin.md).
  - Partial response to the HER2 bispecific antibody [zanidatamab](../drugs/zanidatamab.md): 100% tumor shrinkage by RECIST 1.1, CA19-9 fell from 11,016 to 19, and treatment lasted 24 months.
  - A single escape lesion in the liver showed no uptake on HER2 PET. On biopsy it was HER2 IHC 2+ with no ERBB2 amplification, and had new MYC (23.2-fold) and MET (8.2-fold) amplifications.
  - Off-label [crizotinib](../drugs/crizotinib.md) then shrank the tumor ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Case 2** (metastatic GBC, age 41):
  - Baseline: HER2 IHC 3+, ERBB2 amplification (17.3-fold) and ERBB2 R678Q.
  - Partial response to zanidatamab (84% RECIST reduction) lasting 14 months.
  - At progression, HER2 PET was avid (SUV 14.5). Biopsy confirmed HER2 IHC 3+ and retained ERBB2 amplification (10-fold).
  - [Trastuzumab deruxtecan](../drugs/trastuzumab-deruxtecan.md) then gave sustained tumor shrinkage, ongoing at 16+ months ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Other drivers:**
  - BRAF V600E: serial tumor and liquid biopsies showed oncogenic RAS and MEK SNVs emerging during treatment and at progression.
  - FGFR2 fusions: a second-site FGFR2 N549K mutation arose after FGFR2 inhibitor therapy.
  - NTRK rearrangement: MET amplification appeared at progression on a TRK inhibitor.
  - IDH1-mutant iCCA: no IDH isoform switching was seen in other patients treated with [ivosidenib](../drugs/ivosidenib.md), although the group had previously reported one such case ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

## Genes & alterations

- **[IDH1](../genes/IDH1.md) / [IDH2](../genes/IDH2.md):** IDH1 alterations were enriched in iCCA, and IDH1/2 were altered in 16% of MSS tumors. 73/150 IDH1-mutant patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 4.2 months. In IDH1-mutant tumors, co-occurring TP53 and RAS-RAF pathway alterations were associated with worse OS. The driver was always retained at resistance, and no isoform switching was seen with ivosidenib. IDH2 counted as level 3B actionable ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[FGFR2](../genes/FGFR2.md):** fusions were enriched in iCCA. 37/72 fusion-positive patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 7.4 months. TP53 pathway co-alterations were associated with worse OS. The fusion was retained at resistance, and a second-site N549K mutation arose after FGFR2 inhibition ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[ERBB2](../genes/ERBB2.md):** alterations were more frequent in GBC. ERBB2 mutations in GBC were subclonal in 41.2% of tumors, while amplifications were mostly clonal. 22/54 amplified patients got matched therapy, with median PFS 8.2 months, and SMAD4 co-alterations were associated with worse OS. It was the only RTK driver that was lost at progression on HER2-targeted therapy, in 4 of 8 paired cases, including S310F and R678Q mutation losses ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[BRAF](../genes/BRAF.md):** 25 BRAF-mutant patients: class I 16, class II 5, class III 4. It was the only subgroup where targeted therapy was associated with longer OS (37.0 vs 9.5 months; HR 0.17; P = .013). BRAF non-V600E counted as level 3B actionable. In one BRAF V600E patient, RAS and MEK SNVs emerged at progression ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[NTRK1](../genes/NTRK1.md):** 2/5 fusion-positive patients got matched therapy. NTRK-driven tumors kept the fusion at resistance, and one developed MET amplification on a TRK inhibitor ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[KRAS](../genes/KRAS.md):** altered in 17% of tumors and most frequent in eCCA. G12D was found in 5.4% and G12C in 1.1%, both named as emerging targets. G13D was enriched in GBC with the highest mutant-allele selection, G12V occurred only in cholangiocarcinomas, and minor alleles were mostly subclonal. KRAS amplification appeared at progression in ERBB2-retaining tumors ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[MTAP](../genes/MTAP.md):** deleted in 12.8% of the 336 tumors profiled for it. It was not assessable across the whole cohort because the panel changed over time ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[MDM2](../genes/MDM2.md):** amplified in 6.5% (an emerging target) and less frequent in iCCA than in eCCA or GBC ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[MET](../genes/MET.md):** amplified in 1.5% and a level 3B target (1/14 patients matched). MET amplification recurred at progression on HER2- and TRK-targeted therapy ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[MYC](../genes/MYC.md):** amplification appeared at progression on HER2-targeted therapy, both in tumors that lost ERBB2 and in tumors that kept it ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[TP53](../genes/TP53.md):** the most frequently altered gene in MSS tumors (36%) and less frequent in iCCA. TP53 or TP53-pathway co-alterations were linked to worse OS in IDH1-mutant and FGFR2-fusion subgroups ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[SMAD4](../genes/SMAD4.md):** less frequent in iCCA than in eCCA or GBC. In ERBB2-amplified tumors, SMAD4 alterations were associated with worse OS ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[CDKN2A](../genes/CDKN2A.md):** altered in 21% of MSS tumors. The abstract lists CDKN2A among alterations associated with acquired resistance ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[ARID1A](../genes/ARID1A.md)** (18% of MSS tumors), **[BAP1](../genes/BAP1.md)** and **[PBRM1](../genes/PBRM1.md)** (both enriched in iCCA) ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[ERBB3](../genes/ERBB3.md) / [NF1](../genes/NF1.md):** enriched in GBC samples carrying subclonal ERBB2 mutations ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **[BRCA1](../genes/BRCA1.md), [BRCA2](../genes/BRCA2.md), [FGFR1](../genes/FGFR1.md), [FGFR3](../genes/FGFR3.md):** counted as selected level 3B actionable genes. 2/4 BRCA1-mutant patients got matched therapy ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

## Clinical implications

- Actionable (level 1/2) alterations were found in 15–40% of cases depending on anatomic subtype. The authors say this supports molecular profiling as standard of care for **all** BTC subsets, not just iCCA ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- 22% of patients with a guideline-actionable alteration never got targeted therapy because of poor clinical status or death. 8% of the whole cohort had too little tumor purity for NGS. The authors argue for sequencing earlier in the disease course, re-biopsy, and/or cfDNA profiling ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Matched targeted therapy gave a modest second-line PFS advantage over fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy. The absolute OS gain was about 5 months but not statistically significant, which the authors say matches other retrospective series. They conclude that targeted monotherapy has "a modest benefit that is largely restricted to a small subset of patients" and call for prospective randomized and combination studies ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- For HER2-driven BTC, retained ERBB2 amplification and HER2 overexpression after first-line HER2-targeted therapy may be rescued by a second HER2-targeted agent, as in the trastuzumab deruxtecan case. The authors call this hypothesis-generating. Recurrent MET amplification at progression suggests a role for MET targeting and for combining MET and HER2 inhibition; basket trials such as NCT06084481 are ongoing ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- RAS alterations are common in BTC and emerged as an escape mechanism. The authors suggest testing RAS allele-specific and pan-RAS inhibitors in these settings ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- Co-alteration contexts (TP53/RAS-RAF with IDH1, TP53 pathway with FGFR2, SMAD4 with ERBB2) are proposed as markers of inferior outcome within actionable subgroups ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).

## Limitations & open questions

- **Limitations named by the authors:** single-center design, missing data, lead-time and immortal-time bias in the outcome comparison (partly addressed by starting OS at second line), and genomic heterogeneity that targeted NGS cannot capture ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- The assay changed over time (341 → 505 genes), so MTAP deletion was assessed in only 336 tumors. Its prognostic and resistance role still needs study ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- The subgroup and paired-sample analyses are small: n = 4–40 for OS in the subgroups, and 23 patients with serial samples. Several confidence intervals reach NR. The BRAF OS result rests on 4 treated patients in the OS analysis ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Internal inconsistencies to note before reuse:**
  - Denominators for matched uptake differ between the Results text (e.g., IDH1 73/150) and Table 2 (IDH1 PFS n = 72/165).
  - The reported 95% CI for IDH1-treated median OS in Table 2 (13.0 months; 18.0–21.2) does not contain the median.
  - The "OS adjusted in MV model" HRs in Table 2 do not say which covariate the HR belongs to, and none is significant.
  - ERBB2 loss is described both as 2 mutation losses plus 3 amplification losses and as 4 of 8 cases.
  - The second-line share of matched therapy is given as 100 patients (62.5%), which does not equal 100/176 ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- The abstract's "59% with at least one OncoKB alteration" does not match the Table 1 sum of levels I–IIIB (about 51%). The 59% may include level 4 or other annotations; the paper does not say ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
- **Cross-paper synthesis:**
  - Earlier MSK-IMPACT biliary cohorts, [chol_msk_2018](../datasets/chol_msk_2018.md) ([PMID:29848569](../papers/29848569.md)) and [ihch_msk_2021](../datasets/ihch_msk_2021.md) ([PMID:33765338](../papers/33765338.md)), were drawn from the same institution and assay. The paper does not report how many patients overlap with them.
  - The adverse effect of TP53/RAS-RAF co-alterations in IDH1-mutant iCCA here fits the finding in [PMID:33765338](../papers/33765338.md) that TP53, KRAS and CDKN2A alterations are independent adverse prognostic markers in iCCA. The two cohorts used different endpoints and populations.
- **Open question:** does ERBB2 loss at progression reflect selection of pre-existing subclonal ERBB2-negative populations? The 41.2% subclonal ERBB2 mutation rate in GBC points that way, but the paper does not test it directly ([PMID:42360806](../papers/42360806.md)).
