Neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 immunotherapy promotes a survival benefit with intratumoral and systemic immune responses in recurrent glioblastoma

TL;DR

Cloughesy et al. report a randomized, open-label, multi-institution pilot trial run by the Ivy Foundation Early Phase Clinical Trials Consortium. It enrolled 35 patients with recurrent, surgically resectable glioblastoma (GB). Patients received pembrolizumab either neoadjuvantly (200 mg about 14 days before resection) and then adjuvantly every 3 weeks (n=16), or adjuvantly only after surgery (n=19). Adding the neoadjuvant dose extended overall survival (median 417 vs 228 days; HR 0.39, P=0.04) and progression-free survival (median 99.5 vs 72.5 days; HR 0.43, P=0.03). Tumors resected after neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 had more interferon-γ and T-cell gene expression, less cell-cycle gene expression, and more often showed focal PD-L1 (CD274) induction alongside CD8+ infiltrate. In the neoadjuvant arm, tumor and blood T-cell receptor clonal dynamics moved together. Peripheral CD4+ T cells in that arm showed lower PD-1 (PDCD1) and higher CTLA-4 (CTLA4), and an intermediate monocyte population declined. cBioPortal hosts the cohort as the iAtlas-harmonized study gbm_iatlas_prins_2019 PMID:30742122.

Cohort & data

  • Randomized population: 35 patients were randomized between October 2016 and September 2017 at seven institutions: Dana-Farber, Huntsman, MD Anderson, MGH, MSKCC, UCLA and UCSF. This is the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, with 16 patients in the neoadjuvant arm and 19 in the adjuvant-only arm. Three adjuvant-only patients withdrew consent before surgery. Two patients (one per arm) were replaced after surgery because histology did not clearly show glioblastoma. These five were excluded from tissue studies but kept in the ITT efficacy analysis. Table 1 describes 32 patients (16 vs 16) PMID:30742122.
  • Eligibility: age ≥18; Karnofsky performance status ≥70; prior first-line therapy including at least radiotherapy; first or second relapse; candidate for surgical debulking. Patients could not be on high-dose corticosteroids (>4 mg/day dexamethasone or equivalent for ≥3 consecutive days within 2 weeks of registration) and could not have had prior anti-angiogenic or anti-VEGF therapy PMID:30742122.
  • Treatment: the neoadjuvant arm received pembrolizumab 200 mg IV 14 ± 5 days before scheduled resection. After recovery from surgery, both arms received 200 mg IV every 3 weeks until progression or a toxicity requiring discontinuation PMID:30742122.
  • Baseline characteristics (Table 1, n=32):
    • Mean age was 55.4 (neoadjuvant) vs 59.3 (adjuvant-only).
    • IDH status: wild-type 12 (75%) vs 13 (81%), mutant 3 (19%) vs 2 (13%), unknown 1 per arm.
    • MGMT promoter status: methylated 6 (38%) vs 11 (69%), unmethylated 7 (44%) vs 4 (26%).
    • The authors report no statistically significant between-arm differences in age, sex, KPS, IDH, MGMT, tumor volume, steroid use or extent of resection PMID:30742122.
  • Follow-up: Kaplan-Meier estimated median follow-up was 476 days (15.6 months; IQR 414–522 days) at the July 2, 2018 cutoff PMID:30742122.
  • Tumor transcriptomics:
    • Direct mRNA quantification used the 770-gene NanoString nCounter PanCancer Immune Profiling panel (n=28 tumors).
    • Paired-end 2×150 bp RNA-seq was aligned to GRCh38 with HISAT2, counted with htseq-count and normalized to log2 CPM.
    • Single-sample pathway scoring used GSVA with MSigDB c2.cgp, c6, c7 and hallmark v6.0 gene sets plus CIBERSORT and single-cell immune signatures (neoadjuvant n=14, adjuvant n=15).
    • KEGG gene-set enrichment (GSEA collection, hypergeometric test) was also run.
    • The raw RNA-seq is deposited in GEO as GSE121810 PMID:30742122.
  • Immune profiling:
    • αβ TCRβ CDR3 immunosequencing (Adaptive immunoSEQ) was run on PBMCs at baseline, at surgery and about 6 weeks after surgery, and on all evaluable tumors. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density was calculated as T cells divided by nucleated cells.
    • Time-of-flight mass cytometry (CyTOF; 24-marker panel) was run on 84 PBMC samples from 28 patients, clustered with FlowSOM and ConsensusClusterPlus.
    • Multiplex fluorescent IHC (Opal) stained CD8 (CD8A), PD-1, CD45 (PTPRC), GFAP and PD-L1 (clone SP142) PMID:30742122.
  • Imaging: tumor volumes were measured by volumetric MRI segmentation on contrast-enhanced T1 subtraction maps. Progression was assessed by iRANO criteria PMID:30742122.
  • External comparators: TCGA glioblastoma RNA-seq (HTSeq counts downloaded from the GDC; n=166), linked here to gbm_tcga as the TCGA-GBM cohort, and pre-treatment recurrent GBM RNA-seq from GEO GSE79671 (n=20). Both were batch-corrected with limma removeBatchEffect PMID:30742122.
  • cBioPortal study: gbm_iatlas_prins_2019. cBioPortal’s metadata lists 30 samples on hg38 and describes whole-exome sequencing and neoantigen analysis of 30 recurrent GBMs with matched normals, reprocessed by CRI iAtlas. The WES and neoantigen analysis is not described in the paper text itself.

Key findings

  • Neoadjuvant pembrolizumab extended overall survival (ITT, n=35). HR 0.39 vs adjuvant-only (95% CI 0.17–0.94; P=0.04, log-rank). Median OS was 417 days (13.7 months) in the neoadjuvant arm vs 228 days (7.5 months) in the adjuvant-only arm; the Fig. 1a legend gives 228.5 days. There were 9 vs 12 deaths at cutoff PMID:30742122.
  • Progression-free survival also improved. Median PFS was 99.5 days (3.3 months) vs 72.5 days (2.4 months); HR 0.43 (95% CI 0.20–0.90; P=0.03, log-rank). The authors caution that pseudoprogression complicates PFS assessment (Fig. 1c) PMID:30742122.
  • The effect held in patients with histologically confirmed tumor (n=15 per arm). Median OS was 400 days (13.2 months) vs 192 days (6.3 months); HR 0.35 (95% CI 0.14–0.88; P=0.03) PMID:30742122.
  • Safety:
    • Grade 3–4 adverse events occurred in 10 (67%) neoadjuvant vs 7 (47%) adjuvant-only patients (P=0.46, Fisher). These counts include events judged unlikely to be drug-related.
    • No neoadjuvant patient had surgery delayed.
    • Two neoadjuvant patients stopped for toxicity (grade 3 pneumonitis; grade 4 ALT elevation).
    • The most common treatment-related toxicities were muscle weakness (50%), headache (47%) and hyperglycemia (37%) PMID:30742122.
  • Post-progression therapy: 31 of 32 patients had discontinued pembrolizumab by the cutoff, 27 for progressive disease. Of those 27, 24 went on to a bevacizumab-containing regimen PMID:30742122.
  • Interferon-γ transcriptional induction (NanoString). The 18-gene interferon-γ (IFNG)-related signature scored higher in neoadjuvant tumors (P=0.03, U=49, Mann-Whitney; n=28). KEGG enrichment highlighted cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, chemokine signaling and toll-like receptor signaling PMID:30742122.
  • Cell-cycle signature suppression (RNA-seq and GSVA).
    • Interferon and T-cell pathway induction appeared in 9 of 14 neoadjuvant vs 5 of 15 adjuvant-only tumors.
    • Positive cell-cycle/proliferation enrichment appeared in 3 of 14 vs 11 of 15 (two-sided P=0.01, Fisher; 95% CI 0.01–0.70).
    • Only one neoadjuvant tumor with interferon/T-cell enrichment also showed cell-cycle activation.
    • Classification thresholds were GSVA ≥0.2 for “T_generic” and “Ayers_et_al_IFN_genes”, and ≤0.2 for “FARMER_BREAST_CANCER_CLUSTER_2” PMID:30742122.
  • The trial’s overall cell-cycle rate was typical of recurrent GBM. The whole trial cohort (14/29, 48%) was similar to GSE79671 (11/20, 55%; P=0.8) and TCGA GBM (73/166; P=0.7). Neoadjuvant vs GSE79671 gave P=0.05 (one-sided Fisher) PMID:30742122.
  • Focal PD-L1 induction. CD8+ TIL density did not differ between arms but varied more in the neoadjuvant arm. A focal PD-L1 pattern with high CD8 infiltrate appeared in 7 neoadjuvant vs 3 adjuvant-only tumors. PD-L1 intensity in the adjuvant-only cases was qualitatively weaker PMID:30742122.
  • Coordinated tumor–blood T-cell response, neoadjuvant arm only.
    • Clonal expansion from baseline to surgery trended higher in the neoadjuvant arm (P=0.07, unpaired t test).
    • TIL content, TCR diversity and tumor–blood TCR overlap did not differ by arm or time point.
    • Within the neoadjuvant arm, the fraction of expanded clones after the post-surgery cycle correlated with tumor–blood TCR overlap at all three time points (rs=0.86, 0.84, 0.89; P=0.0002, 0.0003, 0.00004; n=25).
    • TCR overlap at surgery correlated with TIL fraction (rs=0.83; P=0.0005).
    • None of these correlations appeared in the adjuvant-only arm PMID:30742122.
  • Systemic phenotypic shifts (CyTOF), neoadjuvant arm only.
    • An intermediate monocyte cluster (CD11b+CD11c+CD14+CD16+HLA-DRhi) fell from 1.35 ± 0.31% at baseline to 0.56 ± 0.12% on treatment (Benjamini-Hochberg-corrected P=0.007; n=28).
    • CD4+ T-cell clusters showed lower PD-1 (BH P=0.025) and higher CTLA-4/CD152 (BH P=0.0015).
    • The adjuvant-only arm showed no significant changes after multiple-testing correction PMID:30742122.
  • Survival modeling (n=30 evaluable).
    • Elastic-net Cox variable selection picked four variables associated with increased OS: neoadjuvant treatment, IDH mutation status, lower baseline peripheral TCR clonality and higher TIL density.
    • In a Cox model that forced in age and sex, neoadjuvant treatment had HR 0.33 (P=0.045). Baseline TCR clonality had HR 1.48 per SD (P=0.12).
    • Pre- and post-surgical tumor volume, percent resection, gross total resection and dexamethasone dose were not selected PMID:30742122.
  • The cell-cycle GSVA score is a candidate survival biomarker. The FARMER_BREAST_CANCER_CLUSTER_2 score explained 57% of OS variance (R²=0.57). Adding it to the Cox model raised R² from 0.36 to 0.62. It was left out of the initial Cox model because it differed between arms (P=0.02, t-test) and would have confounded the treatment effect PMID:30742122.
  • Steroids: dexamethasone dose at registration did not correlate with the interferon, T-cell or cell-cycle signature scores PMID:30742122.

Genes & alterations

  • PDCD1 (PD-1): the target blocked by pembrolizumab. PD-1 expression on peripheral CD4+ T-cell clusters fell between baseline and post-surgery only in the neoadjuvant arm (BH P=0.025). PD-1 was also stained in the multiplex IF panel PMID:30742122.
  • CD274 (PD-L1): focal PD-L1 with high CD8 infiltrate appeared in 7 neoadjuvant vs 3 adjuvant-only tumors. The authors read this as interferon-γ-driven induction by infiltrating T cells. Two of the three adjuvant-only focal PD-L1 cases also had upregulated cell-cycle genes, and all three had OS below the cohort median PMID:30742122.
  • CTLA4 (CD152): higher on peripheral CD4+ T cells after neoadjuvant treatment (BH P=0.0015). The authors cite this as a rationale for combining PD-1 blockade with CTLA-4 blockade PMID:30742122.
  • IFNG: the interferon-γ-related expression signature was higher in neoadjuvant tumors (P=0.03). The authors propose that interferon-γ signaling from T cells drives the downregulation of tumor cell-cycle genes PMID:30742122.
  • CD8A (CD8): CD8+ TIL density by multiplex IF did not differ between arms but varied more in the neoadjuvant arm PMID:30742122.
  • CD4 / IL7R (CD127): in the neoadjuvant arm, peripheral CD4+ T cells gained CD127 and CD152 and lost PD-1. The authors interpret this as a shift toward activation and memory phenotypes PMID:30742122.
  • PTPRC (CD45) and GFAP: markers in the multiplex IF panel (leukocyte and glial markers) PMID:30742122.
  • IDH1 / IDH2: the paper reports only “IDH mutation status” and does not say which isoform. Of 32 patients, 5 were IDH-mutant (3 neoadjuvant, 2 adjuvant-only), 25 wild-type and 2 unknown. IDH status was balanced between arms and was one of the four variables the elastic net selected as associated with increased OS PMID:30742122.
  • MGMT: promoter methylation was 6/16 (38%) in the neoadjuvant arm vs 11/16 (69%) in the adjuvant-only arm, which the authors report as not statistically significant. MGMT was a candidate covariate but was not among the variables the elastic net selected PMID:30742122.
  • The paper text reports no somatic mutation or copy-number analyses. Mismatch repair deficiency comes up only as background: earlier isolated case reports of PD-1 blockade responses in GBM PMID:30742122.

Clinical implications

  • Timing of PD-1 blockade matters in resectable recurrent GBM. In this randomized comparison, one pembrolizumab dose before resection, followed by adjuvant dosing, gave longer OS and PFS than adjuvant-only dosing. The authors suggest this “may represent a more efficacious approach” and plan larger trials of neoadjuvant combination immunotherapy PMID:30742122.
  • Candidate biomarkers (hypothesis-generating):
    • The tumor cell-cycle GSVA score (R²=0.57 for OS).
    • Lower baseline peripheral TCR clonality, i.e., greater initial T-cell diversity (HR 1.48 per SD increase in clonality, P=0.12).
    • Higher TIL density PMID:30742122.
  • Combination rationale: CTLA-4 was upregulated on CD4+ T cells, and PD-1 blockade did not change TCR diversity. The authors therefore propose testing CTLA-4 blockade combinations in the neoadjuvant setting. They note that nivolumab ± ipilimumab has been tried in recurrent GBM without an obvious survival signal, but not neoadjuvantly PMID:30742122.
  • Corticosteroids: at the protocol-allowed baseline doses (≤4 mg/day dexamethasone), steroid dose did not correlate with immune signatures or OS. The authors contrast this with published findings in NSCLC patients on high-dose baseline corticosteroids PMID:30742122.
  • Response assessment: pseudoprogression makes radiographic PFS harder to interpret in immunotherapy-treated GBM. The trial used iRANO criteria PMID:30742122.

Limitations & open questions

  • Small pilot, not powered for efficacy. The primary objective was an increase in TIL density; 15 patients per arm gave 85% power to detect a 0.5 increase (one-sided α=0.05). The authors say efficacy interpretation “is limited by small sample size” PMID:30742122.
  • The stated primary tissue endpoint was not met. TIL content by immunoSEQ did not differ between arms (two-sided P=0.41, Wilcoxon, n=30). The survival and signature differences are secondary findings PMID:30742122.
  • Dose imbalance: the neoadjuvant arm received an extra pre-surgery dose, and the authors say this may confound the immune comparisons. Multi-center logistics also limited tissue for deep biological analysis PMID:30742122.
  • Readouts did not agree: expanded-clone counts, focal PD-L1 status and gene-signature scores did not correlate significantly with one another. The authors attribute this to small sample size and heterogeneous tumor sampling for multiplex IF PMID:30742122.
  • Post-progression bevacizumab (24 of 27 progressors) was near-universal and varied in timing. The authors call its role “unclear” PMID:30742122.
  • Baseline imbalance to keep in mind: MGMT methylation was numerically more frequent in the adjuvant-only arm (69% vs 38%). The authors report no statistically significant imbalance. The cell-cycle biomarker was itself imbalanced by arm (P=0.02), so its predictive value cannot be separated from the treatment effect in this cohort PMID:30742122.
  • Genomics gap: the paper does not analyze tumor mutational burden, neoantigens or specific driver mutations. The WES/neoantigen data in gbm_iatlas_prins_2019 come from the iAtlas reprocessing, so genomic correlates of the neoadjuvant benefit remain untested in this text.
  • Cross-paper context:
    • The authors cite a single-arm phase II trial of neoadjuvant nivolumab in newly diagnosed or relapsed glioblastoma (NCT02550249; Melero and colleagues, same journal issue) that showed similar intratumoral and systemic immune changes. That trial is not in this corpus PMID:30742122.
    • A separate anti-PD-1 glioblastoma cohort in the corpus, PMID:30742119 (Zhao et al., Nature Medicine 2019; cBioPortal gbm_columbia_2019), studies genomic correlates of response such as PTEN and MAPK-pathway alterations. It could be read alongside this trial’s immune-response data.
