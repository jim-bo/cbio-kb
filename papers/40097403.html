TL;DR

The international A5 (American-Australian-Asian-Adrenal-Alliance) consortium profiled 94 hereditary SDHB-mutant pheochromocytomas (PHC) and paragangliomas (PGNG) (together PCPG) from 79 patients with seven molecular methods: tumour/blood whole-genome sequencing, whole-transcriptome sequencing, small-RNA-seq, EPIC DNA methylation, the C-circle assay, and single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq in a subset. The cohort included paired primary/metastasis and pre/post-chemotherapy samples. Sympathetic (chromaffin) and parasympathetic (non-chromaffin, mostly head-and-neck) tumours had distinct expression and methylation profiles that matched their cell of origin and catecholamine phenotype. Genomes were quiet: therapy-naive tumours had a median of 0.32 mut/Mb. TERT promoter mutations or rearrangements and ATRX alterations were the main secondary drivers. They occurred in 21/34 (61%) metastatic cases and were associated with metastasis (p = 9.349e-08), higher mutation burden, telomere-maintenance and proliferation programmes, and poorer outcome. Rare cooperative drivers included somatic EPAS1 (HIF-2α) mutations. The authors identified two acquired-resistance routes to DNA-alkylating chemotherapy: MGMT overexpression, and MLH1-loss mismatch-repair deficiency with SBS11 hypermutation (PMID:40097403).

Cohort & data

  • Patients and tumours: 94 primary and/or metastatic PCPG from 79 patients with pathogenic germline SDHB variants. Patients were recruited at 11 sites: A5 consortium centres in Australia, the USA and Canada, plus Tufts Medical Center (USA), Waikato Hospital (New Zealand), the National Cancer Centre Singapore and Uppsala University (Sweden). Histopathology and clinical review confirmed the diagnosis in every case (PMID:40097403).

  • cBioPortal study: hnsc_a5consortium_2025 (94 samples). cBioPortal files this study under the hnsc cancer type even though the tumours are PCPG.

    • For 28 primaries from 26 patients who developed metastases, a paired metastasis was available for six patients.
    • Four patients had paired synchronous/metachronous primaries (E128, E136, E159, E229). Discordant somatic profiles showed these primaries were clonally independent.

  • Germline SDHB spectrum (by WGS):

    • Variant types: missense (n = 41), nonsense (n = 21), frameshift indels (n = 6), large deletions (n = 7) and donor splice-site variants (n = 4).

Key findings

Molecular subtype and cell of origin

  • UMAP clustering was run together with published data (WTS n = 315, small-RNA n = 416, methylation n = 413):
    • By WTS, all A5 SDHB-mutant tumours clustered with the C1A (SDHx) subtype.
    • By methylation, SDHB-mutant tumours separated from other PCPG genotypes (one exception, E229-P1) and showed genome-wide hypermethylation.
    • By small-RNA-seq, four A5 tumours from two patients fell into an outlier group. That group included C2Bi (MAX) PCPG, which often has chr14q LOH and silencing of the imprinted DLK1–MEG3 miRNA cluster (PMID:40097403).
  • Within A5 alone, parasympathetic (non-chromaffin) head-and-neck PG (HN-PG) separated clearly from sympathetic (chromaffin) PCPG. Two dopamine-secreting mediastinal PG (E148-P1, E155-P1) clustered with HN-PG, which suggests they are non-chromaffin tumours (PMID:40097403).
  • Differential expression between HN-PG and sympathetic PCPG found 4,615 genes and 36 miRNAs (adj. p < 0.05, logFC > 1), with concordant methylation differences. HN-PG and the suspected non-chromaffin mediastinal tumours had low expression of:
    • the chromaffin marker CARTPT
    • neural transcriptional regulators (TFAP2B, TOX3, GATA3, POU4F, NEUROG2, PAX2)
    • HOX genes (HOXA1-10, HOXB4, HOXB6-9, HOXC4-HOXC13) and the lncRNA HOTAIR
    (PMID:40097403).
  • HN-PG are mostly biochemically silent and had low TH and DBH expression. Low TH/DBH expression tracked with gene-promoter methylation. Tumours from patients with elevated plasma 3-methoxytyramine (a dopamine metabolite) had low DBH (PMID:40097403).
  • TPH1, which is needed for serotonin synthesis, was overexpressed in HN-PG/non-chromaffin tumours. CHAT mRNA was not overexpressed (PMID:40097403).
  • Expression of the norepinephrine transporter SLC6A2 (NET) varied. Promoter methylation matched low expression in some sympathetic PCPG (PMID:40097403).

Genome-wide mutational features

  • Therapy-naive PCPG (n = 84) had a low tumour mutation burden: median 0.32 mut/Mb, range 0.03–4.24 (PMID:40097403).
  • Mutational signatures were mostly clock-like: SBS1 and SBS5, DBS2/4/9, and ID5/8/9. Ten of 94 tumours were resected after cytotoxic treatment, and some had chemotherapy-related signatures such as SBS11 (PMID:40097403).
  • Copy number:
    • Recurrent losses of chr1p, 2q, 3, 8p, 11, 17p and 22 (GISTIC q < 0.05).
    • Chr1q gain in sympathetic PCPG, but frequent chr1q loss in parasympathetic tumours.
    • Chromothripsis in 6 tumours and genome doubling in 9.
    • Significant chr7 gains in the metastatic group (GISTIC q < 0.05).
    • Primaries showed greater clonal diversity than metastases (one-sided t-test p < 0.01).
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Somatic drivers:
    • Only 15 cancer genes had more than one predicted pathogenic mutation (Cancer Genome Interpreter).
    • TERT and ATRX were the most frequently mutated genes. ATRX was significant by dN/dS and pTERT by OncodriveFML.
    • Other recurrent genes: KMT2C (n = 2), BCOR (n = 2) and TP53 (3 metastatic tumours).
    (PMID:40097403).

TERT alterations

  • Hotspot pTERT mutations (chr5:1295113G>A, chr5:1295135G>A; GRCh38) were found in 14 metastatic cases (17.7%). Targeted deep sequencing ruled out subclonal pTERT mutations in the other cases. In three pTERT mutants, snATAC-seq showed an open-chromatin peak centred on the de novo ETS binding site, and all reads carried the variant (PMID:40097403).
  • In case E123, chromothripsis of chr5 created an AFF4–TERT fusion: AFF4 exon 1 is spliced to TERT exon 3, with a breakpoint 390 bp upstream of the TERT transcription start site (PMID:40097403).
  • TERT expression:
    • All TERT-altered tumours overexpressed TERT, and the AFF4–TERT fusion tumour had the highest level.
    • TERT copy-number gain alone did not raise expression.
    • TERT-altered tumours had promoter hypermethylation that included the THOR domain (cg11625005, cg10767223).
    • One TERT-wild-type metastasis (E171-M1) also had promoter hypermethylation and overexpression.
    (PMID:40097403).

ATRX alterations and ALT

  • Pathogenic ATRX variants included nonsense, splice-site and frameshift variants, plus one large deletion (chrX:77,754,782-77,850,226). One missense variant of unknown significance was p.H2260Y. ATRX expression was low in most altered cases, which the authors attribute to nonsense-mediated decay (PMID:40097403).
  • C-circles and telomere length:
    • C-circles were detected in all ATRX-altered tumours and in one TERT-altered primary (E143-P1).
    • The tumour:blood telomere ratio was higher in ATRX-altered tumours than in TERT-altered or wild-type tumours (two-sided t-test p < 0.00025).
    • Four ATRX/TERT-wild-type PCPG, including two HN-PG, had long telomeres without C-circles.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Other ALT features:
    • Telomere variant repeat usage differed between C-circle-positive (n = 12) and C-circle-negative (n = 76) tumours. After normalizing to total reads, TTAGGG, GTTGGG and GTAGGG stayed significant after FDR correction.
    • Only one intrachromosomal telomeric insertion was found, in ATRX-altered case E198-M1.
    • TERRA was higher in ATRX-altered tumours, but the difference was not significant.
    (PMID:40097403).

TERT/ATRX and metastatic progression

  • TERT/ATRX alterations were present in 21/34 (61%) metastatic cases and were significantly associated with metastasis (Pearson chi-squared p = 9.349e-08). The alterations were mutually exclusive. Only one non-metastatic tumour (E156-P1) had a pTERT mutation, and that case had short follow-up (PMID:40097403).
  • Late acquisition of pTERT:
    • In E146, the primary was TERT-wild-type but the paired metastasis carried pTERT.
    • In E143, the primary had a subclonal pTERT mutation (VAF 10.3%) that was clonal in two metastases (VAF 33% and 28%).
    • The E143 primary also had C-circles and long telomeres, but both metastases lacked these ALT features. So ALT and pTERT-mutant populations coexisted in the primary, and only the pTERT cells metastasized.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Metastasis without TERT/ATRX:
    • Three metastases from two patients (E158, E159) had no TERT/ATRX alteration, no evidence of a telomere-maintenance mechanism and no recurrent cancer-gene alterations.
    • Ten primaries from patients with metastatic disease lacked TERT/ATRX alterations, but their metastases were not sequenced.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Mutation burden, tumour size and survival:
    • TMB was significantly higher in TERT/ATRX-altered PCPG (one-sided t-test p < 0.001), while metastases from wild-type cases had low TMB.
    • Only patients with TERT-altered tumours had significantly larger primaries than patients without TERT/ATRX alterations (two-sided t-test p < 0.05).
    • Survival analysis linked TERT/ATRX alterations to poorer outcome (Supplementary Table 1 and Fig. 16; no effect size in the main text).
    (PMID:40097403).

Transcriptional programmes in TERT/ATRX-altered and metastatic PCPG

  • Compared with non-metastatic primaries, TERT-altered tumours had 1,152 differentially expressed genes and ATRX-altered tumours had 1,448. 273 genes were shared by both contrasts, and 180 were also shared with the all-metastatic versus non-metastatic contrast. HN-PG, low-purity (<50%) tumours and the subclonal-TERT case were excluded (PMID:40097403).
  • Metastatic tumours overexpressed cell-cycle and proliferation genes:
    • MKI67 was among the top genes, consistent with Ki67 IHC.
    • TOP2A, BUB1, FOXM1, AURKB and CDK1 correlated with MKI67 at r > 0.88.
    • The PRC2 gene EZH2 and the transcription factor OTX1 were also overexpressed.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • ATRX-altered tumours:
    • Differentially expressed genes were enriched for lncRNAs and pseudogenes (chi-squared p = 9.9e-14).
    • RIPK4 was overexpressed.
    • Downregulated genes included the G2/M regulators RPRM and DRG2, plus USE1, SULT4A1 and COX17.
    • Findings were confirmed with snRNA-seq and in independent TCGA/Flynn bulk RNA-seq.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Few genes were specific to TERT-altered tumours; among them were IRX3, SDK1 and TRIP13 (PMID:40097403).
  • miRNAs:
    • Versus non-metastatic primaries, TERT-altered tumours had lower miR-485-5p, miR-433-3p, miR-539-3p and miR-127-5p, and ATRX-altered tumours had lower miR-3065-3p/5p and miR-148a-3p.
    • Both groups had higher levels of the chr7q32.2 cluster miR-96-5p, miR-183-5p and miR-182-5p, whose shared targets include FOXO1, SNAI2 and RECK.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • snATAC motif enrichment versus normal chromaffin cells:
    • TERT-altered cells were enriched for AP-1 motifs (JUNB, JUND, FOSL1, FOSL2), SMARCC1 and WT1.
    • ATRX-mutant cells were enriched for WT1 and Sp1/Sp2 motifs, and most strongly for CTCF.
    • Normal chromaffin cells were enriched for NR3C1 and AR motifs.
    (PMID:40097403).

Treatment-related mutagenesis and resistance to alkylating chemotherapy

  • Patient E169 (para-aortic PGL with lung, liver and bone metastases): the lung metastasis before CVD (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dacarbazine) and the spinal metastasis after 23 CVD cycles were compared.
    • They shared 3,134 SNVs, including TERT and BCOR mutations.
    • The post-CVD tumour had twice as many SNVs (12,607), including new TP53, RPL5 and POLE mutations.
    • SBS8, SBS12, SBS16, SBS21 and ID1 increased, but SBS11 and SBS14 were absent.
    • MGMT was overexpressed in the post-treatment tumour (E169-M2). The authors propose this as an acquired dacarbazine-resistance mechanism, by analogy with temozolomide resistance in glioblastoma.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Patient E167 had a left adrenal PC, then an HN-PGL and hip metastasis 14 years later. The disease progressed after 131I-MIBG and was then treated with 22 CVD cycles.
    • The pre-CVD liver metastasis and the post-CVD intracranial metastasis shared 2,404 SNVs, including pTERT. MGMT was low in both.
    • The post-CVD metastasis gained more than one million additional SNVs with a strong SBS11 signature.
    • It carried a truncating MLH1 splice-site mutation (chr3:g.37091976G>A) with LOH and low MLH1 expression.
    • Canonical MMR SBS signatures were absent, but ID2 was elevated and the MSI score was 11.7 microsatellite indels/Mb (threshold >4).
    • It also acquired NRAS p.G12A, with predicted MAPK pathway activation.
    (PMID:40097403).
  • Among 75 PCPG in AACR Project GENIE release 13, one hypermutated case (GENIE-DFCI-001077-11300) had a dominant C>T pattern and an MSH6 p.G162E variant predicted to be a driver (PMID:40097403).
  • cfDNA case: plasma was drawn from a patient with high-volume SDHB-associated PCPG after three cycles of temozolomide plus 177Lu-DOTA-octreotate.
    • ctDNA fraction was 45%, and the cfDNA was hypermutated with C>T/SBS11 changes, mostly at low VAF (~0.5%).
    • Truncal mutations had VAF > 10%: pTERT, MYCN p.P44L, NUTM1 p.P88L and MAP2K1 p.Q56P.
    • No MMR-gene mutation was detected, possibly because it was below the assay’s detection limit.
    (PMID:40097403).

Genes & alterations

  • SDHB: germline pathogenic variants in all 79 patients (41 missense, 21 nonsense, 6 frameshift, 7 large deletions, 4 donor splice-site). Two recurrent large deletions possibly arise by homologous recombination. Every tumour had somatic 1p36.13 LOH and was SDHB-IHC deficient. SDHB-mutant tumours cluster as C1A (SDHx) and are hypermethylated (PMID:40097403).
  • TERT:
    • Hotspot promoter mutations (chr5:1295113G>A and chr5:1295135G>A) in 14 metastatic cases (17.7%), plus an AFF4–TERT fusion from chr5 chromothripsis.
    • Promoter/THOR hypermethylation and overexpression in altered tumours.
    • pTERT can be acquired late or be subclonal in the primary.
    • Significant driver by OncodriveFML (PMID:40097403).
  • ATRX: truncating and splice variants plus one large deletion. ATRX was a significant driver by dN/dS. Altered tumours had C-circles (ALT), longer telomeres, lncRNA/pseudogene dysregulation and RPRM/DRG2 repression (PMID:40097403).
  • AFF4: 5′ partner in an AFF4 exon 1–TERT exon 3 fusion (case E123), which gave the highest TERT expression in the cohort (PMID:40097403).
  • EPAS1: somatic variants near the PHD2-targeted prolyl codon 531 in two tumours, p.Tyr532Cys (E230-P1) and p.Asp539Gly (E183-P1). The authors describe these as rare cooperative drivers in SDHB-mutant PCPG (PMID:40097403).
  • MGMT: overexpressed in a metastasis after 23 cycles of CVD, a proposed acquired alkylator-resistance mechanism (PMID:40097403).
  • MLH1: acquired truncating splice-site mutation with LOH and low expression in a post-CVD intracranial metastasis. That tumour had hypermutation, SBS11 and MSI (11.7 indels/Mb) (PMID:40097403).
  • MSH6: p.G162E (predicted driver) in a hypermutated GENIE PCPG (PMID:40097403).
  • TP53: somatic mutations in three metastatic tumours, including one acquired after CVD (PMID:40097403).
  • KMT2C, BCOR: each recurrently mutated (n = 2). The BCOR mutation was truncal in E169 (PMID:40097403).
  • MYCN: hotspot p.Pro44Leu in tumour E129-P1, and p.P44L as a truncal cfDNA variant in the temozolomide-treated patient (PMID:40097403).
  • NRAS: p.G12A acquired in the post-CVD intracranial metastasis (E167-M2), with predicted MAPK activation (PMID:40097403).
  • POLE, RPL5: mutations acquired after CVD in E169-M2. SBS14 (POLE deficiency) was absent (PMID:40097403).
  • MAP2K1 p.Q56P and NUTM1 p.P88L: truncal cfDNA variants (VAF > 10%) in the temozolomide-treated patient (PMID:40097403).
  • MKI67, TOP2A, FOXM1, EZH2: overexpressed in metastatic PCPG as part of a proliferation programme (PMID:40097403).
  • RPRM, DRG2: G2/M checkpoint genes repressed in ATRX-altered PCPG. RIPK4 was overexpressed in the same tumours (PMID:40097403).
  • IRX3, TRIP13: among the few genes differentially expressed specifically in TERT-altered tumours (PMID:40097403).
  • TH, DBH, SLC6A2: expression is silenced by promoter methylation in biochemically silent, dopaminergic or NET-low tumours (PMID:40097403).
  • TPH1, CARTPT, HOTAIR: cell-of-origin markers. TPH1 is high in non-chromaffin tumours, while CARTPT and HOX/HOTAIR are low in them (PMID:40097403).
  • CTCF, WT1: binding motifs enriched in the open chromatin of ATRX- or TERT-altered tumour cells (PMID:40097403).

Clinical implications

  • Metastasis and prognosis: TERT/ATRX alterations mark metastatic SDHB-mutant PCPG and poorer outcome. Because they are often acquired late, their value as predictive biomarkers in primaries may be limited. The authors suggest cfDNA detection as a possible alternative to tissue biopsy, pending validation (PMID:40097403).
  • ATRX-altered tumours: in other neuroectodermal cancers, ATRX-altered cells are reportedly more sensitive to DNA-damage-response inhibitors targeting ATM, WEE1, ATR and PARP. The authors propose testing these in PCPG, including as radiosensitizers with 177Lu-DOTA-octreotate or 131I-MIBG (PMID:40097403).
  • Alkylating chemotherapy (dacarbazine, temozolomide) has no validated response biomarkers. The authors suggest that monitoring for MMR-gene mutations, SBS11 or MGMT overexpression could flag emerging treatment-resistant cells. They also hypothesize, without testing, that MMR-deficient PCPG might respond to immune checkpoint inhibitors (with the caveat that temozolomide-induced hypermutation did not predict ICI response in glioblastoma) or to WRN helicase inhibitors (PMID:40097403).
  • HIF-2α inhibition: whether HIF-2α inhibitors such as belzutifan work in SDHB-mutant PCPG is unknown. The authors consider it plausible that cooperative EPAS1 mutations increase sensitivity, and recommend screening SDHB carriers for somatic EPAS1 mutations in future HIF-2α inhibitor trials (PMID:40097403).
  • Theranostics: low SLC6A2 (NET) expression due to promoter methylation is clinically relevant because NET is needed for uptake of MIBG theranostic agents (PMID:40097403).

Limitations & open questions

  • The study covers a rare tumour type. Evidence for each resistance mechanism comes from single cases: MGMT overexpression from one pre/post-CVD pair, MLH1 loss from one pair, and the cfDNA hypermutation from one patient. The authors state that future studies are needed to validate these findings and to find additional low-frequency biomarkers (PMID:40097403).
  • A subset of metastatic PCPG (for example E158, E159) has no TERT/ATRX alteration and no detectable telomere-maintenance mechanism. How, or whether, these tumours maintain telomeres, and what drives their metastasis, is unresolved. Ten primaries from metastatic patients lacked TERT/ATRX, but their metastases were not sequenced, so late acquisition cannot be excluded (PMID:40097403).
  • Long telomeres without a telomere-maintenance mechanism seem insufficient to drive metastasis. Whether RPRM and DRG2 are direct ATRX targets, and which non-canonical TERT functions matter in PCPG, need experimental models (PMID:40097403).
  • The tag-counting method used to quantify telomeric content cannot separate centromeric from telomeric reads. The TERRA difference was not significant (PMID:40097403).
  • The HN-PG subgroup is small (12 in the sympathetic versus parasympathetic contrast), and HN-PG were excluded from the TERT/ATRX contrasts because none carried these alterations (PMID:40097403).
  • The survival association with TERT/ATRX is reported only in the supplement, without an effect size in the main text.
  • Open question for cross-paper synthesis (not raised by the authors): chr7 gain is recurrent in metastatic tumours, and the chr7q32.2 miR-96/182/183 cluster is upregulated in TERT- and ATRX-altered tumours. Whether the two are linked was not tested.
  • Cross-study context: in TCGA PCPG (PMID:28162975), SDHB-germline tumours with somatic ATRX were among the few with two driver events, and head-and-neck PG were excluded. This A5 cohort adds HN-PG and paired metastases.
