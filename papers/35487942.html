TL;DR

As part of the ChangKang (“Healthy Bowel”) Project at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC), Zhao, Wang, Chen, Chen and colleagues ran ultradeep tumor–normal whole-exome sequencing (HapOnco WESplus; median tumor depth 219×) on 1,015 Chinese patients with colorectal cancer (COADREAD: 729 COAD, 286 READ). Median follow-up was 68.8 months. Only 7.09% of tumors were hypermutated (TMB ≥10 mut/Mb). A five-tool consensus called 46 high-confidence significantly mutated genes (HC-SMGs). Eight of them were absent from the reference driver catalogues: LYST, DAPK1, CR2, KIF16B, NPIPB15, SYTL2, ZNF91 and KIAA0586, together mutated in 14.9% of patients. The authors propose four genomic “SYSUCC subtypes” with distinct overall survival (OS): hypermutated (HM), chromosomal instability high-risk (CIN-HR, marked by 10q11.21 amplification), CIN low-risk (CIN-LR) and genome stable (GS). Four features were each independently associated with worse OS: OncoKB-oncogenic alterations in the cell-cycle pathway, the same in the TGF-beta pathway, an immunogenicity-reduced state with low neoantigen burden, and high tumor mitochondrial DNA copy number (“mScore”). The mScore result was validated by qPCR in an independent cohort. The dataset backs the cBioPortal study crc_sysucc_2022 (PMID:35487942).

Cohort & data

  • 1,015 patients with CRC. For each patient, tumor and matched nonneoplastic tissue (FFPE or frozen) were sequenced. All patients were enrolled in the ChangKang Project (launched 2017) and resected at SYSUCC, Guangzhou, China. Inclusion criteria: age 18–80; resection of the primary tumor at SYSUCC; enough tissue with ≥30% tumor cells (confirmed by two pathologists); no chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy or other anti-tumor treatment before sampling; no history of another malignancy. Ethics approval: SYSUCC B2019-031-01 (PMID:35487942).
  • Cancer types. COADREAD comprising COAD 729 (71.8%) and READ 286 (28.2%), per Table 1 and Fig. 1b. The main text instead states 72.41%/27.59% (PMID:35487942).
  • Demographics (Table 1).
    • Median age at diagnosis 59 (range 18–88); 404 female (39.8%) and 611 male.
    • Family history of CRC in 73 (7.2%); smoking in 240/1,011 (23.7%).
    • Right-sided (cecum to splenic flexure) 320 (31.5%); left-sided 695 (68.5%).
    • AJCC stage I 91 (9.0%), II 384 (37.8%), III 287 (28.3%), IV 252 (24.8%), unknown 1. The main text gives 8.88/38.44/28/24.59%.
    • Mucinous carcinoma 45 (4.4%) (PMID:35487942).
  • Follow-up. Median 68.8 months (95% CI 67.7–70.4); median OS not reached (PMID:35487942).
  • Versus TCGA. This cohort had more metastatic disease, more READ, more males and more left-sided primaries (all P<0.001). It also had younger onset, fewer mucinous tumors, and clinical data missing for only one patient (PMID:35487942). The prior CRC landscapes the authors cite that are also in this corpus are TCGA 2012 (PMID:22810696) and the MSK metastatic CRC study (PMID:29316426). The extracted text does not tie the “TCGA”, “MSKCC” and “DIFC” comparator cohorts to specific study IDs.
  • Dataset and data access. Dataset: crc_sysucc_2022. Raw reads are in GSA-Human (China National Center for Bioinformation) under accession HRA000873 (project PRJCA004622), with restricted access through a Data Access Committee. Processed data and code are on the ChangKang Project portal (changkang.hapyun.com). Clinical data are in Supplementary Data 2 (PMID:35487942).
  • Assay. Whole-exome sequencing with HapOnco WESplus (HaploX), an extended exome capture. Beyond the exome it covers:
    • germline and somatic noncoding hotspots and frequent fusions
    • three viral genomes (HBV, HPV, EBV)
    • the whole mitochondrial genome
    • immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) biomarkers (TMB, MSI, HLA, neoantigens)
    Sequencing was 150-bp paired-end on Illumina NovaSeq 6000, aligned to hg19. Median depth was 219× (43–661×) in tumor and 223× (100–665×) in normal tissue, higher than TCGA whole-exome data (PMID:35487942).
  • Variant calling. A somatic SNV or indel had to be called by ≥2 of three callers (Mutect2, TNscope, VarScan2) and pass three filters: VAF ≥8%, depth ≥8 and ≥2 supporting reads. Variants were annotated with VEP.
    • TMB counted nonsynonymous coding and splice-site variants per Mb, excluding common variants (MAF >0.05 in 1000 Genomes or ExAC).
    • Hypermutation was defined as TMB ≥10 mut/Mb; MSI status came from MSIsensor (PMID:35487942).
  • Copy number, signatures and subtyping.
    • Copy number: EXCAVATOR2; purity and ploidy: ABSOLUTE; recurrent SCNAs: GISTIC2.0.
    • Signatures: mutational signatures fit to COSMIC v2 with a LASSO-based approach.
    • Oncogenic versus VUS calls: OncoKB Annotator.
    • Subtyping: consensus NMF (PMID:35487942).
  • Immune features. HLA class I typing with POLYSOLVER on normal WES. Neoantigens predicted with NeoPredPipe. HLA loss of heterozygosity from LOHHLA. Immunoediting score following Rooney et al. (immunoediting quantification) (PMID:35487942).
  • Mitochondrial DNA. Somatic mtDNA variants were called after de novo assembly (SPAdes) of mitochondrial reads. mtDNA copy number came from ExomeDepth, corrected for purity and ploidy. It was validated by D-loop/B2M qPCR, which the paper calls qRT-PCR (PMID:35487942).

Key findings

Mutation burden and hypermutation - 277,090 nonsynonymous somatic variants in total: median 117.5 per tumor (range 1–22,760). This is significantly lower than TCGA but with larger variance. Median TMB was 1.74 mut/Mb (range 0–339.7) (PMID:35487942). - 7.09% (72/1,015) hypermutated (TMB ≥10), lower than in the TCGA and MSKCC datasets. - 63.8% (46/72) were MSI-H. - 37.5% (25/72) carried nonsynonymous POLE mutations. - 11 hypermutated tumors had neither MSI-H nor a POLE mutation (PMID:35487942). - Genes linked to hypermutation. Besides POLE and MSI, POLD1, APC, ERBB2, PIK3CA, SMAD4, BRAF and KRAS correlated with hypermutation. POLE, POLD1 and PIK3CA mutations were enriched in hypermutated tumors; SMAD4 alterations were enriched in nonhypermutated tumors. Hypermutated tumors had lower CNV burden, mostly because of fewer amplifications (PMID:35487942). - Clinical profile, hypermutated vs nonhypermutated (Table 1). - Mean age 49.6 vs 58.2 (P<0.001). - Male 72.2% vs 59.3% (P=0.04). - Right-sided 79.2% vs 27.9% (P<0.001); COAD 98.6% vs 69.8% (P<0.001). - Stage II 65.3% vs 35.7%, stage IV 8.3% vs 26.1% (P<0.001). - CRC family history 20.8% vs 6.2% (P<0.001) (PMID:35487942).

High-confidence SMGs and hotspots - Method. Five driver-discovery tools were run on nonhypermutated tumors: MutSig2CV, OncodriveCLUST, OncodriveFM, e-Driver and dNdScv, with significance at q<0.1. Genes called by more than one tool were kept, giving 46 HC-SMGs. MutSig2CV alone nominated 402 more genes that no other tool supported (PMID:35487942). - Known drivers ranked first. HC-SMGs were annotated against CGC, IntOGen, OncoKB, TCGA and Vogelstein et al. Known CRC drivers (TP53, APC, KRAS, FBXW7, CTNNB1) ranked at the top (PMID:35487942). - Eight novel HC-SMGs were not reported in any of these sources: LYST, DAPK1, CR2, KIF16B, NPIPB15, SYTL2, ZNF91 and KIAA0586. - Together they were mutated in 14.9% (152/1,015) of this cohort and 19.55% of TCGA. - None is on current gene panels catalogued in cBioPortal (Supplementary Data 10) (PMID:35487942). - HC-SMG burden. 83.6% (849/1,015) of patients had ≥1 HC-SMG mutation, with a mean of 4.49 HC-SMG mutations per patient. APC and TP53 had the highest frequency of mutation combined with LOH (PMID:35487942). - Comparison with Western cohorts. HC-SMG mutation frequencies were lower than in the mostly Western TCGA, MSKCC and “DIFC” cohorts, but the genes ranked in a similar order (PMID:35487942). - Hotspots. The Chang et al. hotspot method found 52 substitution hotspots in 14 genes, many not identified in TCGA. APC had 13 hotspots, 8 of them absent from the existing hotspot database (for example p.R876), and most at higher frequency than the known ones (PMID:35487942).

Primary tumor site (ascending / transverse / descending / rectum) - Sites differed in age, TMB, grade and MSI status (chi-square P<0.05). OS did not differ clearly, apart from slight differences in stage III (PMID:35487942). - 34 of 46 HC-SMGs had site-dependent mutation frequencies (P<0.05), mostly higher in ascending and transverse tumors. APC, TP53 and PIK3CA differed between ascending/transverse and descending/rectal tumors (PMID:35487942). - PTEN deletion became more frequent from ascending colon to rectum. SMAD4 deletion and ERBB4 amplification were enriched in descending and rectal tumors (P<0.05). APC hotspots also varied by site: p.R1450 in ascending, p.T1556 in transverse, R876 in descending and p.R216 in rectal tumors (PMID:35487942).

Copy number and mutational signatures - Recurrent SCNAs (GISTIC2.0). Gains at 1q, 7, 8q, 13q and 20q; losses at 1p, 4, 5q, 8p, 14q, 15q, 17p and 18p. Additional focal gain at 10q and focal loss at 20p (PMID:35487942). - Signatures. More than 13 COSMIC signatures were each present in >1% of samples: - Signature 1 (clock-like) in >84.2% - Signature 3 (homologous-recombination deficiency) 13.4% - Signature 6 (dMMR) 11.5% - Signature 10 (POLE) 5.9% - Signature 9 (AID activity) 5.7% - Signature 8 4.9% - Signature 16 2.6% (PMID:35487942)

Oncogenic pathways (OncoKB oncogenic vs VUS) - Much of the alteration in the NOTCH, Hippo and cell-cycle pathways was VUS. Fig. 4a labels give oncogenic/VUS frequencies by pathway:

(PMID:35487942) - The PI3K, cell-cycle and RTK-RAS pathways were significantly mutated (all adjusted P<0.05). Every clinical feature except nerve invasion and gender correlated with at least one altered pathway (PMID:35487942). - Oncogenic cell-cycle and TGF-beta alterations predict shorter OS (log-rank P<0.001 and P=0.004). In a multivariable Cox model adjusted for TMB, stage and grade: - cell cycle: HR 2.26 (1.53–3.34), P<0.001 - TGF-beta: HR 1.56 (1.15–2.11), P=0.004 - VUS-only carriers did not differ from wild type (cell cycle HR 0.95, P=0.736; TGF-beta HR 1.25, P=0.374) - higher TMB was protective (HR 0.96, 0.93–0.99) (PMID:35487942) - Within the TGF-beta pathway, SMAD2 and SMAD4 were the most altered genes, with co-occurring deletion in 18.2% of patients. Within the cell-cycle pathway, RB1, CCND1 and CCNE1 alterations were mutually exclusive. RB1, E2F3 and ACVR2A had high VUS fractions, and RB1 amplification was classified as VUS (PMID:35487942). - Patients with mutations in both the MYC and PI3K pathways had significantly worse OS than those with mutations in one or neither. A mutation in only one of these pathways was not prognostic (PMID:35487942).

SYSUCC genomic subtypes - Clustering. Hypermutated tumors were set aside first as HM (n=72). Four tumors lacked SCNV or signature data and were excluded. The remaining 939 were clustered by consensus NMF (K=3) on signature exposures plus SCNA lesions. Group sizes from the Fig. 5c numbers at risk: GS n=465, CIN-LR n=253, CIN-HR n=221, HM n=72 (PMID:35487942). - Survival. OS versus CIN-HR (median OS 98.9 months): - CIN-LR: HR 0.64 (0.44–0.91), P=0.014 - GS: HR 0.60 (0.43–0.82), P=0.001 - HM: HR 0.18 (0.07–0.45), P<0.001

Disease-free survival showed the same pattern (PMID:35487942). - 10q11.21 amplification. CIN-HR had more 10q11.21 amplification than CIN-LR, and this lesion was used to separate the two groups. - The amplification was more frequent in metastatic CRC and increased from stage II to stage IV. - The region contains RET and NOTCH4. - In TCGA the amplification was rarer, but still tended toward shorter OS and progression-free survival (PMID:35487942). - The fraction of stage IV tumors rose in the order HM < CIN-LR < GS < CIN-HR (PMID:35487942). - When the scheme was applied to TCGA-CRC, CIN-HR contained the largest fraction of CMS4 tumors. The authors link this to activated stroma (PMID:35487942).

Immunogenicity - Viral sequences. EBV in 6% (61/1,015), HBV in 2% (23/1,015), HPV in one case (PMID:35487942). - Neoantigens. Tumor neoantigen burden (TNB) was mostly between 10 and 1,000. The most frequent immunogenic peptides came from ZNF-family variants, and KRAS/NRAS peptides were among the top 10 (PMID:35487942). - Immunogenicity-reduced (IR) status meant having any of three features: - LOHHLA, in 17.4% of 415 assessable patients - a mutation in a neoantigen-presenting gene (NPG), in 6.7% of 1,015 - immunoediting (extremely low Z-scored immunoediting score, below about −1.64), in 7.4% (Fig. 6b label)

HM had the largest IR fraction, followed by CIN-HR (PMID:35487942). - Survival. High TNB was associated with longer OS. After adjusting for stage and grade, low TNB with IR had the worst OS. Versus that reference group: - TNB-low and nIR: HR 0.61 (0.38–0.97), P=0.037 - TNB-high and IR: HR 0.33 (0.11–0.96), P=0.041 - TNB-high and nIR: HR 0.33 (0.04–2.44), P=0.278 (numbers at risk total 394) (PMID:35487942)

Mitochondrial genome - Somatic mtDNA variants. 2,310 somatic mtDNA SSNVs were found; 66.6% (676/1,015) of patients carried at least one. The adjacent RNR1, RNR2 and D-loop regions were the most mutated noncoding regions, which the authors propose as a hotspot. ND5, CYB and ND4 were the most mutated protein-coding genes, each mutated in >10% of samples (PMID:35487942). - mScore (mtDNA copies normalized for purity) was lower in tumor than in adjacent normal tissue (P<0.001). Tumor and paired-normal values were positively correlated (P<0.001) (PMID:35487942). - High mScore predicts worse OS. The top 10% by mScore (best-cutoff) had worse OS independent of stage and grade: HR 1.84 (1.23–2.75), P=0.003. Fig. 7b numbers at risk were 695 low and 78 high (PMID:35487942). - qPCR validation. The qPCR mScore correlated with the WES mScore. In an independent cohort (Fig. 7c numbers at risk: 77 low, 14 high) it predicted OS after adjusting for stage and grade: multivariable HR 2.77 (1.34–5.71), P=0.006 (PMID:35487942). - Associations. mScore was not associated with stage, grade, nerve invasion or vessel invasion. - mScore-high tumors had more TGF-beta pathway alteration (P=0.024), more TP53 mutation and less DICER1 mutation. - mScore correlated positively with Signature 1 burden and negatively with Signature 6 (dMMR) burden (PMID:35487942).

Genes & alterations

Percentages in parentheses are the per-gene labels printed in Fig. 3c (the HC-SMG oncoplot). The extracted text does not state their denominator, so treat them as approximate cohort mutation frequencies (PMID:35487942).

Known drivers and hypermutation genes - TP53 (45%): top-ranked known driver; one of the two genes with the highest frequency of mutation plus LOH; site-dependent mutation pattern; more often mutated in mScore-high tumors (PMID:35487942). - APC (47%): correlated with hypermutation; the other top gene for mutation plus LOH. - 13 hotspots, 8 of them novel (for example p.R876). - Site-specific hotspots: p.R1450 ascending, p.T1556 transverse, R876 descending, p.R216 rectum (PMID:35487942). - KRAS (24%): correlated with hypermutation. KRAS/NRAS variant peptides were among the 10 most frequent predicted neoantigens (PMID:35487942). - NRAS (2%): HC-SMG; a neoantigen source together with KRAS (PMID:35487942). - FBXW7 (12%) and CTNNB1 (4%): among the top-ranked known drivers (PMID:35487942). - POLE: nonsynonymous mutations in 25/72 (37.5%) hypermutated tumors; enriched in the hypermutated group. Signature 10 (POLE) was present in 5.9% of patients (PMID:35487942). - POLD1: mutations enriched in hypermutated tumors (PMID:35487942). - PIK3CA (13%): enriched in hypermutated tumors; mutation frequency differs by tumor site (PMID:35487942). - SMAD4 (20%): enriched in nonhypermutated tumors; deletion enriched in descending and rectal tumors; co-deleted with SMAD2 (3%) in 18.2% of patients (PMID:35487942). - ERBB2 (7%) and BRAF (4%): correlated with hypermutation (PMID:35487942). - PTEN (7%): deletion frequency rises from ascending colon to rectum (PMID:35487942). - ERBB4: amplification enriched in descending and rectal tumors (PMID:35487942).

Pathway genes - RB1, E2F3, CCND1, CCNE1 (cell cycle): RB1, CCND1 and CCNE1 alterations are mutually exclusive. RB1 and E2F3 carry high VUS fractions, and RB1 amplification is classified as VUS. Oncogenic cell-cycle alteration had a multivariable OS HR of 2.26 (PMID:35487942). - ACVR2A (5%): HC-SMG in the TGF-beta pathway with a high VUS fraction. Oncogenic TGF-beta alteration had a multivariable OS HR of 1.56 (PMID:35487942). - RET and NOTCH4: located in the 10q11.21 amplicon that marks CIN-HR (PMID:35487942). - DICER1 (4%): HC-SMG, mutated less often in mScore-high tumors (PMID:35487942).

Novel HC-SMGs (together mutated in 14.9% of the cohort and 19.55% of TCGA) - LYST (5%) - DAPK1 (5%): one of only two of the eight previously implicated in cancer; its earlier association was with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma only. - CR2 (4%) - KIF16B (3%) - NPIPB15 (3%) - SYTL2 (3%): the other previously implicated in cancer. - ZNF91 (3%) - KIAA0586 (2%): encodes TALPID3 (PMID:35487942).

Remaining HC-SMGs - TCF7L2 9%, SOX9 7%, ZFP36L2 7%, ARID1A 7%, BMPR2 6%, RNF43 6% - BCL9L 5%, AMER1 5%, AXIN2 5%, GNAS 4%, BCL9 4%, CTNND1 4%, PIK3R1 4%, ERBB3 4% - PBRM1 3%, USP9X 3%, ACVR1B 3%, B2M 3%, MAP2K4 3% - PCBP1 2%, CD58 2%, TCF12 2%, ELF3 1%, PDE8A 1% (PMID:35487942)

Mitochondrial genes (reported as RNR1, RNR2, D-loop, ND5, CYB, ND4; HUGO MT-RNR1, MT-RNR2, MT-ND5, MT-CYB, MT-ND4, which are not in the cBioPortal gene list) - The RNR1/RNR2/D-loop region is the most frequently mutated noncoding stretch. - ND5, CYB and ND4 are each mutated in >10% of tumors (PMID:35487942).

Clinical implications

  • WES-based TMB reference for Asian CRC. The study provides WES-based TMB baselines for about 1,000 Asian CRCs, to help define TMB-high versus TMB-low for immunotherapy. The authors note that the FDA approval of pembrolizumab for TMB-high solid tumors rested on limited gene panels, and they call WES-derived TMB the gold standard (PMID:35487942).
  • Panel coverage. None of the eight novel HC-SMGs is on current clinical gene panels. The authors argue for WES, or for extending panels, in clinical genetic testing of CRC (PMID:35487942).
  • Genomic subtyping. The authors present genomic subtyping as simpler and more feasible than transcriptome-derived CMS. They propose 10q11.21 amplification as a biomarker that separates poor-prognosis CIN-HR from CIN-LR and predicts prognosis (PMID:35487942).
  • Pathway alterations as prognostic markers. Oncogenic cell-cycle and TGF-beta pathway alterations are independent adverse prognostic factors. VUS-only alterations in the same pathways are not prognostic, so OncoKB-style oncogenicity filtering matters for pathway-level biomarkers (PMID:35487942).
  • Immunogenicity. Reduced immunogenicity (LOHHLA, NPG mutation or immunoediting) combined with low TNB identifies the worst-prognosis group (PMID:35487942).
  • mScore. The authors propose tumor mtDNA copy number as a prognostic biomarker and qPCR as a clinically feasible way to measure it. They cite Sun et al.: in an animal model, higher mtDNA copy number promotes microsatellite-stable CRC progression through oxidative phosphorylation (PMID:35487942).

Limitations & open questions

  • Tool coverage. The authors note that SMG discovery used only five tools. Algorithms such as MutPanning and RF5 were not included, and they call for a larger integrative analysis (PMID:35487942).
  • Generalizability. This is a single-center Chinese cohort of treatment-naive, resected primary tumors from patients aged 18–80. The hypermutation rate (7.09%) and overall HC-SMG frequencies are lower than in Western cohorts. The median of 117.5 nonsynonymous variants per tumor is significantly lower than TCGA. The paper does not separate how much of this reflects population biology and how much reflects calling thresholds (≥2 of 3 callers, VAF ≥8%) (PMID:35487942).
  • Cross-paper frequency contrast. The Fig. 3c labels (APC 47%, TP53 45%, KRAS 24%) are well below the MSS metastatic CRC frequencies reported by MSK-IMPACT (APC 79%, TP53 78%, KRAS 44%; PMID:29316426). The two studies differ in population, stage mix and assay, and the denominator of the Fig. 3c labels is unclear. This is recorded, not resolved.
  • Internal inconsistencies. The main text and Table 1 / Fig. 1b disagree on the COAD fraction (72.41% vs 71.8%) and on stage percentages (PMID:35487942).
  • Possible circularity in CIN-HR/CIN-LR. The two CIN clusters were distinguished, and named for TCGA, partly by 10q11.21 amplification status. That same lesion is then offered as a biomarker for the split (PMID:35487942).
  • mScore robustness. The mScore cutoff (top 10%, “best-cutoff”) was chosen from the data. The qPCR validation cohort is small (91 patients at risk in Fig. 7c; 14 mScore-high), so the effect may be optimistic (PMID:35487942).
  • LOHHLA coverage. LOHHLA could be assessed in only 415/1,015 patients because some HLA alleles were missing from the LOHHLA reference (PMID:35487942).
  • Signature tool unclear. The Results cite sigLASSO (Li et al.) as the LASSO-based method. The Methods name an R package “LassoSig” but cite deconstructSigs and use its “exome2genome” normalization (PMID:35487942).
  • No functional data for novel genes. The extracted main text reports no functional validation of the eight novel HC-SMGs, and the Cox models shown do not adjust for treatment (PMID:35487942).
  • Comparator cohorts unidentified. The TCGA, MSKCC and “DIFC” comparator cohorts are not identified by study or version in the main text (PMID:35487942).
