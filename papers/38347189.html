TL;DR

Dopeso, Gazzo, Derakhshan and colleagues at Memorial Sloan Kettering (co-senior authors Fresia Pareja and Jorge S. Reis-Filho) asked what drives the lobular phenotype in breast invasive lobular carcinomas (ILC) that do not carry the hallmark bi-allelic CDH1 (E-cadherin) inactivation. They reanalyzed MSK-IMPACT tumor–normal sequencing of 364 primary ILCs and found 25 (6.9%) with no CDH1 inactivating genetic alteration. These 25 cases make up cBioPortal study ilc_msk_2023. Ten of the 16 evaluable cases (62.5%) had CDH1 promoter methylation. Nine of those ten also had 16q loss and complete E-cadherin loss, which points to epigenetic-plus-genetic bi-allelic inactivation. Three cases had inactivating AXIN2 alterations: one W78* truncating mutation and two structural rearrangements, a PLXDC1-AXIN2 fusion and a 17q inversion. Whole-genome sequencing of three cases confirmed the AXIN2 W78* mutation. It also found a new t(3;11) CTNND1–DENND6A fusion that removes the armadillo repeats of p120. The AXIN2-mutant case also carried bi-allelic BRCA2 inactivation and had an HRDetect score of 0.99. Knocking out AXIN2 with CRISPR-Cas9 in MCF7 cells produced lobular-like traits: anoikis resistance, more migration, and less E-cadherin protein with no change in CDH1 mRNA. The authors conclude that ILC is a convergent phenotype: several different mechanisms can disrupt the cell–cell adhesion complex (PMID:38347189).

Cohort & data

  • Screening cohort: 364 primary ILC cases that had clinical paired tumor/normal targeted sequencing with the FDA-authorized MSK-IMPACT assay (msk-impact-panel; the panel version is not stated). Their CDH1 status was reanalyzed (PMID:38347189).
  • Focus cohort: 25 primary ILCs with no CDH1 inactivating genetic alteration. The paper deposits the somatic mutations, copy-number alterations and structural variants from targeted sequencing in cBioPortal as ilc_msk_2023. The cBioPortal study metadata lists 25 samples on hg19 (PMID:38347189).
  • Histopathology: Six breast pathologists reviewed the cases using WHO criteria and graded them with the Nottingham system. ER and HER2 status came from medical charts. Immunohistochemistry was used for E-cadherin (clone 36, Ventana) and, in a subset, p120 (clone 98, Ventana) (PMID:38347189).
  • CDH1 promoter methylation: Tested in 16 of the 25 cases that had FFPE material. Two methods were used on bisulfite-converted DNA:
    • methylation-specific PCR, with primers from Herman et al.
    • droplet digital PCR covering two CpG islands. A methylation frequency above 2.25 for either probe counted as methylated, and samples with fewer than 300 droplets were not evaluable.
    • A case was called methylated if either method was positive (PMID:38347189).
  • Whole-genome sequencing: Three cases (CDW19, CDW20, CDW21) were sequenced with WGS at mean depth 63x (tumor) and 37x (normal), aligned to GRCh37/hg19 (PMID:38347189).
    • SNVs: BWA v0.7.15 for alignment, MuTect v1.0 for calling.
    • Indels: Strelka, VarScan2, Platypus and Scalpel.
    • Copy number and LOH: FACETS. Cancer cell fraction: ABSOLUTE.
    • Structural variants: Manta, SvABA and GRIDSS2 combined.
    • Mutational signatures (COSMIC v3.1): Signal and SigProfiler. HR deficiency: HRDetect.
  • Mutational signatures from targeted sequencing: Inferred with SigMA in the 15 of 25 cases with at least 5 SBSs (PMID:38347189).
  • Functional models: MCF7 was used because it is luminal A and ER-positive/HER2-negative, keeps E-cadherin, and expresses more AXIN2 than CAMA1 or T47D (PMID:38347189).
    • CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of AXIN2 used three sgRNAs in LentiCRISPR-v2, with non-target sgRNA controls. Edits were confirmed by CRISPR-sequencing and Sanger sequencing.
    • Transient siRNA silencing was done for AXIN2 and CDH1.
    • Readouts:
      • TaqMan qRT-PCR, normalized to GAPDH.
      • Growth and viability in low attachment.
      • Scratch wound-healing assay.
      • E-cadherin immunofluorescence in 3D Matrigel culture.
      • Flow cytometry for cell-surface E-cadherin.
  • Statistics: Alteration frequencies were compared with two-tailed Fisher’s exact tests and Benjamini–Hochberg correction. TMB and fraction of genome altered (FGA) were compared with Mann–Whitney U tests. Functional assays used Student’s t-test (PMID:38347189).

Key findings

  • CDH1 status across 364 ILCs:
    • 314 of 364 (86.3%) had bi-allelic CDH1 genetic alterations.
    • 25 of 364 (6.9%) had mono-allelic CDH1 genetic inactivation.
    • 25 of 364 (6.9%) had no CDH1 genetic alteration.
    • E-cadherin IHC was available for 245 cases: 224 (91.4%) had lost E-cadherin, 12 (4.9%) had aberrant staining, 8 (3.3%) decreased and 1 (0.4%) retained. Aberrant staining was about as common in each CDH1-status group (P=0.52, Fisher’s exact) (PMID:38347189).
  • Clinicopathology of the 25 CDH1 genetically wild-type ILCs:
    • Subtype: 14 classic (56%), 10 pleomorphic (40%) and 1 trabecular (4%).
    • Grade: 15 grade 2 (60%) and 10 grade 3 (40%).
    • Receptor status: 21 ER+/HER2− (84%), 3 ER+/HER2+ (12%) and 1 ER−/HER2− (4%).
    • Coexisting in situ disease: LCIS in 18 (72%) and DCIS in 4 (16%). All four cases with DCIS also had LCIS (PMID:38347189).
  • Most frequently altered genes in the 25 cases:
    • PIK3CA: 10 of 25 (40%). The paper calls these hotspot mutations: H1047R ×4, E542K ×3, E545K, G118D, M1043I, N345K and R88Q ×1 each. Two cases had two PIK3CA mutations each.
    • TP53: 5 of 25 (20%).
    • ERBB2: 4 of 25 (16%).
    • AXIN2, CCND1, PAK1, PTEN, RUNX1 and KMT2C: 3 of 25 (12%) each (PMID:38347189).
  • Mutational signatures (15 evaluable cases): Most cases were dominated by an APOBEC signature (7 of 15, which the paper reports as 53%) or a clock-like/aging signature (4 of 15, 27%). This matches earlier findings in CDH1-mutant ILC (PMID:38347189).
  • No genomic difference from CDH1 bi-allelic ILC apart from CDH1: Oncogenic and likely oncogenic alterations were compared against ILCs with bi-allelic CDH1 inactivation. Apart from CDH1, no gene differed significantly. TMB (P=0.07) and FGA (P=0.12, Mann–Whitney U) were also comparable (PMID:38347189).
  • CDH1 promoter methylation is common in CDH1 genetically wild-type ILC: 10 of 16 tested cases (62.5%) were methylated and 6 of 16 (37.5%) were not (PMID:38347189).
    • Nine of the ten methylated cases also had 16q loss and complete E-cadherin loss, which suggests both CDH1 alleles were inactivated.
    • The tenth methylated case (CDW19) had no 16q loss and showed decreased but still present E-cadherin.
    • Of the six unmethylated cases, three had complete E-cadherin loss. The others showed aberrant, decreased or retained staining (one each).
    • Six of the ten methylated cases were classic ILCs. Four of the six unmethylated cases were ILC variants: two pleomorphic (one with apocrine features), one trabecular and one classic/alveolar.
    • Methylated (n=10) and unmethylated (n=6) cases did not differ significantly in histologic subtype, somatic alteration frequency or TP53 mutation frequency. The authors caution that with so few cases a type II error cannot be ruled out.
  • Inactivating AXIN2 alterations in three cases on reanalysis of MSK-IMPACT data (PMID:38347189):
    • CDW21 is a trabecular ILC with reduced E-cadherin and an AXIN2 loss-of-function mutation (W78*).
    • CDW29 is a classic ILC with a PLXDC1–AXIN2 fusion. The fusion deletes AXIN2 exons 3–13, losing the DIX domain and the β-catenin- and GSK3β-interacting domains, and truncates the RGS domain.
    • CDW30 is a pleomorphic ILC with a focal 17q inversion that breaks AXIN2 in exon 5. This truncates AXIN2, losing the DIX and β-catenin-binding domains and cutting the GSK3β-interacting domain.
    • None of the three showed LOH of the wild-type AXIN2 allele. The authors point out that AXIN2 haploinsufficiency is well documented.
    • CDW29 and CDW30 had no material left, so their CDH1 promoter methylation was not assessed.
  • WGS finds a new CTNND1 (p120) fusion (case CDW20): CDW20 is a grade 2 classic ILC with focal alveolar growth (PMID:38347189).
    • It had complete E-cadherin loss and cytoplasmic p120 on IHC.
    • WGS found no mutations in cell–cell adhesion genes. Signatures were clock-like/aging SBS and ID14 indel.
    • A t(3;11) translocation joins CTNND1 intron 11 to DENND6A exon 2. This removes CTNND1 exons 12–20, which encode the armadillo repeat domains. The fusion was validated by RT-PCR.
    • There was no LOH of wild-type CTNND1, but the gene is known to be haploinsufficient.
  • WGS of the mono-allelic methylation case (CDW19): CDW19 is a grade 3 pleomorphic ILC with reduced (not absent) E-cadherin and membranous plus cytoplasmic p120 (PMID:38347189).
    • WGS found no mutations in cell adhesion genes. Signatures were aging/clock-like SBS and ID9 indel.
    • The authors’ most parsimonious explanation is that methylation silenced one CDH1 allele while the other stayed intact, giving an incomplete lobular phenotype.
  • WGS of case CDW21 shows an HR-deficient ILC (PMID:38347189):
    • WGS confirmed that the AXIN2 W78* mutation is clonal.
    • No other cell–cell adhesion gene was altered, and there was no LOH of wild-type AXIN2.
    • It also found a somatic BRCA2 Q2506Sfs*21 mutation with LOH.
    • HRD evidence: a dominant SBS HRD signature, a dominant ID6 (HRD) indel signature with enrichment of microhomology deletions, and an HRDetect score of 0.99.
  • AXIN2 knockout gives MCF7 cells lobular-like features (PMID:38347189):
    • Knockout efficiency: sgRNA2, which targets exon 2, mutated 99.5% of sequenced DNA, and 94.5% of those mutations were frameshifts. AXIN2 mRNA fell by about 70–80%.
    • Anoikis resistance: viability in adherent culture did not change. In low-attachment (suspension) culture, AXIN2-KO cells grew more (Fig. 5b, P<0.05). After 72 h in low attachment followed by reseeding in adherent conditions, they were more viable (Fig. 5c, P<0.001).
    • Migration: wound closure increased at 24 h and 48 h (Fig. 5d, P<0.001).
    • Transient CDH1 silencing in MCF7 produced the same effects.
  • AXIN2 loss lowers E-cadherin protein but not CDH1 mRNA (PMID:38347189):
    • AXIN2-KO 3D Matrigel structures had lower E-cadherin immunofluorescence (Fig. 5e, P<0.001), although colony number, size and morphology did not change.
    • Flow cytometry showed less cell-surface E-cadherin, while CDH1 mRNA was unchanged (Fig. 5a, n.s.).
    • CTNND1 mRNA and p120 protein did not change after AXIN2 knockout or siRNA silencing.
  • Discussion framing: About 80% of ILCs carry bi-allelic CDH1 genetic inactivation, compared with less than 1% of other invasive breast cancers. The paper cites TCGA (PMID:26451490) among its references for the 80% figure. The authors conclude that ILCs lacking CDH1 genetic or epigenetic alterations are driven by inactivation of other cell-adhesion genes (CTNND1 or AXIN2), which supports a convergent lobular phenotype (PMID:38347189).

Genes & alterations

  • CDH1: The hallmark ILC gene.
    • In the 364-case screen, 86.3% had bi-allelic, 6.9% mono-allelic and 6.9% no genetic CDH1 inactivation.
    • Among the genetically wild-type cases, 10 of 16 (62.5%) had promoter methylation. Nine of these also had 16q loss and complete E-cadherin loss, and one (CDW19) was methylated without 16q loss and kept some E-cadherin.
    • CDH1 knockdown in MCF7 caused anoikis resistance and more migration (PMID:38347189).
  • AXIN2: Inactivated in 3 of 25 CDH1 genetically wild-type ILCs.
    • Alterations: a W78* truncating mutation (clonal on WGS; CDW21), a PLXDC1–AXIN2 fusion deleting exons 3–13 (CDW29), and a 17q inversion truncating the gene in exon 5 (CDW30).
    • No wild-type LOH in any of the three. The authors invoke haploinsufficiency.
    • CRISPR knockout in MCF7 produced lobular-like phenotypes: anoikis resistance, more migration, and lower E-cadherin protein (PMID:38347189).
  • CTNND1 (p120): A new deleterious t(3;11) CTNND1–DENND6A fusion was found by WGS in CDW20.
    • The fusion joins intron 11 to DENND6A exon 2 and removes exons 12–20, the armadillo repeats.
    • Wild-type CTNND1 was retained (the gene is haploinsufficient).
    • The authors propose this as the most likely basis for the lobular phenotype in this case (PMID:38347189).
  • DENND6A: 3′ partner (exon 2) in the CTNND1–DENND6A fusion (PMID:38347189).
  • PLXDC1: 5′ partner in the PLXDC1–AXIN2 fusion (CDW29) (PMID:38347189).
  • BRCA2: Somatic Q2506Sfs*21 with LOH in CDW21. This was bi-allelic inactivation with HRD signatures and an HRDetect score of 0.99 (PMID:38347189).
  • PIK3CA: The most frequently altered gene, at 10 of 25 (40%), with hotspot mutations dominated by H1047R (4) and E542K (3) (PMID:38347189).
  • TP53: 5 of 25 (20%). Mutation frequency did not differ between CDH1-methylated and unmethylated cases (PMID:38347189).
  • ERBB2: Altered in 4 of 25 (16%). Three of 25 were ER+/HER2+ clinically (PMID:38347189).
  • CCND1, PAK1, PTEN, RUNX1, KMT2C: Each altered in 3 of 25 (12%) (PMID:38347189).
  • CTNNA1 (α-catenin): Earlier work proposed it as a possible driver of CDH1-intact lobular tumors. The authors describe that evidence as “scant”. WGS found no alterations in other cell–cell adhesion genes in the three sequenced cases (PMID:38347189).

Clinical implications

  • Diagnostic and molecular classification: A lobular phenotype can arise without CDH1 genetic alteration. Mechanisms include CDH1 promoter methylation combined with 16q loss, AXIN2 inactivation, and CTNND1 disruption. The CTNND1 fusion and the extra structural context came from WGS, which fits the authors’ hypothesis that some drivers are “structural rearrangements not captured by targeted sequencing” (PMID:38347189).
  • HRD in a subset of ILC: In one ILC without CDH1 genetic or epigenetic inactivation, WGS-based HRD analysis found bi-allelic BRCA2 inactivation and HR deficiency. The authors suggest HRD “might be operative in a subset of ILCs”, which fits a reported association between germline BRCA2 variants and ILC. The paper does not discuss therapy (PMID:38347189).
  • The paper makes no treatment-response or survival claims.

Limitations & open questions

  • Limitations the authors state:
    • Only a few cases lacking CDH1 genetic or epigenetic alterations had WGS (n=3).
    • Epigenetic alterations in cell adhesion genes other than CDH1 were not tested.
    • AXIN2 protein expression could not be assessed in AXIN2-altered tumors because no material was available.
    • Methylated and unmethylated groups were small (10 vs 6), so negative comparisons may be type II errors.
    • The authors call for a larger systematic cohort (PMID:38347189).
  • No material for CDW29/CDW30: CDH1 promoter methylation was not assessed in these two AXIN2-fusion/inversion cases, so epigenetic CDH1 silencing cannot be excluded for them (PMID:38347189).
  • No second hit: None of the AXIN2- or CTNND1-altered tumors showed LOH of the wild-type allele. The causal argument rests on haploinsufficiency reported in other contexts, not on tumor-level evidence (PMID:38347189).
  • Functional work used one cell line: All functional validation was in MCF7. CAMA1 and T47D were used only to compare AXIN2 expression (PMID:38347189).
  • Mono-allelic cases: The 25 ILCs with mono-allelic CDH1 genetic inactivation are counted in the screen but not analyzed further in the main text.
  • Inconsistent definitions: The abstract and the cBioPortal study description say the cohort “lack[s] CDH1 bi-allelic genetic alterations”. The Results define the 25 as lacking any CDH1 genetic alteration and count mono-allelic cases separately (PMID:38347189).
  • Arithmetic inconsistency: The APOBEC-dominant fraction is given as “7/15; 53%”, but 7/15 is 46.7% (PMID:38347189).
  • Cross-paper conflict on CDH1 promoter methylation: This paper found CDH1 promoter methylation in 10 of 16 (62.5%) CDH1 genetically wild-type ILCs by MSP and/or ddPCR, and says it is “prevalent” in that subset. The authors contrast this with earlier reports that CDH1 methylation is uncommon in ILC (PMID:38347189). The TCGA ILC study found no CDH1 promoter hypermethylation in any tumor on methylation arrays (PMID:26451490). The two studies differ in population (a pre-selected CDH1-wild-type subset vs all ILCs) and assay (locus-specific MSP/ddPCR vs arrays). Both positions are recorded here without resolving them.
