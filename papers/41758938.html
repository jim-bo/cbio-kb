Single-cell protein activity analysis reveals aberrant myogenesis and IGF2-PI3K pathway dependencies in MYOD1-mutant rhabdomyosarcoma

PMID: 41758938 · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aea6453 · Journal: Science Advances (2026)

TL;DR

Single-nucleus RNA-seq (177,051 malignant nuclei from six patient tumors) combined with ARACNe/metaVIPER protein-activity inference identified three conserved tumor cell states — progenitor, transition, and differentiated — in MYOD1L122R-mutant spindle cell/sclerosing rhabdomyosarcoma (SCSRMS), reflecting disruption of normal myogenic differentiation by the mutant transcription factor. A paracrine IGF2–IGF1R–PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling axis from MYOD1-high progenitor cells to receptor-expressing transition/differentiated cells emerged as the dominant druggable dependency, reproduced in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models, a 24-sample bulk RNA-seq validation cohort, and 20 MSK-IMPACT–sequenced tumors (recurrent, largely mutually exclusive IGF2 amplification and PIK3CA/PTEN/AKT pathway alterations). Ex vivo drug screens, 3D organoids, and an in vivo PDX trial showed that the dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor samotolisib (LY3023414), alone and combined with irinotecan/temozolomide, produced significant tumor control and, as a triple combination, objective tumor regression, nominating IGF1R–PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibition as a mutation-independent therapeutic strategy for this chemotherapy-resistant sarcoma PMID:41758938.

Cohort & data

  • Six chemotherapy-exposed, patient-derived, snap-frozen MYOD1L122R-mutant SCSRMS tumors profiled by single-nucleus RNA sequencing (10x Genomics Chromium); age range 7–76 years (median 35.5), five male/one female, tumor size 2.4–24 cm (three lower extremity, two pelvic, one mediastinal) PMID:41758938.
  • After InferCNV/SingleR filtering of nonmalignant immune and stromal nuclei, the integrated dataset comprised 177,051 high-quality malignant nuclei (92.2% of 189,250 total nuclei) — described as the largest single-cell transcriptomic dataset generated to date for this rare sarcoma subtype PMID:41758938.
  • Validation cohort: 24 archival FFPE MYOD1-mutant SCSRMS tumors profiled by bulk RNA-seq (Centre Léon Bérard, Lyon) PMID:41758938.
  • Genomic cohort: 20 unique patients with MYOD1-mutant SCSRMS profiled by MSK-IMPACT (341–505 gene matched tumor-normal DNA sequencing panel), corresponding to cBioPortal study soft_tissue_myod1_msk_2025; median disease-specific survival (DSS) 42.4 months and progression-free survival (PFS) 27.3 months, 5-year DSS 35% and PFS 29% PMID:41758938.
  • Two patient-derived xenograft models — MSKRMS-93202 (derived from patient RMS211.2) and MSKRMS-74711 (previously published) — profiled by snRNA-seq (10,677 tumor nuclei) and bulk RNA-seq, including three multifocal recurrent/metastatic MSKRMS-93202 samples (T1–T3) PMID:41758938.
  • External datasets reanalyzed with the same ARACNe/VIPER pipeline: a single additional MYOD1-mutant SCSRMS sample (GSE195709) and six FP-RMS/six MYOD1-wild-type FN-RMS samples from a published pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma single-cell atlas, plus normal human skeletal muscle scRNA-seq from the Tabula Sapiens atlas as the tumor-versus-normal reference PMID:41758938.
  • All patients received neoadjuvant chemotherapy (most commonly vincristine, dactinomycin, and cyclophosphamide, “VAC”) followed by delayed resection; snRNA-seq tissue was obtained at initial resection in four patients and at first relapse in two PMID:41758938.

Key findings

  • Unsupervised Louvain clustering of metaVIPER protein-activity profiles yielded 20 patient-specific subclusters (2–5 per tumor), consolidated by viperSimilarity, CytoTRACE differentiation scores, and myogenic marker activity into three conserved cell states present in nearly all tumors: progenitor (68,265 nuclei), transition (82,053 nuclei), and differentiated (26,292 nuclei) PMID:41758938.
  • The progenitor state was the least differentiated by CytoTRACE, enriched for a mesenchymal stem cell signature, and lacked canonical myogenic transcription factor activity; unlike normal myogenesis, MYOD1 activity in this tumor was relatively enriched in progenitor/transition cells and decreased with differentiation — the opposite trajectory seen in FP/FN-RMS, where MYOD1 activity is higher in differentiated states PMID:41758938.
  • The MYOD1L122R regulon (134 significant direct targets) and the wild-type MYOD1 regulon (209 targets) shared only 11 genes; the wild-type regulon was enriched for “Myogenesis” (adjusted P = 0.005) and “Apical Junction,” while the mutant regulon was enriched for “Mitotic Spindle” (adjusted P = 0.002), “Myc Targets V1,” and “Protein Secretion,” indicating that MYOD1L122R rewires its transcriptional program away from differentiation toward proliferative/biosynthetic output PMID:41758938.
  • Comparative viperSimilarity analysis against a published FP-RMS/FN-RMS single-cell atlas showed the differentiated SCSRMS state overlaps the FP/FN-RMS differentiated states, the transition state overlaps the FP/FN-RMS proliferative/progenitor states, and the SCSRMS progenitor state is transcriptionally distinct, aligning only with the FN-RMS proliferative state PMID:41758938.
  • IGF2 was among the most activated master regulators in the progenitor state and correlated with high CytoTRACE scores; progenitor cells expressed high IGF2 but lacked IGF1R/IGF2R, while transition/differentiated cells expressed the receptors but had reduced MYOD1 activity. CellChat ligand-receptor analysis confirmed progenitor cells as IGF2 “senders” and transition/differentiated cells as IGF1R/IGF2R “receivers”; IGF1 itself was undetected in any state PMID:41758938.
  • OncoTarget-prioritized oncogenic master regulators (tumor vs. normal skeletal muscle, Bonferroni-adjusted P ≤ 10⁻⁵) were consistently enriched for IGF1R/PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway components — IGF1R, PIK3CA, and GSK3B — particularly in transition and differentiated states; the progenitor state additionally carried unique targetable dependencies including PTK2, SYK, LYN, and ALK PMID:41758938.
  • Both PDX models recapitulated the patient-derived cell-state architecture (two clusters in MSKRMS-74711, three in MSKRMS-93202), with concordant IGF2/MYOD1 activity in progenitor-like clusters and PRKG1/CALD1/MYOG activity in differentiated-like clusters, and the same IGF1R-PI3K/AKT/mTOR oncogenic-MR enrichment in bulk PDX RNA-seq PMID:41758938.
  • Unsupervised clustering of the 24-sample bulk RNA-seq cohort recovered three transcriptionally distinct groups whose average protein-activity profiles recapitulated the single-cell progenitor and differentiated programs (bulk cluster 3 ≈ progenitor, bulk cluster 2 ≈ differentiated), again dominated by IGF1R/PI3K/AKT/mTOR oncogenic MRs PMID:41758938.
  • Genomic profiling of 20 patients by MSK-IMPACT found MYOD1 VAF > 50% in 19/20 cases (range 0.43–0.98, median 0.78), consistent with mutant allele enrichment via chromosome 11p aneuploidy (five patients with high-level 11p amplification) PMID:41758938.
  • The most recurrent co-occurring, largely mutually exclusive alterations were IGF2 amplification and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway genes — PIK3CA (20%), PTEN (10%), AKT/PIK3R3/PIK3C2G (5% each) — enriched in posttreatment samples; other recurrent (5–15%) alterations affected upstream pathway genes (NF1, NF2, NRAS, BCOR, FGFR4), MDM2/CDK4 amplification, and inactivating alterations in MGA, CDKN2A, CHEK2, SMARCB1, ARID1A, and NOTCH4; recurrent (20–45%) arm-level copy-number changes included gains of chr1q, 5, 11p, 19p, and 20 and losses of chr10, 13q, and 16q PMID:41758938.
  • Exogenous IGF2 stimulation of PDX-derived cell cultures induced ligand-dependent phosphorylation of AKT and ERK1/2, functionally confirming an active IGF2–IGF1R–PI3K/MAPK axis PMID:41758938.
  • Ex vivo dose-response screens of PDX-derived disaggregated cells showed submicromolar activity for AZD8055 (mTOR inhibitor), samotolisib/LY3023414 (dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor), and A-674563 (AKT1 inhibitor), and low-micromolar activity for GSK1838705A (IGF1R inhibitor), while cells were resistant to standard relapse chemotherapy (SN-38 IC₅₀ > 29 μM; temozolomide IC₅₀ > 100 μM) PMID:41758938.
  • Combination ex vivo screens pairing samotolisib with irinotecan and/or temozolomide showed additive/synergistic Bliss scores, most pronounced in MSKRMS-93202.T3 PMID:41758938.
  • A 3D patient-derived tumor organoid (PDXO) mini-ring drug screen (42 single-agent/combination regimens) showed broad chemotherapy resistance relative to other RMS/sarcoma PDXOs, but relative sensitivity to ceritinib (ALK), copanlisib (PI3K), linsitinib (IGF1R), linsitinib + palbociclib (IGF1R + CDK4/6), and sapanisertib (mTORC1/2); at the drug-class level, PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibitors, IGF1R inhibitors, and DNA-synthesis blockers fell at or below the predefined 25th-percentile sensitivity threshold PMID:41758938.
  • In vivo PDX treatment (MSKRMS-93202.T3, NSG mice, n = 8/arm) showed all treatment arms significantly improved tumor volume control versus vehicle (P = 0.0003); the triple combination (samotolisib + irinotecan + temozolomide) produced objective tumor regression, outperforming both monotherapy (P = 0.0005) and chemotherapy alone (P = 0.005); PFS was significantly improved in all arms versus vehicle (P = 7.7 × 10⁻⁵), though the absolute median survival benefit of the triple combination over chemotherapy alone was modest (50 vs. 46 days) PMID:41758938.

Genes & alterations

  • MYOD1 — defining L122R hotspot mutation (VAF > 50% in 19/20 genomically profiled patients); drives a rewired transcriptional program distinct from wild-type MYOD1, with activity paradoxically enriched in the least-differentiated progenitor state rather than in differentiated cells as in normal myogenesis PMID:41758938.
  • IGF2 — top activated master regulator of the progenitor cell state; recurrently amplified in a subset of the 20-patient genomic cohort; functions as the paracrine ligand driving progenitor-to-differentiated signaling, confirmed by CellChat and IGF2-stimulation immunoblot experiments PMID:41758938.
  • IGF1R — receptor expressed by transition/differentiated (not progenitor) cells; top oncogenic master regulator by OncoTarget across single-cell, bulk, and PDX datasets; pharmacologically targeted by linsitinib and GSK1838705A with measurable activity in ex vivo/organoid screens PMID:41758938.
  • IGF2R — nonsignaling decoy receptor most abundant in transition/differentiated cells, proposed to buffer IGF2 availability and modulate paracrine signal strength PMID:41758938.
  • PIK3CA — most frequent co-occurring pathogenic alteration (20% of 20 genomically profiled patients), mutually exclusive with other PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway alterations; also a top OncoTarget-nominated oncogenic MR PMID:41758938.
  • PTEN — recurrent alteration (10%) in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, mutually exclusive with PIK3CA/AKT/PIK3R3/PIK3C2G alterations PMID:41758938.
  • PIK3R3 and PIK3C2G — recurrent PI3K-pathway alterations (5% each) PMID:41758938.
  • NF1, NF2, NRAS, BCOR, FGFR4 — recurrent (5–15%) alterations in genes upstream of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling PMID:41758938.
  • MDM2 and CDK4 — recurrent co-amplification (10–15%) PMID:41758938.
  • MGA, CDKN2A, CHEK2, SMARCB1, ARID1A, NOTCH4 — recurrent (5–15%) inactivating mutations or copy-number deletions PMID:41758938.
  • MYOG (myogenin) — late-stage myogenic effector with increased protein activity marking the differentiated cell state; diffusely positive MYOD1/rare-to-negative myogenin was the immunohistochemical signature across all six tumors PMID:41758938.
  • GSK3B — PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway component among the top OncoTarget-nominated oncogenic master regulators PMID:41758938.
  • PTK2, SYK, LYN, ALK — progenitor-state-specific actionable master regulators implicated in immune signaling and stemness, nominated as orthogonal combination targets to address residual, potentially therapy-resistant progenitor populations PMID:41758938.

Clinical implications

  • The authors nominate IGF1R–PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway inhibition — validated in vivo with the dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitor samotolisib (LY3023414), particularly in combination with irinotecan/temozolomide — as a translational strategy for MYOD1L122R-mutant SCSRMS, a disease with historically dismal outcomes and chemotherapy resistance PMID:41758938.
  • This dependency was validated even in PDX tumors lacking PI3K/AKT pathway mutations, supporting a transcriptionally encoded, non-oncogene, mutation-independent vulnerability that may extend PI3K/AKT/mTOR-targeting relevance beyond the subset of patients with detectable pathway mutations PMID:41758938.
  • IGF2 amplification and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway alterations were enriched in posttreatment samples, suggesting possible selection under chemotherapy pressure and a potential mechanism of acquired resistance warranting further study PMID:41758938.
  • Progenitor-state-specific master regulators (PTK2, SYK, LYN, ALK) are proposed as targets for rational combination therapy to eliminate residual resistant tumor populations that may explain the observed tumor regrowth after completion of PI3K/mTOR-inhibitor/chemotherapy treatment in the PDX model PMID:41758938.

Limitations & open questions

  • The clinical cohort is small (6 snRNA-seq patients, 20 genomically profiled patients) and clinically heterogeneous — spanning different sampling timepoints, anatomic sites, and treatment regimens — precluding outcome-stratified analyses of cell-state composition or regulatory programs PMID:41758938.
  • The cohort is enriched for posttreatment specimens, limiting assessment of treatment-naïve transcriptional programs, cell-state evolution under therapeutic pressure, and the relationship between cell states and treatment response/survival PMID:41758938.
  • The paracrine IGF2–IGF1R cell-cell interaction model is inferred from ligand-receptor and protein-activity analyses but not directly validated by spatial methods; multiplex immunohistochemistry to confirm spatial coexistence of the three cell states was not performed, in part because many defining master regulators lack reliable antibodies PMID:41758938.
  • Analysis focused on the malignant compartment; tumor microenvironment interactions with immune and stromal cells, which the authors acknowledge critically affect tumor biology and therapy response, were not systematically characterized PMID:41758938.
  • The triple-combination in vivo benefit over chemotherapy alone, while statistically significant, was of modest absolute magnitude (50 vs. 46 days median survival), and tumors progressed after completion of treatment, indicating that targeting the paracrine axis alone is insufficient for durable control PMID:41758938.
  • Further preclinical work — expanded cohorts, rational combination regimens, and validation of the progenitor-state-specific orthogonal dependencies (PTK2, SYK, LYN, ALK) — is needed before clinical translation PMID:41758938.
