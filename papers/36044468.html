Bioinformatically-expanded next-generation sequencing analysis optimizes identification of therapeutically relevant MET copy number alterations in >50,000 tumors

PMID: 36044468 · DOI: 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-22-1321 · Journal: Clinical Cancer Research (2022)

TL;DR

This Memorial Sloan Kettering study checks whether MET copy number alterations can be called reliably from routine hybrid-capture NGS (MSK-IMPACT), using FISH as the reference. It then applies the refined calls to more than 50,000 sequenced solid tumors (PMID:36044468). In a 70-case FISH validation cohort, the standard read-depth fold-change caller agreed with FISH in only 81% of cases. Adding purity- and ploidy-aware allele-specific integer copy number from FACETS, plus a focality rule (amplified segment <25 Mb), raised agreement to 91% concordance, 97% sensitivity and 89% specificity. The authors recommend a minimum of 20% tumor content. Re-analysis of 408 MET-gained/amplified tumors from 50,748 patients found 358 true amplifications (copy number ≥6; about 0.7% overall) across diverse histologies. Copy number was inversely correlated with the size of the amplified segment. Amplification was highest and most focal when MET was the sole driver or an acquired EGFR-TKI resistance event. It was lower and broader when another mitogenic driver was already present. Among 45 patients treated with MET inhibitors, responses were seen at all amplification levels, and objective response was highest with concurrent MET exon 14 alterations (67%). The authors therefore argue against restricting MET-targeted trials to high-level amplification only.

Cohort & data

  • Setting: Retrospective review of MSK molecular pathology records from 2014–2021, under IRB and Privacy Board approval (PMID:36044468).
  • Landscape cohort: 66,285 tumor samples from 50,748 patients sequenced by MSK-IMPACT. Cases were first screened for MET copy number gains (CNGs) with fold-change (fc) ≥1.5 and p<0.05, a looser threshold than the clinical fc 2.0 so that low-purity samples were not missed. This identified 408 cases, which were re-analyzed with FACETS (PMID:36044468).
  • FISH validation cohort: 70 cases with enough material for MET FISH: 48 with NGS-detected MET CNGs at various levels and 22 without MET copy number alterations. They spanned 16 primary cancer types: lung carcinoma (NSCLC and SCLC) 59%, gastrointestinal carcinomas (gastric, colorectal, biliary, pancreatic, hepatocellular) 13%, renal cell carcinoma (RCC) 7%, and glioblastoma (GB) 7% (PMID:36044468).
  • Clinical (treatment) cohort: 45 patients from the landscape cohort who received one or more MET inhibitors between January 2014 and June 2021: NSCLC 40, renal cell carcinoma 3, gastric adenocarcinoma 1, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (IHCH) 1. Response was assessed by RECIST v1.1 in patients with baseline and follow-up imaging (n=38 evaluable). Endpoints were objective response rate (ORR; unconfirmed CR/PR allowed) and time to treatment discontinuation (TTD). Data lock was June 1, 2021 (PMID:36044468).
  • Dataset: The validation data are public in cBioPortal as mixed_impact_subset_2022. Data for the rest of the cohort are available only on request (PMID:36044468). cBioPortal study metadata list 69 samples on hg19 for this study, against the 70 validation cases described in the text.
  • Assay: MSK-IMPACT is a hybridization-capture panel covering all coding exons of 341–505 genes (IMPACT341 through IMPACT505). It also carries more than 1,000 intergenic/intronic SNP tiling probes spread across the genome (about 1.2 Mb targeted in total) and is run with a patient-matched blood normal. RNA-based fusion testing via Archer (archer-fusionplex) was done in treatment-naïve tumors without a mitogenic driver on MSK-IMPACT, in post-treatment tumors without a resistance mechanism, and in cases with kinase structural variants of unknown significance (PMID:36044468).
  • Copy number callers:
    • Read-depth: GC-normalized depth compared with a pool of FFPE normals. Calls are amplification at fc ≥2.0, CNG/borderline at fc ≥1.5 to <2, and deletion at fc ≤−2.0, each requiring p<0.05.
    • FACETS: allele-specific, purity/ploidy-adjusted integer copy number. Calls are gain at copy number 3–5 and amplification at copy number ≥6. FACETS segmentation also gives the length of the amplified 7q segment, which was used to judge focality (binary cutoff 25 Mb, one quarter of the 7q arm).
    Source: PMID:36044468.
  • FISH: ZytoVision MET (7q31.2, about 800 kb) probe with a CEP7 probe; at least 100 interphase tumor cells scored per case.
    • Amplification requires a MET:CEP7 ratio >2 and a mean of >5 MET signals per cell.
    • Cases with a ratio <2 were graded by mean MET signals per cell: high CNG >8, low CNG 4–8, negative <4.
    • In heterogeneous tumors, the area with the highest mean MET copy number was used.
    Source: PMID:36044468.

Key findings

Binary amplification calling against FISH (n=70) - Read-depth only (fc ≥2.0, p<0.05): 81% concordance, 81% sensitivity, 82% specificity. Errors were under-calls in low-tumor-content samples and over-calls with polysomy, arm-level copy number changes and fragmented genomes. Lowering the threshold did not help: fc ≥1.7 gave 83%/94%/74% and fc ≥1.5 gave 80%/94%/68% (concordance/sensitivity/specificity) (PMID:36044468). - Adding FACETS: 86% concordance, 100% sensitivity, 74% specificity. Many false positives were arm-level or whole-chromosome 7 gains (polysomy) rather than MET-specific amplification (PMID:36044468). - FACETS plus the <25 Mb focality rule: 91% concordance, 97% sensitivity, 89% specificity (PMID:36044468). - All 6 remaining discordant cases were de novo low-level amplifications in samples with high tumor content. - In 5 cases (NGS+/FISH−), low-level CNGs met FACETS criteria but sat in fragmented chromosome 7 with partial gains and losses. - In 1 case (NGS−/FISH+), the amplified segment (31 Mb) exceeded the focality cutoff.

FISH artefacts - MET FISH signals were highly heterogeneous across tumor regions, especially when amplification was acquired as TKI resistance. Counting therefore favored nuclei with many signals and relied on narrow regions (50–100 nuclei in one section). Signal clustering and overlap caused under- or overestimation (PMID:36044468).

Quantitative agreement - FISH MET signals per cell correlated with FACETS log2(total copy number/ploidy) at R²=0.66. Agreement was weaker when FACETS total copy number (R²=0.47) or the FISH MET:CEP7 ratio (R²=0.42) was used instead (PMID:36044468). - FACETS integer copy number calls had tight confidence intervals, for example 105 (95% CI 99–117) for P-0003424-T01-IM5 and 49 (95% CI 46–51) for P-0048436-T01-IM6 (PMID:36044468).

Limit of detection - A tumor at about 30% tumor content (FISH MET copy number 16.49; MET:CEP7 8.36) was serially diluted with matched normal DNA (100% down to 3.125%) (PMID:36044468). - FACETS called amplification undiluted (integer copy number 11) and at 50% dilution (about 15% tumor content; copy number 12). - Read-depth at fc ≥2.0 called only the undiluted sample. - Because FACETS purity estimates become unreliable below 20%, the authors recommend 20% tumor content as a conservative minimum. Samples below this with negative or borderline results should be reflexed to FISH.

Pan-cancer landscape (50,748 patients) - Of the 408 candidates, FACETS classified 50 (12%) as MET gain (copy number 3–5) and 358 (88%) as MET amplification (copy number ≥6). That is about 0.7% of the cohort, in line with AACR GENIE. Tumor purity did not differ between gains and amplifications: low tumor content 13% vs 20% (p=0.3, Fisher’s exact test); median purity 50% vs 40% (p=0.09, Mann-Whitney U test) (PMID:36044468). - MET amplification was most frequent in: - papillary renal cell carcinoma (PRCC), 8.4% - high-grade glioma, 5.3% - gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJ), 5.0%

It recurred in NSCLC at 1.9% and was enriched in pleomorphic and sarcomatoid NSCLC (LUPC/SARCL) at 8.5%. Figure 2b breaks the NSCLC data down by LUAD, LUSC, LUNE and NOS/poorly differentiated (PMID:36044468). - Copy number vs focality: Higher MET copy number went with smaller amplified segments (Spearman rho −0.5, 95% CI −0.5 to −0.4, p<0.0001). Gastric adenocarcinomas (STAD) had the highest copy number and the most focal amplifications (PMID:36044468). - Four clinicopathologic groups across all 358 MET-amplified tumors (PMID:36044468):

Group Definition n (%)
1 De novo isolated amplification 223 (62%)
2 Concurrent activating MET alteration (exon 14 alteration or MET fusion) 18 (5%)
3 De novo with a concurrent non-MET driver 53 (15%)
4 Acquired after targeted therapy for a non-MET driver 64 (18%)
  • MET copy number differed across groups (Kruskal-Wallis p=0.002). Groups 1 and 4 had higher copy number than group 3 (Dunn’s p=0.005 and p=0.002).
  • Amplicon size also differed (p=0.0006). Groups 1, 2 and 4 were more focal than group 3 (p=0.002, 0.03 and 0.002).
  • The authors infer that high-level, focal amplification may be needed for MET to act as a sole driver or to mediate resistance.
  • NSCLC (n=140): 84 (60%) were de novo and 56 (40%) were acquired after targeted therapy (group 4) (PMID:36044468).
    • De novo cases split into group 1, 51/84 (61%); group 2, 15/84 (18%) with MET exon 14 alterations plus 1/84 (1%) with a MET fusion; and group 3, 17/84 (20%). The group 3 co-drivers were EGFR 7, KRAS 6, ERBB2 2, ALK 1 and RET 1.
    • In the acquired group, co-drivers were EGFR 50/56 (89%), ALK 3/56 (5%), RET 2/56 (4%) and KRAS G12C 1/56 (2%).
  • Non-lung tumors (n=218): 210 (96%) were de novo and 8 (4%) were acquired after targeted therapy (PMID:36044468).
    • Among de novo cases, 172/210 (82%) were isolated amplifications and 2/210 (1%) had a MET fusion. The other 36/210 (17%) had a non-MET driver: ERBB2 amplification 10, KRAS mutation 9, BRAF mutation 7, FGFR2 fusion 2, NRAS mutation 2, BRAF fusion 2, EGFR mutation 1, FGFR3 mutation 1, RET fusion 1 and FGFR1 fusion 1.
    • In the acquired group, co-drivers were BRAF mutation 3/8, ERBB2 amplification 3/8, FGFR2 fusion 1/8 and NTRK1 fusion 1/8.

Clinical response to MET inhibition (n=45 treated; 38 RECIST-evaluable) - Agents: crizotinib 22, an unnamed investigational agent 13, cabozantinib 8, capmatinib 1 and tepotinib 1 (PMID:36044468). - Combinations: In 27 cases the MET inhibitor was combined with a non-MET TKI to address acquired resistance: osimertinib 24, selitrectinib 1, selpercatinib 1 and afatinib 1 (PMID:36044468). - Response by group (PMID:36044468):

Group ORR (95% CI) Responders Median TTD
1 25% (3–71%) 1/4 7.8 months
2 (all MET exon 14; no fusions) 67% (39–86%) 8/12 8.3 months
3 25% (3–71%) 1/4 4.3 months
4 44% (25–66%) 8/18 6.1 months
  • Response by MET copy number: ORR was 29% for <6 copies (n=7), 53% for 6–9 copies (n=15) and 50% for ≥10 copies (n=16). Response was numerically, but not significantly, higher with ≥6 copies (PMID:36044468).
  • Focality in treated patients: All but one treated patient had focal amplification. The one broad amplification also carried a MET exon 14 alteration and had a partial response (PMID:36044468).
  • Primary progression: 5% across all MET-amplified cancers, regardless of amplification level (PMID:36044468).

Genes & alterations

  • MET: copy number gain (FACETS copy number 3–5) versus amplification (copy number ≥6), with amplification further described by focality (segment <25 Mb vs arm/chromosome-level). Amplification was found in about 0.7% of 50,748 patients (PMID:36044468).
    • Frequency by type: most frequent in PRCC (8.4%), high-grade glioma (5.3%), GEJ (5.0%) and pleomorphic/sarcomatoid NSCLC (8.5%); NSCLC overall 1.9%.
    • Focality and drivers: copy number was inversely related to amplicon size. Amplification was highest and most focal when de novo isolated or acquired after TKI therapy.
    • Exon 14 overlap: MET exon 14 alterations co-occurred in 15/84 de novo NSCLC amplifications, and this group had the best ORR (67%). MET fusions co-occurred in 1 NSCLC and 2 non-lung cases.
    • Background (Introduction): MET exon 14 skipping occurs in 3–4% of NSCLC, and about 20% of such tumors also carry MET CNGs.
  • EGFR: the dominant driver behind acquired MET amplification in NSCLC (50/56, 89% of group 4). EGFR was also a de novo co-driver in 7 NSCLC and 1 non-lung case. Most combination-treated patients received osimertinib plus a MET inhibitor (n=24) (PMID:36044468).
  • KRAS: de novo co-driver in 6 NSCLC and 9 non-lung MET-amplified tumors. One NSCLC acquired MET amplification on therapy for KRAS G12C (PMID:36044468).
  • ERBB2: co-driver in 2 NSCLC. ERBB2 amplification co-occurred with MET amplification in 10 de novo and 3 acquired-resistance non-lung tumors. The MET NGS validation approach was modeled on the group’s earlier ERBB2 (HER2) NGS amplification validation (PMID:36044468).
  • ALK and RET: in NSCLC, co-drivers in de novo MET amplification (1 each) and in acquired MET amplification (ALK 3/56, RET 2/56). A RET fusion was also a de novo co-driver in 1 non-lung tumor (PMID:36044468).
  • BRAF: in non-lung de novo MET-amplified tumors, 7 BRAF mutations and 2 BRAF fusions; BRAF mutations were also found in 3/8 acquired-resistance non-lung cases (PMID:36044468).
  • FGFR2, FGFR1, FGFR3, NRAS and NTRK1: less frequent co-drivers in non-lung MET-amplified tumors.
    • FGFR2 fusions: 2 de novo and 1 acquired.
    • FGFR1 fusion: 1.
    • FGFR3 mutation: 1.
    • NRAS mutations: 2.
    • NTRK1 fusion: 1 acquired.
    Source: PMID:36044468.

Clinical implications

  • NGS as primary assay: With proper validation, NGS-based MET assessment is suitable and “potentially more informative” than FISH in most solid tumors. NGS averages over far more cells (250 ng of DNA is about 38,000 diploid nuclei), handles heterogeneous or subclonal resistance-acquired amplification better, and can resolve focality, which FISH cannot (PMID:36044468).
  • Recommended pipeline: Supplement read-depth calls with a purity/ploidy-adjusted integer copy number caller (FACETS or an analogous segmental method) and a focality assessment. Require at least 20% tumor content, and reflex samples below 20% with negative or borderline results to FISH (PMID:36044468).
  • Trial eligibility: Durable responses occurred at lower NGS copy numbers, including tumors with FISH MET:CEP7 <5, and primary progression was rare (5%). The authors therefore suggest that “any degree of true MET amplification” is reasonable to explore in MET-inhibitor trials when called by an optimized NGS algorithm. They warn that a high-level-only cutoff “may disqualify patients who could benefit”, while still recommending prospective stratification by amplification level (PMID:36044468).
  • Benefit by context: Benefit was most enriched with concurrent MET amplification and MET exon 14 alteration (ORR 67%). Responses were more attenuated in de novo isolated amplification and when a non-MET driver was present, whether de novo or acquired after EGFR-TKI therapy (PMID:36044468).
  • Regulatory context (Introduction): capmatinib and tepotinib have FDA accelerated approval for MET exon 14 skipping NSCLC. Crizotinib and capmatinib are active in high-level MET amplification but not approved for that indication. NCCN lists MET amplification as an emerging biomarker (PMID:36044468).

Limitations & open questions

  • Single site and single assay: Retrospective, single-institution study using one large hybrid-capture panel with dense SNP tiling and matched normals. The authors call for evaluating MET copy number calling on other NGS assays, especially amplicon-based panels that need less input (PMID:36044468).
  • No standards: There are no established clinical NGS cutoffs, reporting guidelines for somatic copy number alterations, or FFPE reference standards for specific MET copy levels (PMID:36044468).
  • Arbitrary focality cutoff: The 25 Mb threshold (a quarter of 7q) is not a validated clinical definition. The authors say more rigorous studies are needed to define focal amplification for MET and other genes (PMID:36044468).
  • Limit-of-detection design: The limit-of-detection study used one sample with high baseline amplification, which may overestimate sensitivity at low purity. The 20% cutoff is supported by the group’s experience with HER2 (ERBB2), MYC and EGFR amplification assays (PMID:36044468).
  • Small, heterogeneous treatment cohort: The treated cohort was small (38 evaluable; n=4 in groups 1 and 3) and mixed. Treatments included an unnamed investigational agent in 13 patients and combination TKIs in 27. The trend toward higher ORR at higher copy number was not statistically significant (PMID:36044468).
  • Reporting inconsistencies:
    • For the <6-copy stratum, the text gives a 95% CI of “37–71%”, which does not contain the 29% point estimate. This is likely a typographical error in the source (PMID:36044468).
    • The extracted text omits the confidence interval for case P-0000772-T01-IM3 (copy number 20).
  • Access to underlying data: Only the FISH validation subset is deposited in cBioPortal (mixed_impact_subset_2022); the 50,748-patient landscape data are available only on request. Pan-cancer MET frequencies here therefore cannot be re-derived directly from the public study.
  • Unmapped category: “High-grade glioma” (5.3%) is reported as a grouped category (the figure also lists anaplastic astrocytoma and glioblastoma) and is not mapped to a single OncoTree code here.
  • Related corpus paper: The reference list includes the MSK prospective LUAD characterization study (PMID:28336552), which reported matched-therapy rates for MET exon 14 and MET-amplified lung adenocarcinoma in an earlier MSK-IMPACT cohort.
