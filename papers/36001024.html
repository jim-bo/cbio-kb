TL;DR

The authors applied microscaled proteogenomics (germline-matched whole-exome sequencing, RNA-seq, and TMT-based proteomics and phosphoproteomics) to pretreatment core-needle biopsies from 59 patients with stage II/III triple-negative breast cancer (BRCA, TNBC) treated with six cycles of neoadjuvant carboplatin + docetaxel in two single-arm trials (NCT02547987, NCT02124902; cBioPortal brca_dldccc_2022). Tumors without pathologic complete response (pCR) had higher protein-level oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabolism and adipogenesis pathways. Tumors with pCR had higher DNA repair, E2F target, G2–M checkpoint, interferon and immune-checkpoint (CD274/PD-L1) signals at both the RNA and protein level. Cytoband-level proteogenomic analysis identified a resistance-associated 19q13.31–33 deletion that contains the lagging-strand DNA synthesis genes LIG1, POLD1 and XRCC1. The authors checked LIG1 loss or low expression in other trial cohorts, PDX models and TCGA. It was associated with non-pCR, higher chromosomal instability (CIN), carboplatin-selective (not docetaxel) resistance in preclinical models, and poor progression-free survival across several cancer types PMID:36001024.

Cohort & data

  • Discovery cohort: women with clinical stage II/III ER-negative, HER2-negative (IHC 0/1+ or FISH-negative) invasive breast cancer (BRCA, TNBC) enrolled at Baylor College of Medicine and Washington University. Trials were NCT02547987 and NCT02124902. All were treated without randomization with IV docetaxel 75 mg/m² + carboplatin every 21 days for 6 cycles with G-CSF support PMID:36001024.
  • Self-reported race was 70% Caucasian, 27% African American and 3% other PMID:36001024.
  • Samples: pretreatment OCT-embedded, snap-frozen core biopsies from 59 patients had >25% tumor content (TC) and were analyzed (22 pCR / 37 non-pCR per Fig. 1B). 16 patients also gave an on-treatment biopsy 48–72 h after starting chemotherapy (cycle 1, day 3). 14 of these pairs had high TC (13 with RNA) and were used for paired analysis. Samples with average TC <45% lost mRNA–protein correlation, so 9 samples with proteomics data were excluded PMID:36001024.
  • Endpoints: pCR and residual cancer burden (RCB 0 = pCR; RCB I–III = increasing residual disease) PMID:36001024.
  • Assays (microscaled proteogenomics / BioTEXT processing of alternating 50-μm cryosections):
    • Paired tumor/normal whole-exome sequencing at 100× for 59 baseline DNA samples.
    • Strand-specific poly-A+ RNA-seq for 60 samples, aligned to GRCh38.d1.vd1 with STAR 2.7.1a and quantified with RSEM 1.3.1.
    • TMT11 proteomics and Fe³⁺-IMAC phosphoproteomics: eight 11-plexes of nine biopsies each, with a pooled common reference PMID:36001024.
  • Coverage: DNA, RNA and protein data for an average of ~10,500 genes per sample; ~27,000 phosphosites on ~5,000 phosphoproteins. Median per-gene mRNA–protein correlation was 0.37 PMID:36001024.
  • Genomic pipeline:
    • Somatic calls from Strelka2 (Strelka), Mutect2 (MuTect), CARNAC and Pindel.
    • Copy number from CopywriteR with circular binary segmentation, and gene-level CNA calls from GISTIC2 (±0.3 log2 threshold). Copy number was mapped to genes with an hg19 refGene annotation.
    • COSMIC signatures from deconstructSigs and MSI from MSIsensor.
    • A genome-wide CIN index: length-weighted sum of absolute segment log2 ratios over the 22 autosomes PMID:36001024.
  • Pathway and immune scoring:
    • GSEA (WebGestaltR), ssGSEA and PTM-SEA.
    • RNA- and protein-based multigene proliferation scores (MGPS) and protein-based immune modulator scores.
    • ESTIMATE, CIBERSORT (absolute mode) and xCell.
    • PAM50 subtype, and CD3 and PD-L1 IHC PMID:36001024.
  • Validation resources (none of them cBioPortal study IDs in the text):
    • BrighTNess trial RNA-seq: arms A+B, paclitaxel/carboplatin ± veliparib followed by doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide, N = 359; arm C, paclitaxel alone.
    • The TNBC subset of the Hatzis neoadjuvant cohort (metastasis-free survival).
    • A single-agent neoadjuvant cisplatin trial.
    • Three longitudinal orthotopic PDX models (WHIM68/74/75), the NCI PDXNet TNBC PDX cohort, and the BCaPE PDX-derived drug-screen database.
    • DepMap TNBC cell lines.
    • TCGA pan-cancer copy-number/PFS data and CPTAC cohorts for CIN PMID:36001024.
  • Data deposition: dbGaP phs002505.v1 (genomics/transcriptomics); Proteomics Data Commons PDC000408 (biopsy proteome), PDC000409 (biopsy phosphoproteome) and PDC000410 (PDX proteome); MassIVE MSV000089758 PMID:36001024.

Key findings

Chemotherapy-induced changes (14 paired on-treatment biopsies) - Immune-related pathways fell after treatment at both the RNA and protein level. Cell-cycle and metabolic pathways (except glycolysis) rose significantly only at the protein level. DNA replication/repair pathways were induced, consistent with genotoxic stress PMID:36001024. - Treatment-induced phosphosites overlapped signatures of nocodazole and ionizing radiation. Phosphorylation of CDK1, CDK2 and ATM kinase targets increased PMID:36001024.

Clinical and genomic features vs. pCR - PAM50 intrinsic subtype, TNBC type, race and other clinical metadata were not associated with pCR. Germline BRCA1/BRCA2/PALB2 mutations and HRD-associated COSMIC signature 3 were also not associated with pCR; the authors attribute these negative results to limited sample size PMID:36001024. - A higher COSMIC signature 6 (mismatch-repair defect) score was associated with high RCB (II/III; P = 0.03) PMID:36001024. - Nonsynonymous mutation load did not differ by pCR (Wilcoxon P = 0.57; median 77 in pCR vs 78 in non-pCR). It did not correlate with immune scores either (Spearman rho = −0.17, P = 0.25) PMID:36001024.

Pathway signatures - Non-pCR tumors had upregulated MSigDB Hallmark oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabolism and adipogenesis in the proteome but not the transcriptome. Glycolysis was also higher by ssGSEA PMID:36001024. - pCR tumors had higher interferon-α/γ response, G2–M checkpoint, E2F-target and MYC-target pathways in both the proteome and transcriptome PMID:36001024. - PTM-SEA: - pCR tumors were enriched for phosphosignatures of DNA-damaging agents (etoposide, hydroxyurea, ionizing radiation) and for CDK1, CDK2, CDK7 and CDC7 target sites. - Non-pCR tumors were enriched for MARK2 target sites PMID:36001024. - A subset of non-pCR tumors had elevated CDK4 activity and Rb phosphorylation. In DepMap TNBC cell lines, higher Rb (RB1) protein was associated with weaker carboplatin response but stronger CDK4/6-inhibitor response PMID:36001024.

Immune microenvironment - The protein-based immune stimulatory score was higher in pCR tumors (Wilcoxon P = 0.01). PD-L1 (CD274) RNA, protein and phosphoprotein were also significantly higher in pCR PMID:36001024. - The immune stimulatory score was anticorrelated with CIN (Spearman rho = −0.61, P = 6.2e−6). PD-L1 protein and phosphoprotein correlated with PD-L1 IHC PMID:36001024.

Metabolic resistance signature - Resistance-associated metabolic proteins in non-pCR tumors: - TCA cycle: ACO2, FH, MDH2, SUCLG1, SUCLG2, PDP1, DLAT. - Electron transport chain: SDHC, UQCR10. - Fatty acid metabolism: CRAT, ACADS, ACAT1, DECR1, ECHS1, HADHB. - Amino-acid catabolism: ALDH6A1, HMGCL, DBT, BCKDHB PMID:36001024. - 29 of 43 Hallmark metabolic genes had enough mRNA–protein correlation to test in BrighTNess (arms A+B, N = 359). There, the geometric-mean metabolic score was higher in non-pCR than pCR (Wilcoxon P = 0.003) and rose with RCB class (Kruskal–Wallis P = 0.0024) PMID:36001024.

Copy-number-driven biomarkers: the 19q13.31–33 deletion - Cytoband GSEA of non-pCR vs pCR expression found elevated expression from 8q21.3 (amplified) and suppressed expression from 19q13.31–33 (deleted) in non-pCR tumors PMID:36001024. - 8q21.3: RMDN1, CPNE3, DECR1 and OTUD6B had higher mRNA and protein in non-pCR. RIPK2 was higher only at the protein level PMID:36001024. - 19q13.31–33: LIG1, PPP5C, BCL3 and NOSIP had lower mRNA and protein in non-pCR. Their expression was suppressed with single-copy LIG1 loss (GISTIC = −1) PMID:36001024. - Hallmark DNA-repair genes were enriched among 19q13.31–33 genes, with leading edge LIG1, XRCC1, POLD1 and ERCC2. LIG1 had the strongest protein-level association with response, followed by POLD1 PMID:36001024. - Orthogonal clinical validation: - In BrighTNess carboplatin/paclitaxel arms (A+B), LIG1 and POLD1 mRNA were significantly lower in tumors with residual disease. No such difference was seen in the paclitaxel-only arm C (P > 0.05; smaller n). - Low LIG1 and XRCC1 mRNA were associated with poor metastasis-free survival in the Hatzis TNBC subset. - In the neoadjuvant cisplatin trial, LIG1 mRNA was lower in SD+PD than CR+PR. - LIG1 was the most consistent of the four DNA-repair genes across datasets PMID:36001024.

Features of LIG1-loss TNBC (discovery set) - LIG1 loss (GISTIC = −1) occurred in 8 of 31 (~26%) non-pCR tumors. LIG1 copy-number log ratio correlated with LIG1 mRNA (Pearson R = 0.67, P = 2.8e−06) and protein (R = 0.55, P = 8.2e−05) PMID:36001024. - Compared with LIG1-intact (WT/gain) tumors, LIG1-loss tumors had: - lower COSMIC HRD signature 3 (t test P = 0.01); - higher CIN (t test P = 0.0003); - higher protein-based proliferation score (p-MGPS; Wilcoxon P = 0.004); - higher CDK1/2 kinase activity by PTM-SEA; - downregulation of the IL33 phosphosignature PMID:36001024. - LIG1-loss tumors had lower immune stimulatory scores than pCR tumors (P = 0.01), but not than all other tumors. Their EGFR (gefitinib) and PI3K (wortmannin) perturbation phosphosignatures were negatively enriched PMID:36001024.

Preclinical models - Three orthotopic PDXs came from one discovery-trial patient: WHIM68 from the pretreatment primary, WHIM74 from surgery after 5 months of neoadjuvant carboplatin/docetaxel, and WHIM75 from a liver metastasis 1 year after treatment began. LIG1 was lost progressively at the copy-number, mRNA and protein levels. Western blot confirmed the protein loss and also showed reduced POLD1 and XRCC1 PMID:36001024. - WHIM68, which had the highest LIG1, was carboplatin-sensitive; WHIM74 and WHIM75 were progressively and markedly less sensitive. The relationship was much weaker for docetaxel PMID:36001024. - A somatic BRCA2 loss-of-function (frameshift) mutation in WHIM68 could not be detected in WHIM74/75. The authors read this as treatment-induced clonal selection: the BRCA2-mutant clone regressed while a LIG1-deleted clone expanded PMID:36001024. - In the NCI PDXNet TNBC PDX cohort, LIG1 mRNA was significantly lower in PDXs that did not reach complete response to carboplatin. The association was not significant for docetaxel PMID:36001024. - In the BCaPE PDX-derived explant screen, LIG1 copy-number loss was uniquely correlated with carboplatin resistance among more than 100 drugs tested PMID:36001024.

Pan-cancer (TCGA) - LIG1 heterozygous loss (GISTIC ≤ −1) was associated with: - poor progression-free survival (log-rank P < 0.0001); - higher fraction genome altered; - lower COSMIC signature 3, suggesting proficient homologous recombination PMID:36001024. - Cancer types driving the PFS association PMID:36001024:

Cancer type Hazard ratio P
Endometrial carcinoma (UCEC) 2.23 0.02
Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC) 1.46 0.03
Prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) 2.07 0.02
Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) 1.75 0.03
Kidney renal papillary carcinoma (TCGA KIRP; OncoTree PRCC) 4 0.0001
  • These five cancers also had higher fraction genome altered with LIG1 loss. Higher CIN with LIG1 loss was also seen in CPTAC cohorts PMID:36001024.
  • Testicular germ cell tumors (TGCT) had only a marginal PFS association. The carboplatin-sensitive seminoma subtype (SEM) had no cases of LIG1 loss PMID:36001024.

Genes & alterations

  • LIG1: single-copy (hemizygous) deletion at 19q13.31–33 and/or low mRNA or protein.
    • Associated with non-pCR, higher CIN, lower HRD signature 3, higher proliferation, a less active immune microenvironment, and carboplatin-selective resistance in PDX models.
    • Pan-cancer, associated with poor PFS.
    • LIG1 is the main DNA ligase that seals Okazaki fragments during lagging-strand synthesis PMID:36001024.
  • POLD1: co-located on 19q13.31–33. Lower protein in non-pCR (second to LIG1), lower mRNA in BrighTNess residual disease, and reduced protein in the carboplatin-resistant PDXs PMID:36001024.
  • XRCC1: co-located on 19q13.31–33. Low mRNA was associated with poor metastasis-free survival in the Hatzis TNBC subset, and protein was reduced in the resistant PDXs. With LIG3, XRCC1 provides the backup pathway for LIG1-mediated ligation. The authors propose that LIG1/POLD1/XRCC1 codeletion creates a hemizygous compound deficiency in lagging-strand synthesis PMID:36001024.
  • ERCC2: 19q13.31–33 DNA-repair leading-edge gene lower in non-pCR. It was less consistent than LIG1 in validation PMID:36001024.
  • PPP5C, BCL3, NOSIP: 19q13.31–33 genes with lower mRNA and protein in non-pCR PMID:36001024.
  • RMDN1, CPNE3, DECR1, OTUD6B: 8q21.3 (amplified) genes with higher mRNA and protein in non-pCR. RIPK2 (8q21.3) was higher in non-pCR at the protein level only PMID:36001024.
  • BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2: pathogenic germline mutations were not associated with pCR in this cohort. A somatic BRCA2 frameshift in the pretreatment PDX (WHIM68) was lost in the post-treatment PDXs PMID:36001024.
  • CD274 (PD-L1): RNA, protein and phosphoprotein higher in pCR tumors. Protein and phosphoprotein correlated with PD-L1 IHC PMID:36001024.
  • RB1 / CDK4: some non-pCR tumors had high CDK4 activity and Rb phosphorylation. In DepMap TNBC lines, high Rb protein went with lower carboplatin response and higher CDK4/6-inhibitor response PMID:36001024.
  • CDK1, CDK2, CDK7, CDC7: target phosphosites higher in pCR tumors. CDK1/2 activity was also higher in LIG1-loss tumors and after treatment PMID:36001024.
  • MARK2: target phosphosites enriched in non-pCR tumors PMID:36001024.
  • ATM: target phosphorylation increased on treatment PMID:36001024.
  • EGFR: EGFR-inhibitor (gefitinib) perturbation phosphosignature negatively enriched in LIG1-loss tumors PMID:36001024.

Clinical implications

  • The authors propose LIG1 loss as a carboplatin-selective predictive biomarker. Because carboplatin adds toxicity to an already toxic anthracycline-based regimen, it “could potentially be avoided in LIG1-depleted tumors” PMID:36001024.
  • Proteogenomic immune profiling (immune stimulatory scores, PD-L1 protein/phosphoprotein) gives richer information on the immune microenvironment than PD-L1 IHC alone. The authors suggest multiplexed targeted proteomic assays with heavy isotope–labeled peptides to complement IHC in pembrolizumab-era neoadjuvant TNBC PMID:36001024.
  • Protein-level measurement of oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid metabolism pathways could strengthen pCR prediction models. The authors cite an ongoing phase II trial of omeprazole (fatty acid synthase inhibition) with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NCT02595372) PMID:36001024.
  • CDK4/6 or CDK2 inhibitors are suggested as alternatives for RB-intact TNBC PMID:36001024.
  • Given suppressed EGFR and PI3K phosphosignatures, LIG1-loss tumors may respond less to EGFR, PI3K or AKT inhibition. This is a hypothesis, not tested PMID:36001024.

Limitations & open questions

  • Small discovery cohort. The expected associations of pCR with germline BRCA1/2/PALB2 and signature 3 were not observed, which the authors attribute to limited sample size PMID:36001024.
  • The validation cohorts used different regimens: paclitaxel/carboplatin ± veliparib then AC in BrighTNess, single-agent cisplatin, and a taxane–anthracycline regimen (Hatzis). Only the preclinical models separate carboplatin from docetaxel effects PMID:36001024.
  • The longitudinal PDX evidence for LIG1 loss during progression comes from a single patient. The observation of higher LIG1 loss frequency in metastatic disease is in Supplementary Fig. S6A, and its source cohort is not named in the main text PMID:36001024.
  • Single-copy LIG1 loss may not cause enough functional deficiency to produce a phenotype. The compound-hemizygous LIG1/POLD1/XRCC1 hypothesis still needs mechanistic testing PMID:36001024.
  • It is unclear why LIG1 loss tracks so strongly with CIN across cancer types. The authors hypothesize that mitosis with unrepaired lagging strands drives chromosomal breakage, fusion and aneuploidy PMID:36001024.
  • LIG1 loss appears orthogonal to HRD (signature 3), which suggests HRD and LIG1 loss are alternative routes to TNBC pathogenesis. This could explain carboplatin insensitivity but has not been proven PMID:36001024.
  • The cellular origin of the metabolic resistance signal is unresolved. One possibility the authors raise is lipid-laden, immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages PMID:36001024.
  • For cross-paper synthesis:
    • The TCGA pan-cancer analysis does not name specific cBioPortal study IDs, so no TCGA study pages are linked here.
    • Trial identifiers differ within the text: NCT02547987/NCT02124902 in Results/Methods vs “NCT02544987 (BCM) and NCT201404107 (WashU)” in the Fig. 1 legend.
    • RNA-seq was aligned to GRCh38, while copy number was mapped to genes with an hg19 refGene annotation PMID:36001024.
  • Related corpus work: the paper’s reference list includes the CPTAC breast (PMID:33212010; brca_cptac_2020) and colon (PMID:31031003) proteogenomic studies. Its median mRNA–protein correlation (0.37) is described as “comparable with previous CPTAC proteogenomic analyses” PMID:36001024.
